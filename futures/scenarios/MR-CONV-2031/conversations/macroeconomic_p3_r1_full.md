# Macroeconomic, phase 3, round 1

## Analysis

Eight things on this lens can go two or more ways by 2031 and would change whether the magnetic resonance (MR) conventional magnet line delivers inside the 30-day tolerance line. The two that move the most of the eight screened risks at once are the demand cycle at the hospital and the path of the dollar and the policy rate, because each changes the volume and the cost that every single-source supplier and both magnet plants must carry. Helium price and a possible United States allocation rule on helium come next, each moving three risks. The read date is 11 October 2026 and the shared search budget is spent, so the figures below come from the phase 2 transcripts and two direct fetches of Federal Reserve pages. No new primary source was found for hospital capital plans, supplier balance sheets or the Qatari helium units; those stay at low confidence.

**U-M1. Do United States hospital margins cut 2027 MR orders, delay them, or arrive together with Europe's replacement wave?** Moves R3 (N18 hub throughput), R4 (N03 wire volume), R5 (N05 cold-head volume) and R7 (N20 hiring against a volume plan). Kind: causation, with a value element in whether delivery reliability is scored at tender. Predetermined parts: a policy rate projected at 4.1 percent through 2027, hospital operating margin of 2.5 percent for the first half of 2026, Medicaid phasing down from fiscal 2028, and more than a quarter of Europe's MR installed base already past ten years old (Federal Reserve, 16 Sep 2026; Kaufman Hall via Advisory Board, 27 Aug 2026; COCIR, 2023). Uncertain part: what a hospital does with a squeezed margin. Three directions. Orders are cut and the single sources at N03 and N05 lose the volume that keeps MR a priority for their parents. Orders are deferred into 2028 and 2029 and land on top of the European replacement wave, so the 2029 to 2031 build rate is above plan and the single sources are the bottleneck. Orders hold flat on replacement need alone. Why uncertain: no 2026 hospital capital survey was retrieved, and the phase 2 synthesis records that replacement need is fixed while orders are not (P17). Indicator: Kaufman Hall's monthly National Hospital Flash Report (kaufmanhall.com), and the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) projections each quarter (federalreserve.gov). Panel judgement, moderate confidence on the United States path to 2027, low on orders.

**U-M2. Where do the dollar and the cost of money go, and does that move where it is cheaper to build and to hold stock?** Moves R2 (N17 loading between the China and United States plants), R4 (N03 wire priced in euros, pounds and yen), R5 (N05 cold heads priced in yen) and R8 (N19 the carrying cost of helium cover). Kind: outcomes. At 2 October 2026 the yen was 157.81 per dollar, the euro 1.1259 dollars, the yuan 6.7038 per dollar, the Canadian dollar 1.4253 per dollar and the broad dollar index 121.38 (Federal Reserve H.10, 5 Oct 2026). The FOMC median rate path is 4.1 percent in 2026 and 2027, 3.9 in 2028, 3.6 in 2029 and 3.2 in the longer run (Federal Reserve projections, 16 Sep 2026). Three directions. The rate falls on that path and the dollar weakens, so imported wire and cold heads cost more in dollars while the carrying cost of cover and safety stock falls. The rate stays near 4 percent because producer prices keep rising at 5.4 percent a year (BLS, 10 Sep 2026), so stock stays expensive to hold and the strong dollar keeps Japanese and European inputs cheap. The yuan is revalued and the China plant's cost advantage for Asia narrows. Why uncertain: the FOMC's own path has moved by more than a point within a year, and currency is not forecast by any index. Indicator: the H.10 weekly release and the quarterly projections at federalreserve.gov. Panel judgement, moderate confidence: two primary sources, one read date.

**U-M3. What does a helium fill cost in 2028, and who gets helium when there is not enough?** Moves R1 (N16 liquefaction and ramp), R2 (N17, which sits in the market that lost Qatari and Russian-to-West supply) and R8 (N19 cover sized in days at a price). Kind: outcomes. This carries the phase 2 open item U-H1 into price. Predetermined: Qatar's 63 of 190 million cubic metres is impaired with a stated three-to-five-year repair, Russian helium is barred from the United States and European Union, and the United States federal reserve was sold in 2024 (USGS, Jan 2026; P3). Uncertain: a satellite analysis of 6 March 2026 saw main equipment intact and no producer disclosure confirms the helium units were hit. Three directions. Qatari units restart in 2027 or 2028 and the spot premium of 40 to 100 percent seen in 2026 (WestAir, 21 May 2026, secondary) unwinds. Impairment runs to 2030 and suppliers keep allocating by customer class, with healthcare first, so the United States plant pays more but is filled while the China plant is rationed. Impairment runs to 2030 and a United States producer outage coincides, so allocation becomes physical and price does not clear it. Why uncertain: the repair date rests on one ministerial statement. Indicator: the producer's force majeure notices and the annual USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries (pubs.usgs.gov, each January). Moderate confidence on supply figures, low on repair timing.

**U-M4. Does the United States government allocate helium to a bigger buyer than hospitals?** Moves R1, R2 and R8 for the same nodes as U-M3, but by a different mechanism: a rule, not a price. Kind: actions by a government, which the OEM experiences as an outcome. The United States is the only net exporting producer (USGS, Jan 2026), semiconductor fabs and space launch are growing helium users, and the Section 232 chip measures of 2026 show the same administration willing to steer inputs to data-centre and defence uses (EY, 15 Jan 2026). Three directions. No rule, and medical buyers keep contract priority by supplier choice. An export licence or allocation order that keeps United States helium at home, which helps N16 and starves N17. A defence or semiconductor priority rating that puts hospitals and magnet plants behind fabs. Why uncertain: no such rule was proposed at the read date, and this lens underweights politics in allocation. Indicator: the Federal Register (federalregister.gov) and Bureau of Industry and Security notices. Panel judgement, low confidence: no source shows a proposal.

**U-M5. Does a downturn or a fusion and physics boom push the wire mill or the cold-head maker out of MR before demand falls?** Moves R4 (N03) and R5 (N05). Kind: outcomes with a causation element, namely whether MR is a priority for the parent. Phase 2 found the one United States cryocooler maker bought by a quantum-cryogenics company that does not list MR among its markets, and Western wire mills with order books filling with fusion and physics work (P18). Three directions. Parents reaffirm MR and lead times hold near 18 months. Parents reallocate capacity to higher-margin fusion and quantum work and MR lead times and prices rise without a formal exit. A recession in 2027 or 2028 cuts the parents' funding for expansion while physics demand keeps the queue long, so the OEM's 18-month requalification starts from a position behind other buyers. Why uncertain: no supplier balance sheet or order backlog was retrieved, so this rests on public strategy statements. Indicator: the parents' annual reports and quarterly backlog disclosures (sec.gov filings). AI-suggested on which mill is qualified; Sourcing confirms. Low confidence.

**U-M6. Do the 2027 refined copper duty and a wider chip tariff land, and is magnet wire reclassified into the copper annex?** Moves R4 (N03) and R6 (N10 logic, if the non-data-centre exemption narrows). Kind: actions by a government. The 50 percent duty on worked copper is in force since 1 August 2025 and the 15 percent refined duty for 1 January 2027 was undecided in June 2026 (White House, 30 Jul 2025; ING, Jun 2026). Directions: the refined duty lands and rises to 30 percent in 2028; it is deferred; the wire's declared code is moved under the copper annex and the price of every magnet rises by the copper content. Why uncertain: a proclamation not yet signed. Indicator: federalregister.gov. The wire classification is AI-suggested; Sourcing confirms the declared code.

**U-M7. Does regional construction demand price the magnet plant's trades out of its labour market?** Moves R1 (N16) and R7 (N20). Kind: outcomes. The Carolinas grid carries contracted data-centre load from 2027 (NERC, 29 Jan 2026) and producer prices run at 5.4 percent a year. Directions: data-centre and energy construction bids up welders and cryogenic technicians in the plant's region and attrition rises without a strike; the boom cools with a downturn and the roster stabilises; wage inflation is absorbed in the plant's cost without a loss of output. Why uncertain: wage data for the plant's county and trade were not retrieved. Indicator: Bureau of Labor Statistics Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages for South Carolina (bls.gov/cew). Panel judgement, low confidence.

**U-M8. Does memory and logic allocation to AI buyers reach lead time, not only price, for the control electronics?** Moves R6 (N10) and the watch items N11 and N12. Kind: causation. DRAM contract prices roughly tripled across 2026 under allocation reported to run into 2028, and mature-node capacity moves partly out of Taiwan by 2028 (P16). Directions: allocation ends with an AI capital spending break in 2027 and prices fall; allocation holds and field-programmable gate array (FPGA) lead times pass the 18-month requalification time; allocation holds for memory only and logic is unaffected. Indicator: TrendForce quarterly bulletins and foundry quarterly reports. AI-suggested on whether the OEM buys on allocation; Sourcing confirms.

## Critical factors

The two factors that set the rest are the 2027 hospital capital plan in the United States (U-M1) and the FOMC path with its effect on the dollar (U-M2). Helium (U-M3 and U-M4) is the factor that reaches a line stop fastest through N19.

## Predetermined versus uncertain

Predetermined: the rate and reimbursement path to 2027, the tariffs in force, the Qatari impairment at the read date, the demographic age of the installed base. Uncertain: everything above, with the kind of not-knowing named on each item.

## Contradictions with other lenses

None recorded; this is round 1 of phase 3. The phase 2 contradiction on Qatari repair timing is carried as U-M3.

## Blind spots of this lens

I see aggregates and have not read a single supplier's accounts, so U-M5 is the weakest item. I treat U-M3 as a price question when phase 2 evidence shows allocation by supplier choice. I rank U-M4 low because no proposal exists, which is the political blind spot named in my dossier.
