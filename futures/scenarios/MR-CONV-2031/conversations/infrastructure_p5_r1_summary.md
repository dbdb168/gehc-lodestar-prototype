## Key points

The Hormuz daily transit count is the only infrastructure indicator that is live today and that can move U2 within a quarter; it read four ships on 4 Oct 2026 against a typical 85 and leans toward helium still deciding. N01, N23, N27, N19 (straits.live via IMF PortWatch, 4 Oct 2026).

The Qatari share of United States helium imports, 28 percent for 2021 to 2024, is the slow annual confirmation of the same uncertainty; two annual steps in one direction count, one does not. N01, N16, N19, N34 (USGS, 5 Feb 2026).

Red Sea weekly transits, 278 through Suez in the week of 21 to 27 Sep 2026, show the Gulf lane as a rerouting cost and not a stop, which leans U1 toward end A. N27, N28 (Lloyd's List, 1 Oct 2026).

The export port's advisories list no closure or labour item in 2026, and the longshore contract runs to 30 Sep 2030, so the lane stop that would settle U1 toward end B has no reading yet. N25, N26 (SC Ports advisories read 11 Oct 2026; ILA, 25 Feb 2025).

Three of the four readings need a person each quarter; a Taiwan Strait count for N29 exists in the dataset but has no confirmed address. Panel judgement.
