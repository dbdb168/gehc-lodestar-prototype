# Infrastructure, phase 6a, round 1

## Analysis

Bottom line: the eight risks break in three physical places. Helium cover at the United States magnet plant (N19) breaks when helium still decides, the sole cold-head maker (N05) when it does not, and the China plant (N17) when a state stops a part. All four share the 35-day encryption event (C4); the single export port (N26) delays exports everywhere but never stops the line.

F1 (cost only, helium still decides). R8 helium cover (N19) breaks first in the winter of 2028 to 2029, when a grid curtailment takes the liquefier's power at N16 while the next container is inside its 35 to 48 day hold and a flood shuts the oversize road lane (N24). The stop runs 30 to 45 days and breaks R1 (N16) with it; R2 (N17) breaks on the Asian allocation queue in the first half of 2028, as late installs. Cover at N19 above 60 days, or above the hold plus ten days with backup power, removes the break. Confidence low to moderate: no record yet of an elevated grid rating becoming a multi-week curtailment.

F2 (cost only, helium no longer decides). R5 cold heads (N05) breaks first from 2029, once the sealed share passes half, by an earthquake or outage at the one Japanese plant. This lens adds a second door: a Nankai Trough event closes the maker's ports (N30) for weeks, so even an undamaged plant cannot move parts. The stop runs up to 18 months and breaks R1 and R2. An address outside the Nankai zone and a second source turn the break into a cost. Confidence moderate on the node, low on timing.

F3 (a part stops, helium still decides). R2 (N17) breaks first in the second half of 2027 through logic (N10); R6 breaks with it. The lane to Japan and India (N29) carries nothing, and the United States plant cannot cover Asia because ramp and test capacity (N21) takes 24 months to change. R8 and R1 breach second by early 2028 on allocated helium. The stop lasts the licence queue, two quarters or more. Logic stock longer than the queue removes the break. Confidence moderate: the licence approval rate is unpublished.

F4 (a part stops, helium no longer decides). R5 (N05) breaks by 2029 when the maker's parent allocates capacity away and a dual-use listing closes the licence route. Both plants stop for most of 18 months, breaking R1 and R2. The allocation price is the Placeholder. Confidence moderate; the allocation is unannounced.

Shared by all four: C4 and an intact design record (N22). F1 and F3 share N19 as the margin every grid and road event walks through; F2 and F4 share N05 as the only cold part. One each: F1's winter grid path, F3's idle lane N29.

## Critical factors

The container hold against the N19 cover figure, the grid ratings at N16 and N18, the cold-head maker's ports, and the China plant's logic stock.

## Predetermined versus uncertain

Predetermined: the container fleet and hold, the export port, the N21 replace time, the grid ratings. Uncertain: curtailment from rating (causation), the Gulf repair date (outcomes), the sealed test draw (causation), the licence rate (outcomes), the allocation price (value).

## The shared encryption break

A 35-day loss of systems lands on the gas carriers (N23) and the port terminal (N26) as well as the plants. In F1 and F3 it is worst at the carrier: dispatch stops, N19 runs out inside the 35 days, and the line stops for 50 to 60 days. In F2 and F4, at the cold-head maker, it stops both plants for 35 days plus the shipping backlog; in F4 that is the first break by another name. At the port it is 35 days of late exports; at N16 or N18 it is a 35-day line stop in every future. Confidence moderate: the 35-day figure is public, a carrier's recovery time is not.

## Contradictions with other lenses

Carried from phase 4: the six to five split on which plant breaks first in F1, and the cold-head break-versus-cost split; the N19 cover figure and the test draw settle both.

## Blind spots of this lens

I see assets and miss money and politics: I treat the allocation and licence queues as physical waits and underrate the insurer who stops a magnet before any crane does.
