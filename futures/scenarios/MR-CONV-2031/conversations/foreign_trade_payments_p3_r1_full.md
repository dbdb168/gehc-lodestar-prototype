# Foreign trade and payments, phase 3, round 1

## Analysis

Bottom line: the trade rules that can still go two ways by 2031 mostly move cost and the 2027 sourcing cycle, not shipments. Only two of them can stop the line. The first is a United States and China rupture that cuts the two-way parts flow through the China plant (N17) and strands the Eastern Hemisphere supply. The second is a producer country putting superconducting wire or cold heads on an export-control list, which would turn an 18-month requalification (N03, N05) into a licensing queue with no published clearance rate. Everything else on this lens, tariffs on scanners and parts, the copper duty, the chip tariff, the helium fences and the payment rails, raises prices and lengthens customs time and is absorbed inside a 30-day tolerance line in most futures. That is Panel judgement, and the evidence that would overturn it is a tariff proclamation that covers parts at a rate the OEM's customers will not accept (N35, N36).

Three things changed since phase 2. The Section 232 investigation into medical equipment, which names magnetic resonance imaging (MR) machines in scope, still shows no report or proclamation on the status page read on 11 October 2026 (Sandler, Travis and Rosenberg). The Strait of Hormuz remained closed to commercial traffic on 11 October 2026 with four transits counted on 4 October against a baseline of 85 (straits.live, 11 October 2026). China's helium export ban, Announcement 2026/29 of 10 July 2026, carries no revocation date and no amendment (Global Trade Alert, read 11 October 2026). None of these resolves an uncertainty; each keeps one open.

## Critical factors

Each uncertainty below is written as a question, then the risks it moves (R1 to R8 in the screen's order), the kind of not-knowing, the directions, why it is open, and the indicator with its publication place. Ranked by how many of the eight risks it moves; ties are broken by whether the move is a stop or a cost.

**U-T1. Does the United States put a tariff on imported MR scanners and their parts, and at what rate?** Moves R1, R3, R4 and R5: the magnet plant (N16) and the hub (N18) import wire (N03) and cold heads (N05), so a proclamation that reaches parts raises their landed cost and forces the 2027 sourcing cycle to re-price every foreign component. It does not stop shipments unless the rate is prohibitive. Outcomes. Three directions: no proclamation (the investigation lapses, as the critical minerals one did in January 2026); finished scanners only, which taxes competitors' imports and leaves this line's inputs alone; finished goods and derivative parts, which is the costly case. It is uncertain because the statute lets the President act at any time with no deadline and the status page shows nothing since the October 2025 comment close (Sandler, Travis and Rosenberg, read 11 October 2026; Federal Register, 26 September 2025). Indicator: a proclamation under Section 232 in the Federal Register listing 9018.13 or derivative headings, and the annex of covered codes. Confidence moderate, on the statute's open timing.

**U-T3. Does any producer country put superconducting wire, niobium or cryocooler cold heads on an export-control list?** Moves R1, R2, R4 and R5: a listing by Germany, the United Kingdom or Japan reaches the United States plant's wire (N16, N03); a Chinese listing reaches the China plant's parts in both directions (N17); a Japanese listing of cold heads reaches both plants (N05). This is the one trade uncertainty that can turn into a stop, because a licence queue has no published clearance rate and the requalification clock is 18 months. Outcomes, with a causation element (whether governments see MR wire as dual-use). Directions: no listing (today's state; control entry 1C005 catches niobium-titanium only outside a copper matrix, and China's October 2025 package named no superconductors); a narrow listing with a general licence for medical end-use; a broad listing with case-by-case licensing. It is uncertain because China added rare earths and magnets to its list inside one year and the same instrument could add superconductors (Clark Hill, October 2025), while no government has signalled it. Indicator: a MOFCOM announcement, a Bureau of Industry and Security rule or a European Union dual-use annex update naming 8544.11 wire, niobium or cryocoolers, and the licensing statistics that follow. The wire classification is AI-suggested; Sourcing confirms the declared code.

**U-T6. Do United States and China parts keep flowing both ways through the China plant?** Moves R2, R3, R4 and R6: the truce that suspends China's 24 percent retaliatory tariff runs to 10 November 2026 (Global Times, 5 November 2025); the China plant takes United States-designed logic (N10) and sends parts and finished systems to Asia (N17, N29); the hub takes China-origin power electronics (N08, N18); China is one listed origin of wire (N03). Outcomes. Directions: the truce rolls over annually and flows continue at today's rates; the truce lapses and both sides raise tariffs, which is cost; an entity listing or a control on United States-origin content to the China plant, which is a stop for Asia with no second plant. It is uncertain because the truce has a date and the two governments have reversed course twice since 2025. Indicator: the Federal Register notice on or about 10 November 2026 and the Entity List, and MOFCOM's unreliable entity list. Which parts cross the China border is AI-suggested; the S&OP council confirms.

**U-H2. Do the fences around helium stay, widen or lapse?** Moves R1, R2 and R8: Russia's licensing lapses on 31 December 2027 by its own terms, China's ban has no date, and a United States export control or allocation rule on helium would turn the United States plant's advantage (N16, N19) into the China plant's loss (N17), since Chinese helium now stays at home. Actions by producer governments, with outcomes for the strait. Directions: fences hold and Hormuz stays shut, with world price doing the work; fences lapse as Qatar returns; the United States adds its own fence. It is uncertain because the Russian decree has an expiry, the Chinese ban has none, and the strait has no reopening date (straits.live, 11 October 2026; Global Trade Alert, 10 July 2026). Indicator: Decree 404's renewal or lapse on the Russian legal register, a MOFCOM revocation notice, a Bureau of Industry and Security notice on helium, and IMF PortWatch transits.

**U-P1. Do customs and payment rails for parts between the United States, Europe, Japan and China keep clearing inside days?** Moves R2 and R3 through N31. Outcomes. Directions: no change; secondary sanctions or a bank de-risking that forces payment to the China plant through slower channels, which is weeks; a sanctions designation of a bank in the chain, which is a stop until a new bank is found. It is uncertain because the Treasury's SPFS alert of November 2024 shows the tool exists and is used (US Treasury, 21 November 2024). Indicator: Office of Foreign Assets Control designations and the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network alerts.

**U-T4. Does the 25 percent semiconductor tariff widen to programmable logic and graphics, and does the industrial exemption hold?** Moves R2 and R6 (N10, N12, N17). Outcomes. Directions: exemption holds; the tariff widens to all logic, which is cost; a control on advanced Taiwan-foundry logic to China, which delays the China plant's consoles. Uncertain because the January 2026 proclamation was narrow by design and its exemption's application to reconstruction processors is unconfirmed (EY, 15 January 2026). Indicator: Federal Register amendments to the January 2026 annex. AI-suggested on the exemption; Sourcing confirms.

**U-T2. Does the refined copper duty start on 1 January 2027, and does magnet wire stay outside the copper annex?** Moves R4 only (N03, N04). Outcomes. Directions: the duty starts and wire stays outside; the wire is reclassified into the annex, which adds 50 percent on copper content to every magnet; neither. Uncertain because the Commerce report due 30 June 2026 has not surfaced and the wire rests on a 1991 ruling (Federal Register, 5 August 2025; usebase.io). Indicator: a Customs and Border Protection ruling on 8544.11 in the CROSS database.

Watch items that move none of the eight: a court ruling on the Section 301 duties (N31, cost only), and whether hospitals accept tariff pass-through (N35, N36), which is a value uncertainty the customer lens owns.

## Predetermined versus uncertain

Predetermined, from phase 2: Section 232 and 301 duties with no expiry (P7), the European Union and Chinese procurement walls (P9), Brazil's niobium position (P6), and the four-state helium market (P3). Uncertain: the seven items above. The strongest single change from phase 2 is that the medical equipment tariff (U-T1) is a cost uncertainty, not a stop, unless parts are covered.

## Contradictions with other lenses

No other phase 3 summaries have been read. One tension with the phase 2 synthesis stands: it frames the China plant's exposure as "export controls or a trade rupture" in one line, and this lens splits it, because a tariff lapse is cost and an entity listing is a stop. The Entity List is the evidence that settles which future the OEM is in.

## Blind spots of this lens

I read rules and may miss a producer holding wire or helium back without any rule, which the contrarian and financial lenses watch (P18). I treat the Section 301 rates as stable because they have no expiry. I could not open the copper proclamation at source today and rely on the phase 2 read. The India duty on finished scanners is unverified. Confidence on the helium fences is high (three primary notices); on the export-control listing it is low (no signal, one analogous precedent).
