# Security, phase 4, round 3, summary

## Key points

Votes: F1 helium cover at the US magnet plant from Natural disasters, F2 cold heads from Technology shift, F3 the China plant from Geopolitical, F4 cold heads from Macroeconomic (N19, N05, N17, N05; Panel judgement).

This lens withdraws its round 2 counterfeit break because a bad lot produces faults and a recall, not a late install, so it never crosses the tolerance line as written (N10, N31; Panel judgement).

The four futures differ on both axes and the dates are in order; the one inconsistency is a sealed future that treats service helium as a cylinder trade while the fleet is still mostly conventional to 2029 (N34; Panel judgement).

The thing every page misses is the logistics layer: the 35-day encryption event is tested at the plant, the mill and the cold-head maker, never at the two specialist gas carriers or the port terminal system, which is where the sector's incident record sits (N23, N26, N19; Panel judgement, Logistics confirms manual dispatch).

## In one hundred words

The panel's votes from this lens are N19 in F1, N05 in F2, N17 in F3 and N05 in F4. The pages hold together across both axes, and the stop in F3 lands before the cost in F1 turns into a stop, as it should. What none of them tests is an encrypted carrier. Every shared break is placed at the OEM's plant, the wire mill or the cold-head maker, with the design record assumed intact, yet the incident record in this sector sits with forwarders, terminals and gas carriers. A five-week outage at the helium carriers walks through cover at the US plant with no weather or rule.
