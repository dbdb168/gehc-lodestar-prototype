# Geopolitical, phase 4, round 3, summary

## Key points

In F1 this lens votes for the China plant, because the plant with no domestic helium producer sits at the back of a queue another state controls, while the N19 break still needs a hazard and a Placeholder (N17, N01, N19; Panel judgement).

In F2 and F4 it votes for the cold head, through a parent reallocating capacity rather than a listing, because an owner needs no notice and a destination-addressed control cannot strand both plants (N05; Panel judgement).

In F3 it keeps the China plant through programmable logic within a quarter of an entity listing, the instrument already on the record for this sector (N17, N10; MOFCOM Announcement No. 1, 6 January 2026).

Two breaks sit in the wrong future: Customer and market's parent exit belongs in F4 by the phase 3 fold, and the storm is a shared break in every helium future, not an F1 signature (N05, N16, N19; Panel judgement).

The thing missing on every page is that export controls name a destination, so a cryocooler or wire control aimed at China strands N17 and leaves N16 supplied; the first notice's destination clause settles it (N17, N16, N05, N03; AI-suggested, Quality and regulatory confirms).

## In one hundred words

Votes: F1 China plant (N17) on allocation seniority; F2 cold head (N05); F3 China plant (N17) through logic (N10); F4 cold head (N05) through the owner. The four pages differ on both axes and the dates run in order, with F3 earliest in 2027. Two breaks are misfiled: the parent exit belongs in F4, and the storm is shared. The missing point is geopolitical: controls are addressed by destination, so a listing aimed at China strands one plant, not both. Only an owner exit or a ban on all buyers stops both plants at once. Confidence moderate, on the instruments' dated record.
