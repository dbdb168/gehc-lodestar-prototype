# Geopolitical, phase 3, round 1

## Analysis

The bottom line is that one uncertainty moves four of the eight risks at once, and it is not helium. It is whether the United States and China keep their trade truce through the 2027 sourcing cycle or break into export controls, counter-lists and settlement blocks. Everything else this lens can see moves two risks at most; helium reaches only the China plant and the US plant's cover, because the US plant sits inside a net-exporting producer (P4). A mark in the table at the end means the uncertainty changes whether that risk breaches the 30-day line, not only its cost. Every fact is carried from the phase 2 transcripts with its source.

**U1. Does the United States and China trade truce hold through the 2027 sourcing cycle, or does it break into a rupture that strands the China plant? (N17, N03, N05, N10, N29, N31)** Kind: outcomes. The truce was reported extended to 10 Jan 2027 (New Straits Times, Sep 2026, https://www.nst.com.my/amp/business/economy/2026/09/1543289/china-us-agree-tariff-cuts-us60bil-goods-including-agriculture), four months before the 2027 sourcing decisions. Three directions. Renewed: nothing on the map changes. Hollow: tariffs hold while entity listings and chip controls grow, so every US-origin input into the plant needs a licence. Rupture: US licensing of US-designed logic to China stops N10 reaching Tianjin, a Chinese counter-list reaches Western wire and Japanese cold heads bound for China, and dollar settlement stops while yuan settlement on CIPS continues (FXC Intelligence, May 2026, https://www.fxcintel.com/research/analysis/cips-volumes-may-2026). It moves R2 directly and R4, R5 and R6 through the plant's inputs; if the OEM's one qualified wire mill is Chinese, R4 moves for the US plant too (AI-suggested; Sourcing confirms). Why uncertain: both states have escalated and de-escalated within a quarter, and 10 Jan 2027 is a decision date with no known decision. Observable: Federal Register notices from the Bureau of Industry and Security (entity list) and the United States Trade Representative (truce extension), and announcements by China's Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM).

**U2. Does grey-zone pressure around Taiwan become interdiction of the strait, which carries both the foundry's output and the China plant's sea lanes? (N10, N12, N29, N17)** Kind: outcomes. Coast guard incursions hit a 2026 high in June while air sorties fell 58 percent year on year (Taiwan Security Monitor, Jul 2026, https://tsm.schar.gmu.edu/?p=1629). Three directions. Grey zone continues: nothing stops. Quarantine or inspection regime: ships move with delays of days to weeks, which eats most of the 30-day line on N29 and lengthens logic lead times. Blockade or conflict: N29 closes and the foundry's output stops for every system. It moves R2 and R6. Why uncertain: the capability is growing, the intention is not observable, and threatened closures often do not happen. Observable: Taiwan's Ministry of National Defense daily activity releases, and the Lloyd's Joint War Committee listed areas, where a Taiwan Strait listing would precede any physical act.

**U3. Who gets the helium that is left, and does any state ration it? (N01, N19, N23, N27, N34, N17)** Kind: outcomes, with a causation element on whether Russian licensing steers volume to China. Qatar's units are reported out for up to five years with a 14 percent export cut (IOM3, 23 Mar 2026, https://www.iom3.org/resource/middle-eastern-war-disrupting-helium-supply.html), Hormuz carried 117 non-Iranian transits in the week of 14 to 20 Sep 2026 against a pre-war baseline near 85 a day (Lloyd's List Intelligence, 30 Sep 2026, https://www.lloydslistintelligence.com/resources/blog/strait-of-hormuz-brief-30-september-2026), and Russian exports need prime-ministerial approval to 31 Dec 2027 (Global Trade Alert, https://globaltradealert.org/intervention/154600). Three directions. Loosening: Hormuz reopens in 2027, Qatar partly restarts, the China plant buys on the open market. Bloc helium: the Gulf stays closed into 2028 and Russia licenses helium to China by rail, while service helium in Europe and Japan stays tight. Rationing: the United States adds helium to export licensing or priority allocation, which starves the China plant and could reorder the US plant's cover. It moves R2 and R8. Why uncertain: the repair timing is contested (U-H1) and no state has yet rationed helium by rule. Observable: QatarEnergy force majeure notices, the weekly Lloyd's List Hormuz brief, Russian decrees on Global Trade Alert, and the USGS helium chapter due Jan or Feb 2027.

**U4. Does the China-Japan dual-use spiral reach cryocooler parts shipped from Japan to Tianjin? (N05, N06, N17)** Kind: causation, then outcomes. MOFCOM Announcement No. 1 of 6 Jan 2026 escalated controls on dual-use exports to Japan (Greenberg Traurig, Feb 2026, https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/2/china-imposes-escalated-export-controls-on-dual-use-items-to-japan). Two directions. Contained: lists stay clear of Gifford-McMahon cold heads and shipments continue. Spiral: Japan answers under its Foreign Exchange and Foreign Trade Act and cryogenic equipment to China needs a licence, so the China plant's magnets cannot ship while the US plant is untouched. It moves R5 and R2. Why uncertain: I have not verified whether cold heads sit on any control list; Quality and regulatory can confirm. Observable: MOFCOM announcements and Japan's Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry export notices.

**U5. Does Brazil or China turn niobium into a trade instrument? (N02, N03)** Kind: outcomes. Brazil holds 93 percent of production and China takes 49 percent of its ferroniobium exports (USGS, Feb 2026, https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-niobium.pdf). Two directions. Traded as now: cost rises, nothing stops. Weaponised: China restricts processed niobium as it did gallium and germanium, or Brazil prioritises one buyer, and Western mills ration wire. It moves R4, and R2 where Tianjin draws on Chinese wire. Why uncertain: Brazil has never restricted; China has shown the pattern on other metals. Observable: MOFCOM announcements and Brazil's Comex Stat monthly export data.

**U6. Does the Section 232 medical equipment investigation end in a tariff on finished scanners or their parts? (N17, N18, N31, N36)** Kind: outcomes. Opened 26 Sep 2025 with no proclamation by 11 Oct 2026 (Federal Register 2025-18729, https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/09/26/2025-18729). Three directions: no action, finished scanners only, or parts too. This lens reads it as a cost and share mover, since neither plant ships finished scanners against its home market; it moves R2 and R3 only by flipping which plant serves which market. I expect the foreign trade and customer lenses to weigh it higher. Observable: Federal Register proclamations.

## Critical factors

The 10 Jan 2027 truce date and any Taiwan Strait listing by war-risk insurers are the two observations that move the most risks fastest. Helium reopening dates decide the China plant's 2027, not the US plant's.

## Predetermined versus uncertain

Predetermined, high confidence on public statistics: the four-state helium market, Brazilian niobium, the procurement wall and the parallel payment rail (P3, P6, P9). Uncertain: U1 to U6. One point to flag for round 2: the foreign trade lens placed magnet-grade wire outside the control lists (P7), and I have not verified that against the dual-use category for superconductive composite conductors; a read of the current European Union and United States lists by Quality and regulatory settles it.

## Contradictions with other lenses

None yet; this is round 1.

## Blind spots of this lens

I assume capability becomes action; the 2017 Qatar blockade and the Russian helium licence show states often hold the lever without pulling it. I discount domestic politics, which decide the 232 outcome and the truce more than geography does. I underrate substitution: sealed magnets (P1) cut helium demand per magnet and shrink U3 over the horizon.
