# Infrastructure, phase 2, round 1, summary

## Key points

Qatari helium, about 25% of world supply at full output, is reduced for three to five years after the March 2026 strikes on Ras Laffan, and the Hormuz leg carried four ships on 4 Oct 2026 against a pre-war 85 a day (N01, N19, N23, N27, N34; QatarEnergy LNG page read 11 Oct 2026; Santiago Company 1 Apr 2026 citing CNBC 19 Mar 2026; IMF PortWatch via straits.live 11 Oct 2026).

A 40-foot helium ISO container holds liquid for about 35 to 48 days, the global fleet is estimated at 500 to 700 units with one maker behind most of it, and the US public helium reserve was sold to a private firm on 27 Jun 2024, so there is no buffer outside commercial stock (N23, N19, N01; Linde product page read 11 Oct 2026; Santiago Company 1 Apr 2026, AI-suggested on fleet size; BLM 27 Jun 2024).

The reliability body for North America rates the Carolinas grid elevated from 2027 with winter-morning risk and the Midcontinent grid high from winter 2028, on scheduled coal retirements and contracted data-centre load (N16, N18, N21; NERC 2025 Long-Term Reliability Assessment, 29 Jan 2026).

The East Coast longshore contract runs to 30 Sep 2030 with automation protections, the export port has spare berth after suspending its newest terminal on 1 Aug 2026, and the closure class that breaches 30 days is a channel blockage like Baltimore's 11 weeks in 2024 rather than a hurricane (N26, N25; ILA 25 Feb 2025; Maritime Executive 25 Jun 2026; Transport Topics 11 Jun 2024; Panel judgement on the closure class).

Oversize permits remain a state regime with superloads over 130,000 pounds taking seven to 10 days in South Carolina, and air freight capacity is slack at a 47.1% load factor (N24, N25, N30; J.J. Keller 10 Nov 2025; IATA December 2025 analysis, 29 Jan 2026).

## In one hundred words

The world of 2031 is mostly built. Helium arrives in a few hundred containers that hold liquid for roughly five to seven weeks, the Gulf producer that supplied a quarter of it is down for years with its strait near closed, and the US federal reserve is gone. Both US plant grids are rated tighter from 2027, with the Carolinas risk in January mornings. The port has spare berth and a labour contract to 2030; its real closure risk is a blocked channel, not a storm. Permits cost days. Timing is the uncertainty: Hormuz, Qatari rebuild, container supply, grid curtailment.
