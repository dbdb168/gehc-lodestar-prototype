# Infrastructure, phase 3, round 1

## Analysis

The bottom line from this lens is that the physical questions still open to 2031 are about timing and coincidence, not about whether an asset exists. The helium path into the United States magnet plant depends on a container fleet, a sea route and a domestic pipeline that each exist in small numbers; the two United States sites sit on grids whose ratings tighten from 2027; and the China plant's power and port record has not been checked at all. Seven uncertainties follow, ranked by how many of the eight screened risks each one moves. Every fact below is dated; the read date is 11 Oct 2026. Risks are numbered as in the screen: R1 the United States magnet plant (N16), R2 the China plant (N17), R3 the United States hub (N18), R4 wire (N03), R5 cold heads (N05), R6 programmable logic (N10), R7 technicians (N20), R8 helium cover (N19).

**U1. When does the Gulf helium route reopen to insured traffic, and how much of the container fleet comes back with it?** This moves R8 directly, because cover at N19 is measured in days against a container that holds liquid for about 35 to 48 days (Linde product page, read 11 Oct 2026; Santiago Company, 1 Apr 2026). It moves R1 because the magnet plant's ramp and test at N21 cannot pause once started, and R2 because the China plant imports helium under a Chinese export ban with no end date (phase 2 synthesis P3). Kind of not-knowing: outcomes. Three directions: the strait reopens in 2027 with the roughly 200 stranded containers (Santiago Company, 1 Apr 2026, AI-suggested on the count) returned to rotation within a season; the strait reopens but insurers keep war-risk premiums high enough that producers route by air or by Oman, so the fleet turns slowly; or the strait stays near closed, at the four transits a day seen on 4 Oct 2026 against 85 before the war (IMF PortWatch via straits.live, 11 Oct 2026; NBC News, 24 Sep 2026), into 2028. It is uncertain rather than predetermined because the producer's restart is conditioned on security that satisfies insurers, which no schedule governs. The observable is the daily transit count at IMF PortWatch and the producer's own notice lifting force majeure, published on its operations page. Confidence that this is the first physical uncertainty to watch is high: the holding time is physics. Touches N01, N19, N21, N23, N27, N34. Panel judgement.

**U2. Does the United States helium system stay free of outage and free of export allocation while the Gulf is out?** The United States is the only net exporter among the four producer states and the magnet plant sits inside it (USGS, Feb 2026; phase 2 P4). The federal reserve was sold on 27 Jun 2024 (BLM, 27 Jun 2024), so the Cliffside storage field and its pipeline are one firm's assets. Moves R8 and R1, because a domestic outage leaves the plant with only its own cover; and R2, because the China plant's imports lean on United States and Canadian helium while Qatar is impaired. Kind: outcomes for an outage, actions for an allocation rule, which a government would take. Directions: the system runs through the horizon and Canadian supply keeps rising from its 47 percent share of United States imports (USGS, Feb 2026); a plant or pipeline outage of weeks coincides with the Gulf outage; or a federal allocation or export licence appears, which has no precedent since the sale. Uncertain because a single private asset with no public reserve behind it has no published maintenance calendar. Observables: the annual helium chapter of the USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries (January or February), Federal Register notices, and the owner's force majeure letters, which reach the trade press. Touches N01, N16, N19, N21, N34. AI-suggested that the plant's supplier draws on this system; Logistics confirms.

**U3. Does the elevated grid rating at the two United States sites become curtailment of an industrial customer in a winter morning between 2027 and 2030?** The reliability body rates the Carolinas elevated from 2027 with risk in January mornings and the Midcontinent high from winter 2028 (NERC, 29 Jan 2026, pages 42 and 102). Moves R1 and R3, because an outage stops winding, ramp and assembly; and R8, because on-site liquefaction at N16 needs grid power, so a blackout shortens cover at the moment supply is hardest to replace. Kind: causation. A rating is a probability of shortfall across a region; whether it reaches one plant depends on its tariff class, its interruptible contract and its backup, none of which is public. Directions: no industrial load is shed and the rating stays on paper; rotating outages of hours reach the plant and stop a ramp in progress; or interconnection queues delay any plant expansion. Observables: the reliability body's Winter Reliability Assessment each November, the Midcontinent operator's capacity auction results each April, and energy emergency alerts logged by the regional entities. Grid recovery after Helene ran about ten days (Duke Energy, 30 Sep 2024), which sets the storm case. Touches N16, N18, N19, N21. AI-suggested that liquefaction is grid-dependent and that no full backup exists; Site operations confirms.

**U4. Can the China plant's power and port carry the Eastern Hemisphere load, and the Western load as well if the United States plant is down?** This is a gap, not a finding: the plant's grid record and its port were not researched because the search budget ran out (phase 2 synthesis, gaps). Moves R2 directly and R1 indirectly, because the screen treats the two magnet plants as each other's only cover at N21. Kind: outcomes, with an internal fact to confirm. Directions: the plant has reliable power and a port with oversize handling and can absorb Western orders at a cost in weeks; it can serve Asia only; or export controls or helium scarcity under the Chinese ban idle it. Uncertain because nothing public has been read. Observables: Chinese customs and commerce ministry notices on helium export control, the port's published throughput, and the grid operator's summer and winter supply notices. Touches N17, N21, N29. Placeholder until Site operations supplies the record.

**U5. Do the Asian straits stay open to the OEM's outbound cargo and to the Taiwan foundry's output?** The Taiwan Strait saw about 1,200 crossings a week in 2023 (ONS, 24 Apr 2024) and Malacca 102,500 transits in 2025 (Reuters via Gulf Times, 24 Apr 2026). Moves R2, because N29 has no second plant able to cover a closure, and R6, because the foundry's parts leave Taiwan by air and sea through the same contested space. Kind: outcomes. Directions: grey-zone pressure stays below interdiction; insurers withdraw cover for weeks at a time, which stops sailings without a blockade; or a closure of months. Uncertain because it turns on political choice that no indicator fixes. Observables: the monthly ship crossings bulletin from the United Kingdom statistics office and IMF PortWatch strait counts. Touches N10, N17, N29, N30. Panel judgement on R6.

**U6. Does the one export port close for the class of event that breaches 30 days?** Hurricane closures are two to three days on the port's logs and the longshore contract runs to 30 Sep 2030 (ILA, 25 Feb 2025; SC Ports, 2019 and 2024). The closure that breaches tolerance is a blocked channel, 11 weeks at Baltimore in 2024 (Transport Topics, 11 Jun 2024), or a terminal dispute like the 21-month idling of the newest terminal (Maritime Executive, 25 Jun 2026). Moves R3 only, because the hub's exports leave through it. Kind: outcomes. Directions: short closures as logged; a channel event with no schedule; or a 2030 bargaining stoppage in the final year. Uncertain because the base rate is low and the alternative port, with its own oversize permit chain, has not been shown to have been used. Observables: captain of the port orders, the port authority's monthly volumes, and the 2030 bargaining calendar. Touches N25, N26. AI-suggested that the magnets use this port and that an alternative has never been exercised; Logistics confirms.

**U7. After a large Japanese earthquake, how fast does the cold-head lane recover through air freight and the nearest open port?** The hazard is the natural disasters lens's call; this lens sees only the lane. Air capacity is slack at a 47.1 percent load factor (IATA, 29 Jan 2026), so the constraint is the maker's own restart, not the aircraft. Moves R5. Kind: outcomes. Directions: parts move within days by air; the maker's plant is down for months and no lane helps; or Japanese ports and roads are the bottleneck for a season. Observables: the monthly IATA air cargo analysis and the Japanese earthquake research body's notices. Touches N05, N30. Panel judgement.

## Critical factors

The helium path dominates: U1 and U2 together decide whether cover at N19 is a buffer or a countdown, and they move three risks each. Grid curtailment (U3) is the one uncertainty that can stop both United States sites in the same hour. Confidence on the ranking is moderate: one or two public sources per element and no internal data.

## Predetermined versus uncertain

Predetermined from phase 2: the container holding window, the sale of the federal reserve, the 2027 and 2028 grid ratings, the port contract date and the state permit regime. Uncertain here: U1, U2, U4, U5, U6 and U7 are outcomes; U3 is causation; the allocation branch of U2 is an action by a government. The cover the plant holds at N19 is a value question and a Placeholder. Oversize permits and bridge postings on N24 and N25 cost days and are a watch item, not an uncertainty.

## Blind spots of this lens

I counted ships, containers and megawatts and did not price the war-risk premium or the interruptible tariff that decide whether either moves. I have no view on the China plant beyond its absence from the record.
