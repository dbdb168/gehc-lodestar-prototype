# Financial, phase 3, round 1

## Analysis

Bottom line: the money questions still open to 2031 are about owners and prices, not about whether a supplier exists today. Seven uncertainties follow, ranked by how many of the eight screened risks they move. Most financial uncertainties move the cost of a risk, not the stop, and I say which each time. The round starts from the open list and adds three items it misses: the cost of holding cover, insurance, and insolvency in the private second tier. No new search was run; every source is carried from phase 2.

**U-F1. Do the owners of the wire mill and the cold-head maker still want MR (magnetic resonance) business in 2031?** Extends U-S1. Moves R4 (N03) and R5 (N05) as a stop, because an exit leaves an 18-month requalification with no bridge. Kind: outcomes, with a causation element (is MR a priority for a parent whose backlog fills elsewhere). Directions: the parent reaffirms MR with a long-term agreement; the parent puts MR wire on allocation, so price and lead time rise without a stop; the mill or the cryocooler maker is sold to a buyer who drops MR, as the 2023 sale of the second cryocooler maker to a quantum-cryogenics owner shows can happen ([Bluefors, 28 Mar 2023](https://www.bluefors.com/press-releases/bluefors-closes-the-acquisition-of-cryomech/)). Why uncertain: the listed parent's backlog rose to about $2,569 million at end-2025 "primarily driven by higher BEST order bookings" ([Bruker 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1109354/000119312526082523/brkr-20251231.htm)), and a filing says nothing about who is served when a mill is full. Observable: backlog mix in that annual report on sec.gov each February or March; the Japanese materials group's annual securities report for the second mill; acquisition announcements from either cryocooler owner. AI-suggested: Sourcing confirms the qualified mill and MR's share of its output. If the China plant draws on a Chinese mill, which is unverified, this also moves R2.

**U-F2. Will a year of cover stay priced near 4 percent, and what is a day of cover worth?** Not on the open list. Moves R4, R5, R6 and R8 on cost: stock of wire, cold heads, FPGA (field-programmable gate array, the programmable control chips) and helium is the only bridge inside 18 months for each, and the policy rate prices it. Kind: outcomes for the rate; value for the cover line (Placeholder). Directions: cuts toward 3 percent after 2027; the projected 4.1 percent through 2027 holds; a rise above 5 percent on producer prices up 5.4 percent a year makes a year of magnets in stock an expensive line in the 2027 capital plan. Why uncertain: the Federal Reserve projects only to 2027 ([Federal Reserve, 16 Sep 2026](https://federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm)). Observable: the Federal Open Market Committee statement and projections, eight times a year at federalreserve.gov. The worth of a day of cover is a Placeholder the S&OP (sales and operations planning) council sets.

**U-F3. Does helium stay an allocation market, and does the OEM's contract priority hold through a second Gulf closure?** The price side of U-H1. Moves R8 (N19) as a stop, R1 (N16, which liquefies on site from bulk helium) as a stop if allocation falls short, and R2 (N17, barred from Chinese helium and sitting among Qatar's customers) as a stop. Kind: outcomes for repair timing and price; causation for whether contract seniority survives a producer's force majeure. Directions: Qatar restores volume by 2027 and the base price returns toward the $12 per cubic metre of 2025 ([USGS, Feb 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-helium.pdf)); impairment runs to 2029 or later and buyers stay in allocation near the $97,000 to $128,000 per tonne of early 2026 ([IMARC, 20 May 2026](https://www.imarcgroup.com/news/bulk-helium-price-index)); a second closure brings force majeure again, and the United States plant is served from domestic and Canadian supply while the China plant is not. Why uncertain: one lens found a satellite read that main equipment was intact, five took the minister's three to five years, and no producer has disclosed the state of the helium units. Observable: the USGS helium chapter each February; QatarEnergy media releases; the bulk helium price index. AI-suggested: Sourcing confirms the OEM's contract terms and allocation tier.

**U-F4. What will imported parts and exported scanners cost at the border, in tariffs and in currency?** The cost side of U-T1 and U-T2, plus currency, which no lens priced. Moves R2, R3, R4, R5 and R6, almost entirely on cost: wire, cold heads and logic carry 10 to 12.5 percent today; reclassifying niobium-titanium wire into the copper annex would add 50 percent of its copper content; a Section 232 tariff on finished scanners changes what the United States hub's exports face in retaliation. Only R2 can move as a stop, if the United States and China truce fails before the 2027 sourcing cycle. Kind: outcomes. Directions: current tiers hold; a medical-equipment proclamation lands with retaliation; a trade deal removes the Section 232 measures. On currency, the dollar against the yen, euro and yuan decides whether a fixed-price supply agreement stays economic for either side; I fetched no rate and hold this at low confidence. Why uncertain: the investigation was undecided in June 2026 ([MichBio, 12 Jun 2026](https://www.michbio.org/news/medical-device-industry-watches-key-deadlines-in-federal-tariff-investigation)) and the refined copper duty is proposed, not decided ([MedTech Dive, 2 Apr 2026](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/trump-steel-aluminum-copper-tariff-adjustments/816583/)). Observable: Federal Register proclamations; the wire's declared customs code, which Sourcing confirms; the Federal Reserve's weekly H.10 exchange-rate release.

**U-F5. Will contingent business interruption cover respond to anything on this map by 2031?** Not on the open list. Moves R1, R2 and R8 on the size of the loss, not on delivery: plant damage is insurable today, while a strait closure, a sanction or a producer's allocation is excluded, so a helium stop at N19 is borne in full. Kind: actions (will any insurer write non-damage supply cover) and value (is the premium worth the recovery). Directions: the market stays damage-only; a named-peril product for a Gulf closure appears at a price; cover narrows further after the 2026 losses. Why uncertain: my sources are old ([Business Insurance, 1 Aug 2010](https://www.businessinsurance.com/supply-chain-disruption-coverage-offering-more-options/); [Marsh, undated](https://www.marsh.com/content/marsh2/europe/dk/en_dk/services/business-interruption-supply-chain.html)) and no 2026 market report was reached. Observable: insurer and broker market reports; the OEM's policy wording, which Site operations holds. Low confidence: two old sources.

**U-F6. Does the rise in insolvencies reach the private suppliers under the eight risks?** Not on the open list. Moves R8 through N23: the specialist helium carriers are private, and a carrier's failure lengthens a reroute past the cover line. The fabricators (N07), contract manufacturers (N13) and riggers (N32) are also private but sit outside the eight. Kind: outcomes. Directions: insolvencies rise 6 percent in 2026 and the second tier holds; a carrier fails and a larger gas company absorbs it; a failure is not absorbed. Why uncertain: a fifth consecutive rise is counted ([Allianz Trade, 22 Apr 2026](https://www.allianz-trade.com/en_global/news-insights/news/insolvency-report-2026.html)) but no firm-level accounts are public. Observable: trade registers and Allianz Trade's report each April.

**U-F7. Does hospital margin pressure cut MR orders or only delay them?** The financial side of U-C1. Moves none of the eight risks; it moves the value column (N35, N36), and a smaller order book shrinks the margin that funds cover under U-F2. Kind: value. Directions: capital holds to 2028 and softens on the Medicaid phase-down ([HANYS, Oct 2025](https://www.hanys.org/government_affairs/federal/docs/medicaid-reform/2025-07-obbba-final-summary-chart.pdf)); orders are delayed, not cancelled; orders are cut. Observable: Kaufman Hall's monthly hospital report and Fitch's annual outlook.

## Critical factors

The intentions of two listed parents toward MR wire and cold heads (N03, N05). The policy rate that prices every month of cover (N19, and stock at N03, N05, N10). Helium allocation and contract seniority (N01, N16, N17, N19). Tariff tiers and the dollar against the yen, euro and yuan (N03, N05, N10, N17, N18). The exclusions in business interruption cover (N16, N17, N19). Solvency of the private gas carriers (N23).

## Predetermined versus uncertain

Predetermined from phase 2 and not reopened: private ownership of United States helium, the Russian helium ban, Brazilian ownership of niobium, the Medicaid phase-down dates, and the tariff tiers through 2027. Uncertain: U-F1 to U-F7. The order of consequence is not the order of count: U-F1 moves two risks as a stop with no bridge, while U-F4 moves five almost entirely as cost. Panel judgement.

## Contradictions with other lenses

None yet; this is round 1. One difference with the contrarian's phase 2 reading stands: the contrarian holds that helium is not a Gulf story for the United States plant because imports shifted toward Canada; I hold that the China plant and the world price still carry it (U-F3). The USGS import shares each February and the China plant's helium contract, which Sourcing holds, would settle it.

## Blind spots of this lens

I read ownership and rates, not engineering, so I may still underweight the 18 months a willing mill takes to requalify. I could not read private accounts for the cryocooler maker, the gas carriers or any Chinese mill. I priced no currency. My insurance sources are old.
