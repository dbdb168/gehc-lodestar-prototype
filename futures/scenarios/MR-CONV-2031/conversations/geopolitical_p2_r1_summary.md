# Geopolitical, phase 2, round 1, summary

## Key points

Helium is a four-state market (United States 81, Qatar 63, Russia 18, Algeria 11 of 190 million cubic metres in 2025), Russia is sanctioned by the EU and United States and has licensed its own exports until 31 Dec 2027, and the US magnet plant sits in the only producer that is a net exporter (N01, N16, N19, N34; USGS, 5 Feb 2026; Global Trade Alert, 14 Apr 2026).

Qatar's Ras Laffan helium has been offline since early March 2026 and about 14 percent of its export capacity is reported damaged for up to five years, so roughly a tenth of 2025 world capacity is out to about 2031 whatever happens to the war (N01, N23, N34; IOM3, 23 Mar 2026, reporting a QatarEnergy statement).

The Gulf sea lane is under active attack at the date of writing, with major container lines avoiding Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb transits 70 percent below pre-crisis, so 2026 and early 2027 Gulf installs run through a closed or near-closed chokepoint (N27, N23; Lloyd's List Intelligence, 18 and 30 Sep 2026).

Niobium is 93 percent Brazilian with China taking 49 percent of Brazil's ferroniobium exports, so by 2031 the leverage on magnet wire sits with one producer state and one dominant buyer, reaching the OEM through the wire mill rather than the ore (N02, N03; USGS, Feb 2026; Panel judgement on the mill).

The EU excluded Chinese bidders from device procurement above 5 million euros on 20 Jun 2025 and China barred EU firms from imported-device procurement above 45 million yuan on 6 Jul 2025 while exempting products made in China, so the Tianjin plant keeps market access that a US-built scanner loses (N17, N29, N36; Regulation (EU) 2025/1197; CMS, Jul 2025; AI-suggested as to which plant serves which market, S&OP council confirms).

## In one hundred words

Four things are fixed to 2031. Helium comes from four states, two of which have already acted: Russia is sanctioned and self-licensed, and Qatar's plant is partly damaged for years. Niobium comes from Brazil whatever anyone does. The EU and China have built a reciprocal procurement wall with no expiry that rewards the China plant. The Gulf lane is a war zone, not a route, today. Still open: when Hormuz and Ras Laffan reopen, whether the US-China truce survives the 2027 sourcing cycle, whether Section 232 reaches finished scanners, and whether Taiwan grey-zone pressure becomes interdiction.
