# Natural disasters, phase 6a, round 1, summary

## Key points

Weather breaks the line only where helium still decides: in F1 and F3 a Carolinas storm or winter curtailment takes the liquefier's feeder and the trailer road while the next container is in its 35 to 48 day hold, and helium cover runs out, from the 2027 hurricane season at the earliest and the winter of 2028 to 2029 on schedule (N16, N19, N24; Panel judgement).

Geology breaks the line only where the cold head carries every magnet: in F2 and F4 a Nankai Trough event or outage at the one Japanese maker stops both plants for longer than 30 days, because 18 months of requalification outlasts any stock (N05, N16, N17; AI-suggested, Engineering and Sourcing confirm the address).

One hazard reaches three nodes at once: an Atlantic storm that closes the magnet plant, the oversize road and the export port together, which is why the single-site exposure is shared by all four futures (N16, N24, N26; Panel judgement).

The 35-day encryption event breaches in every future and lands worst in F1, where a locked gas carrier or port terminal walks through the helium cover and stops the plant for 35 to 50 days (N23, N26, N19, N16; Panel judgement).

Three Placeholders decide whether three risks break or cost: cover days at N19 above 60, backup power at N16 off the plant feeder, and the cold-head maker's address outside the Nankai zone (N19, N16, N05; Placeholder).

## In one hundred words

Through this lens the four futures split in pairs. Where helium still decides (F1, F3), the first hazard break is helium cover at the US magnet plant, reached by a storm or winter curtailment that takes power and road while a container is in its hold, from 2027. Where helium no longer decides (F2, F4), the break is the sole cold-head maker, where an earthquake or outage stops both plants for the 18 months a second source takes. The encryption break breaches everywhere, worst in F1. Against five lenses, a cold-head loss breaks the line in every future.
