# Contrarian, phase 6a, round 1

## Analysis

Bottom line: the four pages differ in cause but not in shape. Three risks never break anywhere, one breaks everywhere once the encryption event (C4) is counted, and the China plant breaches in all four.

F1. Helium cover (N19, R8) fails in the winter of 2028 to 2029 and takes the US magnet plant (N16, R1) into a 30 to 45 day stop. The China plant's allocation queue (N17, R2) breaches by late installs in the first half of 2028. Confidence low to moderate; a confirmed cover above 60 days removes the US break.

F2. The cold head (N05, R5) stops both plants (N16, N17) between 2029 and 2031, undated. The technicians (N20, R7) put installs two to three quarters late in 2029, low confidence. Confidence moderate on the node, low on the date. The maker's address against the Nankai Trough zone removes the hazard path but not the outage path.

F3. The China plant (N17, R2) stops through its logic supplier (N10, R6) in the second half of 2027; share moves. Helium cover (N19, R8) breaches second by early 2028 and carries the US plant (N16, R1) with it. Confidence moderate on the node, low on the date. Logic stock at N17 longer than the licence queue turns the first break into a cost.

F4. The cold head (N05, R5) stops both plants by 2029 for most of 18 months; the installed base breaches service cover through N34. Confidence moderate. A multi-year agreement with the parent in hand turns it into a cost; a wire listing first moves the node to N03.

## Critical factors

Three breaks are shared; only the first was planned.

First, the encryption event (C4). At 35 days it breaches the line in every future at whichever single-source site has no manual fallback: N16, N03, N05, the gas carriers (N23) or the Charleston terminal (N26). In F1 it is worst at the carriers and the terminal, which walk through the helium cover. In F2 and F4 it is indistinguishable from the first break if it lands on the cold-head maker. Cost everywhere is one stop past 30 days. Confidence high that it breaches, moderate on where, because the fallback Placeholder is unset.

Second, the China plant (N17) breaches in every column: by queue in F1, by listing in F3, by cold head in F2 and F4. The panel has written four futures in which the Eastern Hemisphere always fails tolerance and not named it as a shared break.

Third, the risks that never break are shared too, and that is the attack. The US hub (N18, R3) only costs on every page. The wire (N03, R4) only costs. The technicians (N20, R7) move on no external uncertainty, so their break date is the same in all four futures, and no page sets it. They are dashed because no future tested them.

## Predetermined versus uncertain

Predetermined across all four: one cold-head maker, one wire mill, one design record, a China plant that breaches. Outcomes uncertainty: which plant a trade stop lands on and when. Causation uncertainty: whether an elevated grid rating becomes a multi-week curtailment (F1), and whether the sealed test draw is a delivery or a cylinder (F1 against F2). Value uncertainty, on no page: whether a late install is punished at all (C5, N36), which decides whether any break costs share.

## Contradictions with other lenses

The sealed-side dates are votes, not evidence. F2 says "no schedule" and then "2029 to 2031"; F4's 2029 rests on an allocation nobody has announced. The parent's segment disclosure of MR's share of the cold-head order book settles it (Sourcing).

The panel kept the cold head over the wire in F4 by eight to three because a destination-addressed listing strands one plant. That applies equally to a cryocooler listing. What makes the cold head total is the parent leaving, not the listing; if the wire mill's parent is the weaker owner, the node flips.

## Blind spots of this lens

Dashing the hub, the wire and the technicians may be provocation rather than impact; no page tested them, and I have no evidence they break before 2031.
