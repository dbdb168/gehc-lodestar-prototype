# Infrastructure, phase 4, round 2, summary

## Key points

F1 is the future where trade stays a cost and magnets still need helium, so the delivery clock is the container, the grid and the road rather than any rule. N19, N23, N16; Panel judgement.

The first break is helium cover at the United States magnet plant, when a winter curtailment or basin flood takes the liquefier's power and the trailer's road while the next container is still in its 35 to 48 day hold. N19, N16, N24; Placeholder on cover days, NERC 29 January 2026.

It lands in the winter of 2028 to 2029, or any hurricane season from 2027, and costs one line stop of 30 to 45 days and one quarter of Western Hemisphere installs late. N16, N19; Panel judgement, moderate.

Financial puts the first break at the China plant through an allocation letter, which I reject because a shorter delivery is absorbed by Asian spot and the hold window while a dark liquefier is not; Contrarian, Macroeconomic and Customer and market agree with Financial. N17, N19; Panel judgement.

The break disappears if confirmed cover at N19 exceeds the container hold plus the ten-day grid rebuild and the liquefier has backup power off the plant feeder. N19, N16; Placeholder, Duke Energy 30 September 2024.

## In one hundred words

In F1 nothing is listed and nothing is stopped by a rule, so the line lives on the physical margin: a 500 to 700 unit container fleet cycling against grids rated elevated from 2027 and high from 2028. Cover at the United States magnet plant is the node every weather and grid event walks through, and it breaks in the winter of 2028 to 2029 for 30 to 45 days. Four lenses say the China plant breaks first on Gulf helium; I say a shorter delivery is not a stop. Cover days and allocation seniority would settle it, and both are Placeholders.
