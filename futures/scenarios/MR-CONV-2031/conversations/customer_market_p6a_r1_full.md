# Customer and market, phase 6a, round 1

## Analysis

All four futures breach the tolerance line, but only F3 and F4 move share. In F1 and F2 every supplier is short of the same input at once, and a tender committee cannot mark one supplier unreliable for a lateness its competitors match.

F1. Helium cover at the United States plant (R8, N19) breaks in the winter of 2028 to 2029, giving a 30 to 45 day stop at N16 (R1) and a quarter of Western installs more than 30 days late. The China plant's allocation queue (R2, N17) breaches in the first half of 2028. Every competitor needs the same helium, so share holds (Panel judgement). Confidence moderate, low on the date because cover days are a Placeholder; cover above the container hold plus the ten-day grid rebuild removes the Western break.

F2. The cold head (R5, N05) breaks between 2029 and 2031, and both plants (R1, R2) stop longer than 30 days. The customer sees a dark scanner: sealed magnets warm when a cold head fails and service (N33, N34) competes with new builds for the part. If competitors buy from the same maker the market warms together and share holds; if one holds a second source, installed-base lock-in flips against the OEM (Panel judgement; Sourcing confirms the maker's customer list, AI-suggested). Confidence moderate. A second source qualified before the sealed share passes half removes the break.

F3. The China plant (R2, N17) breaks through United States-origin logic (R6, N10) in the second half of 2027, and Asian installs run late for two quarters or more. Share moves: Japan and India are one tender cycle from a China-built competitor that keeps delivering (N36, N29). Helium cover (R8, N19) breaches second by early 2028. Confidence moderate. The Placeholder is the plant's logic stock in weeks; stock longer than the licence queue removes the break.

F4. The cold head (R5, N05) breaks by 2029 with the gap into 2030. Both plants stop for most of 18 months and the installed base breaches service cover through N34. The lateness lands on Europe's replacement peak, so share moves through N36. Confidence moderate. The Placeholder is the price at which the OEM loses its allocation; a multi-year agreement with the maker's parent or a second maker before 2029 removes the break.

Shared and single. Every future stops at least one plant longer than 30 days, and R2 breaks in all four. The cold head breaks in F2 and F4. Helium cover as first break belongs to F1 alone; the China plant through logic to F3 alone. R3, R4 and R7 cost only everywhere.

The shared encryption break (C4). A 35-day loss of systems is a stop longer than 30 days in every future, and it is the one break where share can move in all four, because competitors are not hit together and a committee reads it as the supplier's own failure (Panel judgement, moderate). In F1 it lands at the gas carriers (N23) or the port terminal (N26), reaches N19 and costs a quarter of Western installs. In F2 and F4 it lands at the cold-head maker and is indistinguishable from the first break. In F3 it lands at the China plant on top of the listing stop.

## Critical factors

Whether competitors share the same helium, cold-head and wire sources decides whether lateness is common to the market. Whether tenders score delivery reliability at all (C5, N36) is unverified and decides whether lateness becomes lost share or only margin.

## Predetermined versus uncertain

Predetermined: Europe's 2016 to 2021 cohort passes ten years old by 2031 (COCIR, 2023); cold-head requalification takes 18 months. Uncertain: outcomes, whether any tender scores lateness; value, whether a committee weighs a dark scanner above a late install; causation, whether a competitor's cold head comes from the same maker.

## Contradictions with other lenses

On F2 the Technology shift draft treats the dark scanner as the OEM's event; this lens says that if the maker serves the whole market, share holds. The maker's customer list settles it. On F1 the panel put the Western cover break first; from the buyer's chair the scheduled China queue is worse, because it lands in short-cycle tender markets.

## Blind spots of this lens

This lens sees the buyer and misses the factory, overrates how rationally tenders score, and may miss shared upstream dependencies.
