# Contrarian, phase 4, round 1

## Analysis

Bottom line: three futures break first at a node the obvious story does not name, and F2 contains no external break at all, which is why the sponsor will like it. Every future assumes the design and qualification record (N22) is intact; the encryption break (C4) sits in phase 6a. Demand shape (C5) changes each bill, not each break.

### F1 "Cost only, helium still decides" (U1 A, U2 A)

2026 to 2028: October 2026 extended. The Section 232 medical investigation lands a duty on finished scanners, not parts (Federal Register, 26 Sep 2025); the truce is renewed in 2027; Hormuz reopens in stages and Qatari helium stays under force majeure (straits.live, 11 Oct 2026). 2028 to 2031: helium is allocated rather than priced; the Carolinas grid is rated elevated from 2027 (NERC, 29 Jan 2026).

The attack: the panel will write this as the United States magnet plant (N16) walking through its helium cover (N19) in a winter curtailment. But that plant sits inside the one net-exporting producer, and Canada already supplies 47 percent of United States imports (USGS, Feb 2026). The plant with no domestic helium is the China plant (N17): its Asian supply ran through Qatar and Hormuz (USGS, Feb 2026; P3). So R2 N17 breaks first, through N01 and N27, with allocated test helium pushing Asian installs past 30 days in late 2027. Panel judgement. Cost: a quarter's Asian installs late, no line stop. Most confident: the two plants face different helium worlds (P4). Least confident: whether the China plant holds a buffer; Site operations confirms.

### F2 "Cost only, helium no longer decides" (U1 A, U2 B)

2026 to 2028: Hormuz reopens, the Qatari units restart faster than the minister's estimate (the satellite read of 6 March 2026 is the only evidence), and the sealed 1.5 tesla platform (FDA K253779, 5 Feb 2026) takes new builds while a 3 tesla sealed clearance arrives by 2028. 2028 to 2031: a buyers' market; every measure stays a 10 to 12.5 percent duty (Supreme Court, 20 Feb 2026).

The attack: this future is defined by the absence of breaks. The only risk moving on no external uncertainty is R7 N20, and it breaks first, not by a strike (South Carolina union density is 2.8 percent, BLS 2025) but because sealed conversion retools the winding and test trades on a roster whose age is an unconfirmed AI-suggested fact. A two-quarter retraining gap around 2029 puts Western installs past 30 days. Panel judgement, low confidence. The bill runs through N35 and N36: hospital margins at 2.5 percent (Kaufman Hall, 27 Aug 2026) and European tenders closed to Chinese-origin devices (Regulation (EU) 2025/1197, 20 Jun 2025) move share, not delivery. Most confident: nothing stops. Least confident: the 3 tesla clearance arrives; no filing exists.

### F3 "A part stops, helium still decides" (U1 B, U2 A)

2026 to 2028: the truce lapses at its first read date (10 November 2026) or the second, entity listings follow, and the two-way parts flow through the China plant (N17) is cut while Hormuz stays shut. 2028 to 2031: the Gulf repair slips; a producer-country notice names a cryogenic part.

The attack: the consensus break is wire (N03) behind a licence. Wire has mills in five countries, 18 months to qualify and no listing (P2, P7), so it breaks second. R2 N17 breaks first, through N31 and N29, because the stop most ready to land is the one already dated (P9). A line stop at the China plant longer than 30 days by mid-2027; Eastern installs late for the rest of the horizon. Panel judgement, moderate confidence. Most confident: the truce has a date and listings follow it. Least confident: whether the listing names control logic (N10) or cold heads (N05); the Federal Register notice settles it.

### F4 "A part stops, helium no longer decides" (U1 B, U2 B)

2026 to 2028: the Gulf reopens, the only route to U2 B that does not require a conversion programme during a parts stop; conventional magnets still dominate. 2028 to 2031: a producer-country listing lands on a cryogenic input by 2029.

The attack: the panel wrote the sealed futures with the cold head (N05) as the break. But sealed and conventional magnets are both wound from niobium-titanium wire, and no cleared magnet exists without it (P2). Wire is the one input common to every magnet, with one qualified mill and niobium leverage sitting with one producer state and one dominant buyer (USGS, Feb 2026; P6). R4 N03 breaks first, through N02 and N31, with line stops at N16 and N17 longer than 30 days from the first quarter of 2029. Panel judgement, moderate confidence. Most confident: wire is the common denominator. Least confident: that a listing reaches wire before cold heads; the owner question (U-S1) could land earlier.
