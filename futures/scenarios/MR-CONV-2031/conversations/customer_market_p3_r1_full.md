# Customer and market, phase 3, round 1

## Analysis

Bottom line: from the buyer's side, the uncertainty that touches all eight risks at once is not upstream. It is whether a late install costs the OEM share, or whether a shared shortage makes every supplier late together so that nobody moves. Behind it sit four uncertainties about what buyers pay, what they specify, and where a scanner must be built to be allowed to bid. Five uncertainties follow, ranked by how many of the eight risks (R1 to R8, in the screen's order) each moves. A risk counts as moved if the chance of a 30-day breach changes or the cost of that breach changes; the table at the end shows which. No new web search was possible, so every fact comes from a phase 2 transcript that carries its source.

**U1. When the OEM is late, does the hospital punish it, or is everyone late together?** Kind: value, with a causation element. No retrieved tender framework scores delivery reliability (P19; NHS England, Oct 2020). Three directions: delivery performance enters award criteria with a published weight, as origin and carbon already have (Regulation (EU) 2025/1197, 20 Jun 2025; PPN 06/21, 5 Jun 2021); it stays unscored and is punished quietly at re-tender; or a shortage hits every supplier and committees accept allocation, as gas suppliers already cut customers to 50 percent while prioritising healthcare (WestAir, 21 May 2026, secondary). The third direction holds only if competitors share the OEM's wire mill and cold-head source: one wire producer claims over a third of global MR (magnetic resonance) scanners (SEC Form C/A, 2025, low confidence) and four-kelvin cold heads come principally from one Japanese group (producer page, 2026), but no supplier publishes its customer list. AI-suggested; Sourcing confirms. It is uncertain because no health system publishes a delivery audit and no retrieved award cites a late delivery. It moves the cost, not the chance, of all eight: in the first direction a 31-day install from any cause is lost share at N36; in the third it is a waiting list. Panel judgement, moderate confidence. Indicator: an award criterion naming lead time or past delivery in an MR tender on the European Tenders Electronic Daily portal or the UK Find a Tender service, or competitor delays coinciding with the OEM's.

**U2. Does the United States tax imported scanners and parts, and does the China truce hold through the 2027 sourcing cycle?** Kind: outcomes. The Section 232 investigation names MRI machines and had no proclamation by 11 Oct 2026 (Federal Register, 26 Sep 2025; P8). Three directions: no tariff; a tariff on finished scanners only, which favours the US-assembled line against imports and pushes US demand onto N18; or a tariff reaching components on top of the 10 to 12.5 percent Section 301 rates (EY, Jul 2026; P7). Lenses disagree on the truce: renewal around 10 Nov 2026 (foreign trade) or extended to 10 Jan 2027 (geopolitical). Uncertain because a Section 232 proceeding can end without a tariff and the rate and country list are political choices. It moves R2 (N17 loses the US market), R3 (N18 carries more demand with no backup) and R4, R5, R6 (component cost, or a forced change of qualified source). Indicator: a proclamation headed "Adjusting imports of medical equipment" in the Federal Register, and the tariff notices at the truce dates.

**U3. Do hospitals require sealed magnets, or merely accept them?** Kind: value, with outcomes. Sealed 1.5 tesla magnets are cleared at three makers (P1), and the English NHS (National Health Service) stops buying from suppliers outside its net zero commitment before 2030 (NHS England, Oct 2020). Three directions: tenders require sealed designs by 2029 and the conventional order book shrinks to 3 tesla and to markets without carbon scoring; tenders prefer but do not require them; or hospitals hold back from a first-generation design and the conventional line keeps most orders to 2031. Uncertain because no sealed installed-base count and no tender requiring one were found. It moves R8 (less helium per new build at N19 if the mix shifts), R5 (a sealed magnet has no helium bath to ride through a cold-head outage, so the single Japanese source becomes a field dependency at N33 as well as a build one) and R1 and R2 (which plant converts its test capacity at N21). Panel judgement on the cold head; Engineering confirms whether the sealed design uses the same one. Indicator: tender specifications requiring sealed designs, new clearances under FDA product code LNH in openFDA, any maker's sealed installed-base count.

**U4. Does hospital money pressure cut MR orders, delay them, or get overtaken by the replacement wave?** Kind: causation, with a buyer-side action. US hospital margins were 2.5 percent in the first half of 2026, Medicaid phases down from fiscal 2028, and 2026 Medicare rates carry a 2.5 percent efficiency cut (Kaufman Hall, 27 Aug 2026; HANYS, Oct 2025; CMS, 31 Oct 2025). Against that, over a quarter of Europe's installed base was more than ten years old in 2023 (COCIR, 2023) and China funds renewal to 2027 (State Council, 13 Mar 2024). Three directions: a US slump that eases the single plants; a surge in Europe and Asia that loads N16, N17 and N18 at once; or deferral that compresses the surge into 2029 to 2031. Uncertain because replacement need is fixed and orders are not, and no 2026 capital survey was retrieved. It moves R1, R2, R3 (volume against single sites) and R7 (hiring against a tight roster at N20). Indicator: the monthly Kaufman Hall flash report, CMS final rules each November, COCIR's next age profile, Eurostat MRI counts.

**U5. Does where a scanner is built decide where it can be sold?** Kind: outcomes. The EU excludes Chinese-origin devices from tenders above 5 million euros with no expiry; China bars imported EU devices above 45 million yuan while exempting China-made ones (Regulation (EU) 2025/1197; CMS Law, Jul 2025; P9). Three directions: the walls stay, so N17 serves Asia and N18 the rest; the EU adds a European preference to its procurement directives, as signalled for 2026, reaching a US-built scanner through N28 and N36; or China extends domestic preference below the tender threshold through subsidies and permit quotas after 2027. Uncertain because neither measure has a sunset, the EU revision is a proposal, and China's permit plan after 2027 is unpublished. It moves R1 and R3 (the US plants lose or keep Europe) and R2 (N17 becomes the only route into China, or loses Japan and India through N29). Indicator: the directive proposal on EUR-Lex, Ministry of Finance notices on gov.cn, and a new large-equipment configuration plan from China's health authority, not retrieved this round.

## Critical factors

U1 decides how much the tolerance line is worth: if committees never score delivery and shortages are shared, the eight risks cost waiting lists, not share. U2 and U5 together fix which plant may serve which market, and the single US final assembly site (N18) serving Europe is the node every wall reaches. U3 is where the buyer's choice changes the physics of the list, trading helium cover (R8) for cold-head dependency (R5).

## Predetermined versus uncertain

Relied on as predetermined: the procurement walls (P9), the open Section 232 file (P8), the 2026 Medicare rates and Medicaid schedule (P17), the aged European fleet (P17), the cleared sealed class (P1) and the absence of a published delivery score (P19). U1 and U3 are value questions with no schedule; U2 and U5 are outcomes with named documents that appear or not by mid-2027; U4 resolves only through data a function gathers.

## Contradictions with other lenses

Round 1 is isolated. Expected: the technology lens rates sealed adoption faster than this lens, settled by the sealed share of 2027 orders held by the S&OP council; the foreign trade and geopolitical lenses give different truce dates, settled by the tariff notices; any lens treating a wire or cold-head loss as the OEM's alone contradicts the third direction of U1 until Sourcing names the mill.

## Blind spots of this lens

This lens reads the buyer and misses the factory, so U4 is about volume and says nothing about whether a plant can absorb it. It overrates how rationally committees decide. It could not retrieve China's permit plan, a sealed installed-base count or a 2026 capital survey, so U3 to U5 rest on 2023 to 2025 data. It treats Japan and India (N29) as a China question, which they may not be.
