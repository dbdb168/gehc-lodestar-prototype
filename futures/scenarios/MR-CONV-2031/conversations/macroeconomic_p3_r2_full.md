# Macroeconomic, phase 3, round 2

## Analysis

Bottom line: this lens accepts the moderator's axes, C1 (restriction becomes a stop) and C2 (helium stays a line risk), with one wording change: C1 covers parts and finished scanners only, because a United States export rule on helium (my U-M4) decides the helium question and inside C1 would make the axes overlap. Neither of my round 1 leaders survives: demand (now C5) moves what a break costs, not whether it breaks, and the rate and dollar path prices every future without opening a risk. Panel judgement, moderate confidence.

Independence, one sentence per corner. Stop and helium stays a risk: a listing of wire or cold heads strands the China plant while the Gulf is shut, so N17 loses parts and N19 loses cover together (N17, N03, N05, N19). Stop and helium no longer a risk: sealed magnets take new builds, but the listing still stops the China plant's parts (N17, N03, N05, N34). No stop and helium stays a risk: parts move freely, yet Qatari units stay impaired and suppliers allocate, so shortage reaches the cover (N01, N16, N19). No stop and helium no longer a risk: only the single sites, the design record and the technicians remain, and no external axis moves them (N16, N18, N20, N22). All four corners hold.

C1 and C2 move seven of eight risks. The rate and dollar path (Federal Reserve, 16 Sep and 5 Oct 2026) prices every corner; helium is the fastest route to a stop in two of them (N19).

## Predetermined versus uncertain

Predetermined: the rate path to 2027, tariffs in force, Qatari impairment at the read date, the age of Europe's fleet. Uncertain: which corner the world reaches by 2031 (outcomes), and the helium a sealed magnet consumes at test (causation, Placeholder for Engineering).

## Contradictions with other lenses

Three corrections to my round 1. The Customer and market lens is right that demand moves consequence only, except through supplier exit (N03, N05). The Contrarian lens showed Qatar's share of United States imports fell to 28 percent while Canada's rose to 47 percent (USGS, Feb 2026), so a Gulf shortage reaches the China plant before the United States cover, not both equally. The Technology shift lens showed helium turns on an unpublished litres-at-test number, which no price clears. One contradiction stands: the Financial lens ranks supplier exit (C6) a stop with no bridge; I rank it a watch item because no filing shows intent. The parents' next annual reports settle it.

## Blind spots of this lens

I see aggregates. The litres-at-test Placeholder and the China plant's helium origin decide more than any index I read. I underweight the political route to a helium rule, hence the request that C1 stay on parts.
