# Financial, phase 6a, round 1, summary

## Key points

The sole cold-head maker breaks in F2 and F4 and only costs in F1 and F3, because helium is the fallback that turns an outage into a bill, and the parent's minority MR share means no hazard is needed for the stop (N05, N16, N17; Panel judgement, phase 2 P18 AI-suggested). Helium cover is a working-capital failure in F1, inventory carried at a 4 percent rate against producer prices rising about 5 percent a year, so it thins each cycle unless funded (N19, N16; Federal Reserve, 16 September 2026). F3 is the costliest per quarter because share moves and payment rails become a licence queue, and government-action stops are uninsurable (N17, N10, N31, N36; Panel judgement). The 35-day encryption event is a line stop in all four futures at the wire mill, the cold-head maker or the US plant, with cyber cover partly excluded for state action (N03, N05, N16, N23, N26; Panel judgement). The Placeholders that remove breaks are cover days at N19, logic stock weeks at N17, and the cold-head parent's MR share (N19, N17, N05; Placeholder).

## In one hundred words

Through the financial lens two breaks are shared by all four futures: the sole cold-head maker, whose parent earns a minority of revenue from MR and can allocate away or be bought without any hazard, and the 35-day encryption event, a line stop wherever it lands. Helium cover breaks only where helium still decides, and it fails as working capital before it fails as logistics. The China plant breaks earliest, in F3, and that stop is uninsurable. The bills are set by three Placeholders: cover days, logic stock in weeks, and the cold-head parent's revenue share, which Sourcing confirms.
