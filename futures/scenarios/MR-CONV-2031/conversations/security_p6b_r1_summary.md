# Security, phase 6b, round 1, summary

## Key points

The rehearsed 30-day manual fallback (A9) works in all four futures because the 35-day encryption event is dated by no future and can land any day, and it only counts if the wire mill and cold-head maker are in the room, since the OEM's own network is rarely the way in. N16, N17, N18, N03, N05, N22, N23, N26. Panel judgement.

Security adds a missing action, M1: an offline, restore-tested copy of the design and qualification record, because every future page assumes that record intact and no candidate action keeps it so; a record loss turns every 18-month requalification into an unknown. N22, N03, N05, N16, N17. Panel judgement.

Confirming the Placeholders (A10) works in all four futures and costs nothing; every other action is currently sized on synthetic numbers. N19, N16, N05, N03, N17. Placeholder.

The second cold-head maker (A1) and the non-United States logic path (A6) are the two-future actions that bind hardest on the 2027 clock, because their 18-month requalification started after mid-2027 lands after the F3 and F4 breaks. N05, N10, N17. Panel judgement.

Security M2, counterfeit avoidance and cyber attestation for sole suppliers and carriers, is cheap and works in all four, but attestation must not replace the tabletop. N03, N05, N08 to N12, N23, N26, N31. Panel judgement.

## In one hundred words

Through the security lens the top three are A9 (manual fallback, rehearsed with the sole suppliers), Security M1 (an offline, restore-tested copy of the design and qualification record) and A10 (confirm the Placeholders). All three work in every future; A9 and M1 cost more to delay because the encryption break is undated and every page assumes N22 is intact. A1 and A6 are two-future actions whose 18-month clocks make 2027 bind. A2 and A5 are single-future bets. A11 moves the line between futures and raises the value of A1 and A9 if conversion makes one Japanese part hold every magnet.
