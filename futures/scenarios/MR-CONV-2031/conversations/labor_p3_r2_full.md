## Analysis

Bottom line first. This lens keeps the moderator's first axis, C1, and replaces the second. C2 as written, "does helium still decide whether a magnet ships", can resolve to no because the Gulf route reopens, which changes nothing on the plant floor, or because the magnet is sealed, which changes every trade on it. Those are different futures for N16 and N20, so the second axis is C2 merged with C3 and worded as an outcome: **"In 2031, is most of what the OEM builds and ships still a helium-cooled conventional magnet?"** Customer and market made this an outcome, not only a decision, by showing tenders may require sealed magnets; Technology shift showed the sealed platform is cleared and sold (FDA K253779, 5 Feb 2026). Gulf helium and allocation become indicators inside the axis. Kind: outcomes. Confidence moderate; the trade consequence is Panel judgement.

Two corrections to round 1. The moderator's note says this lens found no external axis that moves R7. My L1 table marked R7: a strike or retirement cliff is internal, but a sealed majority replaces one 18-month trade with another, which is external when the market drives conversion (N20; Panel judgement). Second, I wrote that R8 eases under conversion. Technology shift is right that test-helium litres per sealed magnet are unpublished, so R8 is a Placeholder for Engineering (N19, N21).

**Independence.** Stop and helium majority: a wire or cold-head listing stops a conventional line and every helium hand is still needed (N03, N05, N16, N20). Stop and sealed majority: the Contrarian's Shenzhen dependence holds for a competitor, but the OEM's sealed magnet is wound at N16, so it can convert behind a wall, with the cold head at N05 the one part a listing reaches. Cost only and helium majority: open trade, Gulf helium back, hospitals keep buying conventional 3 tesla; the only labour risk is roster age at N20. Cost only and sealed majority: the easy combination. All four occur; the axes are independent.

## Predetermined versus uncertain

Predetermined: the state labour gaps, the roster age profile, the 2030 port date, the supplier countries' shrinking working-age populations. Uncertain: both axes. C4 is an event, not a structure; its labour form, whether a plant can run 30 days on paper, is set by whether the fallback was drilled, which is internal (N16, N22; causation).

Placement: C4 is a shared break in phase 6a, as the moderator suggests. C5 is a cost modifier on every future, with its volume element a watch item for the S&OP council. C7 is a dated watch item (NERC, 29 Jan 2026) and a modifier on the helium-majority futures only, because curtailment reaches the line through liquefaction (N16, N18, N19).
