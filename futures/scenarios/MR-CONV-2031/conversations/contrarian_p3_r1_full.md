# Contrarian, phase 3, round 1

## Analysis

The bottom line is that the two axes the scope packet proposed move at most four of the eight risks between them, and the uncertainty that moves five is one neither axis touches: an encryption event at one of the three single sites or at a sole supplier. Dimension B is withdrawn, and what replaced it (U-A1, the pace of sealed magnets) is mostly the OEM's own decision. Dimension A, read as tariffs, moves none of the eight, because every input tariff on this map raises cost and stops nothing; read as interdiction, it moves three.

The eight risks keep the screen's order: R1 the United States magnet plant (N16), R2 the China plant (N17), R3 the United States hub (N18), R4 the wire (N03), R5 the cold head (N05), R6 the logic (N10), R7 the technicians (N20) and R8 helium cover (N19). An uncertainty moves a risk only if it changes whether losing that node breaches the 30-day line, not the cost of keeping it.

**C1. Does an encryption event take out one of the three single sites, or the sole wire mill or cold-head maker, for longer than 30 days?** Outcomes. A vehicle maker attacked on 2 Sep 2025 restarted in phases by 8 Oct 2025, and a brewer had 30 plants disrupted for months (BleepingComputer, 15 Nov 2025; Food Ingredients First, 18 Mar 2026). Neither is a magnet maker, so the transfer is Panel judgement, moderate confidence. It is uncertain because the base rate is measured but the hit list is not. Directions: no event inside the horizon; an event at a supplier that the 18-month requalification cannot absorb; an event at a site. It shares no cause with trade or magnet technology, so it is the third uncertainty that makes both axes irrelevant. It resolves through Item 1.05 of Form 8-K filings by the listed parents of the wire mills and the cold-head second source at SEC EDGAR, and through the OEM's incident log, which only Site operations can read. Moves R1, R2, R3, R4, R5.

**C2. Does the elevated grid rating at both United States sites become a multi-week curtailment?** Outcomes, with a causation element: whether liquefaction runs on grid power is AI-suggested, and Site operations confirms it. NERC (the North American Electric Reliability Corporation) rates the Carolinas elevated from 2027 and the Midcontinent high from winter 2028 (NERC, 29 Jan 2026); Helene produced about ten days of grid recovery (Duke Energy, 30 Sep 2024). A rating is a chance of shortfall, not an outage. Directions: no curtailment; winter-morning curtailments of hours; a storm on a thin grid that takes liquefaction out for weeks. It resolves in NERC's winter assessment each November and the utilities' outage reports. Moves R1, R3, R8.

**C3. Which trade restriction is it, tariff or interdiction?** Outcomes. Dimension A conflates two things. Section 232 duties of 10 to 12.5 percent on wire, cold heads and logic are in force (EY, Jul 2026), and the node map says a tariff shock "raises cost more than it stops shipments" (N08); that holds for every tariffed input in the top eight. What stops is interdiction: an export control reaching cryocoolers or wire, a trade rupture stranding N17, or a strait closure cutting N29 and the Taiwan foundry (U-G1). The tools exist, with the medical equipment file open since 2 Sep 2025 and no proclamation by 11 Oct 2026 (Federal Register, 26 Sep 2025), but their rate, country list and use are open. Directions: duties only; duties plus a scanner tariff that moves demand (N35) not delivery; controls or interdiction. It resolves in Federal Register proclamations, Bureau of Industry and Security list changes and Lloyd's List strait transit counts. Moves R2, R4, R6 in the interdiction direction and none in the tariff direction.

**C4. Which helium world does each plant live in?** Causation. The United States plant sits in the only net-exporting producer, and Qatar's share of United States imports fell to 28 percent while Canada's rose to 47 percent (USGS, Feb 2026). The China plant's helium origin appears in no phase 2 transcript; if it is Gulf helium, U-H1 moves R2 more than R8. AI-suggested; Sourcing confirms. Directions: Ras Laffan back inside two years; impaired to 2031 with the United States plant insulated by Canadian supply and the China plant paying world price; impaired plus a Cliffside or Hugoton outage that removes the insulation. It resolves in the USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries each year and in producer force majeure notices. Moves R2, R8.

**C5. Does the OEM convert its conventional 1.5 and 3 tesla line to sealed magnets, and when?** Actions, not outcomes. The OEM's own 1.5 tesla platform was cleared on 5 Feb 2026 with a sealed option (FDA K253779), so this is a decision the OEM has partly made, not an external uncertainty. It stays open for the 3 tesla line and for hospital acceptance of a first-generation design. Directions: 1.5 tesla only by 2031; both fields; neither at scale. It moves R8 down, because less helium is needed at test (N21), and R5 up, because a sealed magnet has no helium bath to ride through a cryocooler outage (technology lens). It resolves in the FDA 510(k) database for a sealed 3 tesla clearance and in the OEM's sealed share of orders, which only the S&OP council sees. Moves R5, R8.

**C6. Does a wire mill or the cold-head maker leave MR?** Causation (is MR a priority for the parent) with outcomes. The United States cryocooler maker was bought in March 2023 by a quantum-cryogenics firm that does not list MR, and the wire mills' listed parents have backlogs filling with fusion and physics orders (Bluefors, 28 Mar 2023; Bruker 10-K FY2025). Directions: reaffirmation; quiet allocation that lengthens lead times without a stop; sale or exit. It resolves in parent annual reports and in Sourcing's supplier reviews. Moves R4, R5.

**C7. Is the cold head a single site or a single company with three sites?** Causation. The maker lists plants in Japan, the United States and the Philippines (producer page, 2026), and the Nankai Trough probability rose in Sep 2025. Directions: the 4 kelvin head the OEM uses is made at all three, and the natural-disaster threat on R5 drops; it is made only in Japan, and R5 holds. Nothing public shows it; Engineering confirms the plant of origin. Moves R5.

**C8. Does mature-node allocation reach a low-volume console customer?** Outcomes. Taiwanese 12-inch mature capacity falls 15 to 20 percent by 2028 and legacy DRAM lead times run 26 to 42 weeks (Counterpoint, 23 Jan 2026; RAND Technology, 19 Nov 2025). The screen dropped N11 because three memory makers exist; three makers all preferring a larger customer is one failure, not three. Directions: allocation eases by 2028; it reaches the console and stretches lead times past cover. It resolves in TrendForce's quarterly contract price releases and the OEM's lead-time reads. Moves R6.

The tolerance line itself moves none of the eight. Each has a replacement time of 12 months or longer, so whether hospitals punish a 30-day or a 90-day delay (U-C1, value) re-ranks nothing at the top. It changes the port, the lanes, the field force and what cover is worth. That is a watch item, not a finding.

## Critical factors

The two axes are not independent, because a competitor's helium-light platforms are already built in Shenzhen for the United States market (openFDA, product code LNH, 2025 to 2026), so sealed adoption (C5) runs through the trade wall (C3). C1 and C2 share no cause with either axis. C5 becomes an axis only if a decision is admitted as an uncertainty.

## Predetermined versus uncertain

Predetermined and carried: the restriction tools, the sealed 1.5 tesla clearance, the four-state helium market, the grid ratings, the 18-month wire qualification. Uncertain: C1 to C8, with kinds stated. Decisions wearing uncertainty's clothes: C5 (conversion) and U-L1 (the roster), which belong to the baseline.

## Contradictions with other lenses

Not applicable in round 1. The likely contradiction is with lenses that make U-H1 the first axis; by this count it moves two of eight, and the plant it moves most is the Chinese one.

## Blind spots of this lens

No new search was possible; every figure comes from the phase 2 transcripts. C1 rests on two non-medical cases. C4 assumes the China plant buys Gulf helium without a source. C7 may be wrong if the three plants build different product classes. The table is the check against contrariness for its own sake.
