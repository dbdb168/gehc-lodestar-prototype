# Geopolitical, phase 6a, round 1, summary

## Key points

The China plant and the United States magnet plant break in all four futures, because each sits where a state holds a lever over helium or over a listed part, and only the carrying node changes between futures (N17, N16; Panel judgement).

The China plant is the earliest break on the map, in the second half of 2027 in F3 through an entity listing on its logic supplier, and the first-half-2028 helium queue break in F1 because it has no allocation seniority (N17, N10, N01; Panel judgement, Sourcing confirms logic stock).

On the sealed side of the helium axis the lever passes from states to one Japanese cold-head maker, and both plants stop together for longer than 30 days by 2029 to 2031 (N05, N16, N17; openFDA product code LNH, read 11 October 2026).

The 35-day encryption break breaches the tolerance line in every future, and through this lens it lands on the China plant or its logic route in the stop futures and on the gas carriers or port terminal in F1 (N17, N23, N26; Panel judgement, moderate confidence).

Three Placeholders remove breaks: cover at N19 above 60 days removes the United States break in F1, logic stock at N17 longer than the licence queue removes the F3 break, and a cold-head maker outside the Nankai Trough zone removes the hazard version of F2 (N19, N17, N05; Placeholder).

## In one hundred words

Two risks break in every future: the China plant and the United States magnet plant. States hold the levers, helium allocation on one side of the axis and listings on the other, and the plants cannot cover each other. The China plant breaks earliest, in the second half of 2027 in F3, and is the back-of-queue helium buyer in F1. Once helium leaves the story the lever moves to one cold-head maker and both plants stop together. The 35-day encryption event breaches everywhere. Cover days, logic stock weeks and the cold-head maker's address decide how much of this is real.
