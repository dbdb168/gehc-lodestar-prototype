# Natural disasters, phase 3, round 1

## Analysis

Bottom line first. Nothing in the hazard record says weather alone stops this line for 30 days, so the uncertainties that matter in my domain are about coincidence and correlation: a storm that takes the grid, the road and the helium trailer together behind the US magnet plant (N16), a Taiwan earthquake that stops control logic (N10) for every system at once, and a Japanese earthquake under a cold-head source (N05) whose address nobody on the panel has confirmed. Seven uncertainties follow, ranked by how many of the eight screened risks each moves. Risk numbers follow the screen: R1 is N16, R2 is N17, R3 is N18, R4 is N03, R5 is N05, R6 is N10, R7 is N20, R8 is N19. Numbered citations point to the Sources list.

**U-N1. Does a hurricane or a Pee Dee flood take the US magnet plant's power, roads and helium deliveries down together for more than 30 days before 2031?** It moves R1 directly, R3 because the final assembly hub (N18) starves when the inbound magnet lane (N24) is cut, R8 because liquefaction and helium cover (N19) depend on grid power, and R7 because a Helene-class storm displaces the households the technicians (N20) live in. Four risks. Kind of not-knowing: outcomes. Directions: a quiet run like 2025, with no mainland landfall [8]; a Helene-class inland storm, with the plant shut for days and the grid rebuilt in about ten days [4], inside tolerance; or a Florence-class basin flood, where the river crested 16 feet above flood stage [1, 2] and road, grid and trailer access fail together for weeks, which breaches. It is uncertain because the Charleston hurricane return period of 3.8 years [3] says a strike is likely in five years, but the closure logs say most strikes close the port for two to three days [5, 6], and the plant's own flood class and backup power are unconfirmed. AI-suggested: Site operations confirms the plant's county, flood class and on-site generation. Resolving observables: National Hurricane Center advisories in season, SC Ports Authority advisories, the Duke Energy outage map, and the plant's own closure log. Confidence moderate.

**U-N2. Does an earthquake or typhoon on Taiwan stop the one foundry long enough to halt control electronics for every system?** It moves R6 directly and R1, R2 and R3 because no magnet at any site ships without a spectrometer board. Four risks. Kind: outcomes. Directions: the April 2024 pattern, a magnitude 7.4 shock near Hualien [7] followed by a foundry restart inside days (Panel judgement; the restart time is not in my sources); a shock nearer the foundries with weeks of lost wafer starts, which a nine-month-plus pipeline absorbs; or a shock plus a typhoon season of 26 storms [9] that lands on the same quarter as a political closure of the strait. It is uncertain rather than predetermined because the hazard is classified but the event timing is not, and because mature-node capacity is moving partly out of Taiwan by 2028, which changes exposure without any weather. Resolving observables: USGS event feeds, the Central Weather Administration typhoon log, and foundry disclosures of lost wafer starts. Confidence moderate on hazard, low on recovery time.

**U-N3. Does a Nankai Trough earthquake happen inside the horizon, and does the cold-head source sit in its zone?** It moves R5 directly and R1 and R2 because both magnet plants need cold heads to ship; it moves R4 only if the OEM's qualified wire mill is the Japanese one, which is unconfirmed. Three firm risks, one conditional. Kind: outcomes for the event, actions for the location, since the OEM can find out. Directions: no event by 2031, which is the likelier reading of a 30-year probability of 60 to 90 percent [10, 11]; an event that closes the cold-head plant and its port for a quarter, past 30 days on any single-source part; or a lesser Pacific-coast typhoon or flood that does the same for a week. It is uncertain because a 30-year probability says nothing about five years and because the panel has no address. AI-suggested: Engineering confirms where cold heads and wire are made. Resolving observables: the Headquarters for Earthquake Research Promotion annual evaluation, Japan Meteorological Agency advisories, and the cold-head maker's own disclosures. Confidence high on classification, low on exposure.

**U-N4. Does a winter or summer grid emergency in the Carolinas or the Midcontinent cut liquefaction at the magnet plant and assembly at the hub in the same week?** It moves R1, R3 and R8. Three risks. Kind: outcomes, with a causation element: whether liquefaction can ride through a rolling outage. Directions: no emergency, with the elevated and high ratings from 2027 and 2028 [12] never called; a day of rolling outages, which a helium plant on standby survives; or a multi-day event like the ten-day Helene rebuild [4], which runs the helium cover down. It is uncertain because the reliability body rates risk, not events, and the plant's dependence is unconfirmed. Resolving observables: the annual long-term and seasonal reliability assessments, utility energy emergency alerts, and the plant's generator record. Confidence moderate.

**U-N5. Does weather at a United States helium source or on the trailer route push helium cover below the time to reroute, while Qatar is still out?** It moves R8 and R1. Two risks. Kind: outcomes, with a value element: whether a helium delay counts as a line stop. Directions: a normal winter; a 2021-style plains winter storm that takes a plant down for months [13]; or a Gulf hurricane that shuts a coastal source and the container fleet's depots at once, with no Qatari slack to pull from [14, 15]. Resolving observables: NOAA winter storm and hurricane warnings over Kansas, Texas and Wyoming, and the annual mineral commodity summary.

**U-N6. Does the rewritten rainfall atlas move either US site into a higher flood class?** Atlas 15 arrives no earlier than September 2027 [16], and Milwaukee County's 24-hour record was broken in 2025 [17]. It moves R1 and R3. Kind: outcomes. Directions: no change; a higher class that raises premiums and continuity assumptions; or a class that makes the current site uninsurable at current terms. Resolving observables: the atlas publication page and reinsurer catastrophe reports each January.

**U-N7. Does a Bohai surge or subsidence flood reach the China plant's floor?** It moves R2 only. Kind: causation, because the October 2024 surge mechanism is unexplained [18, 19]. Directions: never, because the plant stands above reclamation land; once, for days; or repeatedly as subsidence continues. Resolving observable: Chinese tide-gauge reports and port closure notices, which I could not reach.

## Critical factors

The factor my domain hinges on is correlation, not magnitude. A hazard that closes one node is inside tolerance on every log I have; a hazard that closes a plant, its grid and its helium together, or a single foundry that every plant shares, is the only route to 30 days.

## Predetermined versus uncertain

Predetermined from phase 2: closure durations at N26, the ten-day grid rebuild, the rising classifications over N05 and N17, the helium baseline. Uncertain: U-N1 to U-N7 above. Passed to the baseline as internal facts: the addresses of N03 and N05 and the plant's backup power (actions).

## Contradictions with other lenses

None yet; round 1 is isolated. Phase 2 resolved the port against me and I accept it: N26 stays off the top eight.

## Blind spots of this lens

I still cannot see N03 or N05 on a map, so U-N3 is a classification with no exposure behind it. I underweight heat limiting rigging crews (N32) and drought on hydropower feeding N17. I read the US record far better than the Chinese one.
