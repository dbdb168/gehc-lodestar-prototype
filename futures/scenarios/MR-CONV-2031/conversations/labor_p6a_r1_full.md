# Labor, phase 6a, round 1

## Analysis

Bottom line: no future breaks first on a labour node. Two futures turn a labour risk into a second break. People decide how long a stop lasts, not whether it starts.

**F1, cost only, helium still decides.** First break is helium cover (N19), winter 2028 to 2029, a 30 to 45 day stop at the United States magnet plant (N16). Technicians (N20) do not lose their trade in 45 days, so labour reads this as cost. The labour event is the port. The east and gulf coast longshore master contract runs to 30 September 2030 (phase 2 Labor record; Logistics confirms). The 2024 stoppage lasted three days, so a 2030 repeat holds Atlantic exports (N26) for days, not 30. Moderate confidence. Placeholder that removes the first break: cover at N19 above 60 days.

**F2, cost only, helium no longer decides.** First break is the cold head (N05), 2029 to 2031, both plants stopped past 30 days. The labour break is the second and is this future's own. The floor changes trade mid-run, from helium handling to vacuum and cryocooler work, and the trades swapped are the ones that take more than a year to train (N20). Installs run two to three quarters late in 2029, no line stop. Low confidence: time to competence is a causation uncertainty with no record. Placeholder that removes it: Human resources confirms the vacuum trade already exists at N16 at a fifth of winding headcount.

**F3, a part stops, helium still decides.** First break is the China plant (N17) through programmable logic (N10), second half of 2027. Labour lengthens it: a plant stopped two quarters in a tight labour market loses its technicians, so the restart is paced by rehiring, not the licence. Moderate on the mechanism, low on the months. The United States plant cannot cover Asia; ramp and test (N21) and the winders (N20) both take over 18 months to double. Helium cover (N19) breaches by early 2028.

**F4, a part stops, helium no longer decides.** First break is the cold head (N05) by 2029, both plants stopped for most of 18 months. Here labour turns into a break. An 18-month stop exceeds the retention horizon of any skilled trade; technicians at N16 and N17 leave or retire, and the 18-month replacement on N20 runs after the cold head returns, not alongside it. A second cold-head source is qualified by people who have never built the design, so the 18 months is a Placeholder for Sourcing until the second maker's trade depth is read. Panel judgement, moderate confidence on the mechanism, low on the dates.

## Critical factors

The age profile at N20, which Human resources holds and no axis moves. Trade depth at the sole suppliers (N03, N05), which decides whether requalification is 18 months or 30. The port contract calendar at N26.

## Predetermined versus uncertain

Predetermined: the port contract expiry in 2030; the 18-month replacement on N20; the one-town workforces at N03 and N05. Uncertain: the retirement curve at N20 (outcomes, Placeholder); time to competence for the vacuum trade (causation); whether a stopped China plant rehires (outcomes).

## Contradictions with other lenses

None yet in round 1. This lens holds the technicians as F2's second break, consistent with the nine to two cold-head vote.

## Shared breaks and the encryption event

Shared by all four: the 35-day encryption event (C4) and the ageing of the three one-town trades (N20, N03, N05), which no axis moves. Belonging to one: the trade swap at N20 (F2) and the post-stop workforce loss at both plants (F4).

Winding is instrumented and test is a digital record, so a 35-day loss of systems at N16 or N17 is a 35-day stop: a tolerance breach in every future. At the cold-head maker (N05) it is 35 days in F1 and F3 and indistinguishable from the first break in F2 and F4. At the gas carriers (N23) and port terminal (N26) it walks through helium cover in F1 and F3 and costs days in F2 and F4. Moderate confidence. The Placeholder that removes it is a tested 30-day manual fallback (Site operations), which this lens doubts exists for test.

## Blind spots of this lens

This lens overrates the three-day port strike, misses winding automation, and underweights how fast N16 retrains in F2.
