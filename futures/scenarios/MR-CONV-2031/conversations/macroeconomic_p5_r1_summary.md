# Macroeconomic, phase 5, round 1, summary

## Key points

The annual USGS helium chapter is the only public price read on helium; its 2025 reading of about 12 dollars per cubic metre with surcharges still posted leans toward U2 end A, helium still decides. N01, N19, N34. USGS, February 2026.

The BLS producer price index for industrial gas manufacturing is a live monthly series at 295.224 for August 2026 and rising, but it blends helium with argon and oxygen, so it leans toward end A only weakly. N01, N19, N34. BLS, read 11 October 2026.

The Federal Register's JSON feed of Section 232 presidential documents is live; of 71 documents none since July 2025 and January 2026 names copper, chips or medical devices, and no quota or licence exists on any mapped part, which leans toward U1 end A. N04, N08, N10, N12. Federal Register, read 11 October 2026.

Memory contract prices are still rising, 10 to 15 percent in the fourth quarter of 2026, which is a cost and not a stop; the stop reading would be industrial customers receiving no quotation for two quarters. N11, N12. TrendForce, 30 September 2026.

A duty on finished imported MR scanners would look like a trade stop and is not one for a line assembled in the United States. N18, N31. Panel judgement.

## In one hundred words

This lens names four indicators. Two are live today: the BLS industrial gas producer price index, monthly, and the Federal Register feed of Section 232 presidential actions, daily. Two need a reader: the annual USGS helium chapter and the quarterly memory contract price bulletin. On the 11 October 2026 readings all four lean toward end A on both uncertainties: helium still priced with surcharges, trade measures still duties rather than quotas, memory allocation still a cost. The blind spot is that no public series isolates helium, so a physical shortage can sit inside a flat index, and none of the four watches a sole supplier's owner.
