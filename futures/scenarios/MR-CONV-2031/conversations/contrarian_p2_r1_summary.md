Helium is not the Gulf story the packet assumes, because Qatar's share of United States helium imports fell from 40 to 28 percent and Canada's rose from 36 to 47 percent between the 2020 to 2023 and 2021 to 2024 windows, while the base price fell from $14 to $12 per cubic metre, and the duller predetermined facts are that the federal reserve went private on 27 Jun 2024 and the European Union banned Russian helium from 26 Sep 2024 (N01, N19, N23, N34; USGS, Jan 2025 and Feb 2026; BLM, 27 Jun 2024).

The ordinary dated failures nobody has mentioned are the longshore contract expiring 30 Sep 2030, the hurricane season every June to November, and memory makers all allocating at once with conventional DRAM contract prices forecast up 10 to 15 percent in the fourth quarter of 2026 and tightness through 2027 (N11, N12, N16, N18, N26; USMX, 11 Mar 2025; National Hurricane Center; TrendForce, 30 Sep 2026).

## In one hundred words

Two of the exercise's open questions are settled and two of the screen's settled facts are open. Low-helium magnets are already on sale, so the helium-free axis is really a question about the OEM's own conversion decision. Trade restriction tools survived the courts and already name copper, semiconductors and medical equipment, so only the rates are uncertain. The technician cliff rests on no public data. Helium supply to the United States moved away from Qatar within two years and prices fell. The boring, dated risks are the 2030 port contract, the annual hurricane season and memory allocation through 2027.
