# Technology shift, phase 3, round 2

## Analysis

The bottom line is that this lens accepts the moderator's axes, C1 (restriction becomes a stop) and C2 (helium stays a line risk), rewording C2 so the external half of C3 sits inside it. The OEM's own conversion is an action for phase 6b; a sealed 3 tesla clearance, hospital uptake and the test-floor draw are outcomes. Reworded C2: "In 2031, does a magnet still need a helium delivery to be built, tested and shipped, or has the sealed design made helium a service matter only?" (N01, N05, N16, N17, N19, N34; Panel judgement).

Two corrections to round 1. The contrarian lens is right that market adoption of sealed magnets runs through the trade wall, because the helium-light platforms cleared for the United States are built in Shenzhen; the OEM's own sealed option (FDA K253779, 5 February 2026) is built where the OEM builds, AI-suggested until Sourcing confirms, so only the competitive pace crosses the wall (N17, N29, N36). Second, dual-use controls on cryocoolers and wire were not "missed" in phase 2; the foreign trade and geopolitical lenses carried them under C1 (N03, N05, N17).

Independence, four combinations. Stop and helium still a risk: the China plant loses cold heads or wire behind a control list while every magnet still needs a fill (N17, N03, N05, N19). Stop and helium no longer a risk: the fill stops mattering, but the sole cold head becomes the part without which nothing ships and sits behind the same list (N05, N19). No stop and helium still a risk: the Gulf route or a United States allocation rule starves both plants under open trade (N01, N16, N17, N19). No stop and helium no longer a risk: only the single sites, the logic foundry and the design record remain (N16, N18, N10, N22). All four hold.
