# Contrarian, phase 5, round 1, summary

## Key points

The Federal Register listing indicator (C1) is live but confirms a stop only on the day it starts, and its silence is being read as end A when the Section 232 report is simply unpublished (N03, N05, N10, N17; Panel judgement). Hormuz transits (C2) count ships, not helium, so a recovering count can settle U2 toward end B while Qatar's liquefaction still waits on repair; four transits on 4 October 2026 against 85 (straits.live, read 11 October 2026). The USGS Grade-A base price (C3) fell from 14 to 12 dollars per cubic metre while Hormuz closed, leans b against the panel's a, and lags a full year (USGS, February 2026; N01, N19). The low-field share of new LNH clearances (C4) settles no axis; if it passes one third for two years the grid was the wrong grid, and openFDA gives the count live but not the field strength (openFDA, read 11 October 2026; N35, N36).

## In one hundred words

Four indicators, four failure modes. The Federal Register API is live and useless as a warning, because a listing is published when it bites, and silence is not a settle. Hormuz transits are live, a week late, and count ships rather than helium carriers or Qatar's plant. The USGS base price is the only public helium price, annual, and it leans the other way from the panel. The low-field share of new United States MR clearances tests whether the axes matter at all; the count is live, the field strength needs a person each quarter. Three live today, two manual.
