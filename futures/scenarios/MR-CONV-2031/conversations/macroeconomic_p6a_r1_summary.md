# Macroeconomic, phase 6a, round 1, summary

## Key points

Both plants break in all four futures, through helium cover on the helium side and through the cold head on the sealed side, and in no future is the cause macroeconomic; money sets the bill and the date, not the break (N16, N17, N19, N05; Panel judgement).

A 4 percent policy rate and producer prices rising about 5 percent a year thin the cover held at the United States plant each budget round, which is why F1 breaks in the winter of 2028 to 2029 rather than later (N19, N16; Federal Reserve, 16 September 2026).

F4 is the one future where price is the mechanism, since the cold-head parent allocates capacity to better-paying buyers; the price at which the OEM loses its allocation is a Placeholder for Sourcing and a price the OEM would pay turns the break into a cost (N05; Placeholder).

The 35-day encryption event breaches the line at either plant in every future, breaches at the wire mill or cold-head maker only where inventory is under 35 days, and lands worst in F1 at the gas carriers and port terminal through thin cover (N16, N17, N03, N05, N23, N26; Panel judgement).

R3, R4 and R7 cost in every future and break in none, and demand shape scales every bill and no break (N18, N03, N20, N35, N36; Panel judgement).

## In one hundred words

Through the macroeconomic lens the two plants break in every future, but the cause is always a container, a cold head or a listing, never a price. Rates and input inflation decide how much cover and stock the OEM holds and so bring F1 and F3 forward. F4 is the exception: the cold-head parent sells capacity to better-paying buyers, and the price at which the OEM loses its place is an unset Placeholder that decides whether that future breaks or costs. The 35-day encryption event breaches at either plant in all four futures; at a sole supplier it depends on inventory nobody has stated.
