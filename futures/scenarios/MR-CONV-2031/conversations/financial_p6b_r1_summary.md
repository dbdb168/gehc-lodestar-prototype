# Financial, phase 6b, round 1, summary

## Key points

Confirming the Placeholders (A10) works in all four futures, costs a phone call, and is the only action that makes every other action's bill real, so it ranks first (N19, N16, N05, N03, N17; Placeholder).

A multi-year agreement with the cold-head parent (A2) works in all four futures only if it carries a change-of-control clause, because an owner's exit lands on the same node as an export listing and the 2027 sourcing cycle is the last one before F4's exit (N05; AI-suggested, Sourcing confirms the parent's revenue share).

A second cold-head maker (A1) works in F2 and F4 and must start by the second quarter of 2027 or its eighteen-month requalification misses F4's 2029 break (N05; Panel judgement).

Ramp capacity for Asia (A8) carries the heaviest working-capital drag on the list and does nothing in F1, where helium rather than capacity binds the United States plant (N21, N16, N17; Panel judgement).

Two actions are missing: contingent business interruption cover naming the sole suppliers, and an annual credit watch on the wire mill and cold-head parent so that an exit is read in filings before the press (N03, N05, N19; AI-suggested, Finance and Sourcing confirm).

## In one hundred words

The cheapest cover on this map is paper and stock, not steel. One owner, the cold-head parent, can end the line with a sale document in every future, so binding that owner by contract outranks building capacity. The ranking is A10, A2, A1: confirm the Placeholders now, sign the parent with a change-of-control clause in the 2027 cycle, and start a second maker by mid-2027. Helium cover and backup power are bets on the futures where helium still decides. Ramp capacity for Asia is the most capital for the fewest futures. Insurance and a credit watch are missing.
