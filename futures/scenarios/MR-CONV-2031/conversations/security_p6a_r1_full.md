# Security, phase 6a, round 1

## Analysis

Bottom line: through the security lens the four futures share one break, a 35-day loss of systems (C4), and it lands on a different node in each. In F1 it lands on the logistics layer around helium cover (N23, N26, N19). In F2 and F4 it lands on the cold-head maker (N05), where it is indistinguishable from the first break the page already names. In F3 it lands on the China plant (N17) and its rails (N31), where a state already using grey-zone tools has both motive and precedent. The design and qualification record (N22) is assumed intact on every page; if that assumption fails, every replacement time on the map stretches and no future stays inside tolerance. Panel judgement throughout; no new research this round.

**F1, cost only, helium still decides.** The first break is helium cover at the United States magnet plant (N19), winter 2028 to 2029, 30 to 45 days, one quarter of Western Hemisphere installs late. Through this lens the same break has a second door: an encryption event at one of the two specialist gas carriers (N23) or the port terminal system (N26) delays a container past the hold window and cover runs out without any storm. The break lasts as long as the carrier's recovery plus the next container cycle, which on the 35-day record outlasts any cover the panel has seen quoted. R1 (N16) breaks through N19; R2 (N17) breaches on the allocation queue in 2028, a cost of lateness rather than a stop. R3, R4, R5, R6 are costs only. R7 (N20) is a Placeholder. Confidence moderate on the path, low on the date, because the cover days are a Placeholder. A cover figure above 60 days removes the United States break.

**F2, cost only, helium no longer decides.** The first break is the cold head (N05), 2029 to 2031, both plants (N16, N17) stopped for longer than 30 days because 18 months of requalification outlasts inventory. Through this lens the sole maker is also the sector's richest ransomware target on the map: one site, one product everyone needs, a parent for whom MR is a minority. An encryption event there in 2029 or later is the first break arriving early, not a separate one. R4 (N03) is a cost; a one-mill wire supply is a counterfeit and substitution exposure, not a stop, because no second wire exists to counterfeit against. R1, R3, R6, R8 are costs only. Confidence moderate, on the six-to-five split carried from phase 3. The Placeholder that removes the break is a tested 30-day hold without a working cold head.

**F3, a part stops, helium still decides.** The first break is the China plant (N17) through its logic supply (N10), second half of 2027, within a quarter of an entity listing, a stop longer than 30 days with Asian installs late for two or more quarters. Through this lens the earlier door is an investigation under the amended cyber law with a payment freeze on the rails (N31), which stops the plant before any listing is published, and a data-localisation order that fences off the qualification record (N22) for the Asian build. That is the one future where N22 is at risk of partition rather than loss. R8 (N19) breaches second by early 2028 on allocated helium. R5 (N05) breaks a quarter later if the listing names cold heads first. R3, R4 are costs; R7 is a Placeholder. Confidence moderate, high that the tools exist, low on the date because the licence approval rate is unpublished. A China plant logic stock longer than the licence queue removes the break.

**F4, a part stops, helium no longer decides.** The first break is the cold head (N05) by 2029, both plants stopped for most of 18 months, the installed base breaching service cover through N34. Through this lens the parent's exit from MR is also the moment a grey-market cold head appears: a part with one qualified maker, high value and a stopped line is the textbook counterfeit case, and a substandard cold head on a sealed magnet is a dark scanner in a hospital. That is a cost on top of the stop, not a second break. R4 (N03) breaks instead only if a producer-country notice names wire first. R1, R2 break through R5. R3, R6, R8 are costs. Confidence moderate; the counterfeit route is panel judgement with no incident record for this part class.

## Critical factors

The shared encryption break (C4). It lands wherever the first break of each future already sits, because the same property, a single qualified site, makes a node both a supply risk and a target. Duration is taken at 35 days, the longest public recovery record. Cost: in F1 a 30 to 45 day stop at N16 if it lands on N23 or N26 during a hold window, a cost only if it lands at the plant with cover above 35 days; in F2 and F4 a stop of 35 days plus the time to restart cold-head output, indistinguishable from the first break and worse if it coincides; in F3 a stop longer than 30 days at N17, with the freeze on N31 the more likely form. At the wire mill (N03) in every future a 35-day stop is a cost, because mills hold inventory and the OEM holds wire, until the stop runs past the wire buffer, a Placeholder for Sourcing. The break is removed in every future by one reading of one Placeholder: a tested 30-day manual fallback at the plants and sole suppliers.

## Predetermined versus uncertain

Predetermined: one wire mill, one cold-head maker, one record. Uncertain: outcomes, whether the event occurs; causation, whether a fallback works; value, whether a counterfeit part is a quality or a delivery problem.

## Contradictions with other lenses

None yet; round 1.

## Blind spots of this lens

This lens overrates the attacker's interest in a niche part and underrates the ordinary failure the pages already carry: a storm, a queue, a parent's order book.
