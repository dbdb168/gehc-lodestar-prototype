# Security, phase 6a, round 1

## Analysis

Bottom line: the four futures share one break, a 35-day loss of systems (C4), and it lands on a different node in each: the helium logistics layer (N23, N26, N19) in F1, the cold-head maker (N05) in F2 and F4, the China plant (N17) and its rails (N31) in F3. Every page assumes the design and qualification record (N22) is intact; if that fails, every replacement time stretches and no future stays inside tolerance. Panel judgement throughout; no new research.

**F1, cost only, helium still decides.** First break: helium cover (N19), winter 2028 to 2029, a 30 to 45 day stop at N16, a quarter of Western installs late. This lens adds a second door: an encryption event at a gas carrier (N23) or the port terminal system (N26) delays a container past the hold window and cover runs out with no storm. R2 (N17) breaches on the allocation queue in 2028 as lateness, not a stop. R3 to R6 are costs; R7 (N20) is a Placeholder. Confidence moderate on the path, low on the date. Cover above 60 days removes the break.

**F2, cost only, helium no longer decides.** First break: the cold head (N05), 2029 to 2031, both plants (N16, N17) stopped for longer than 30 days because requalification outlasts inventory. The sole maker is also the richest ransomware target on the map. An encryption event there is the first break arriving early, not a separate one. R4 (N03) is a substitution exposure, not a stop. R3, R6, R8 are costs. Confidence moderate, on the six-to-five split from phase 3. A tested 30-day hold without a cold head removes the break.

**F3, a part stops, helium still decides.** First break: the China plant (N17) through logic (N10), second half of 2027, a stop longer than 30 days and Asian installs late for two or more quarters. The earlier door is an investigation under the amended cyber law with a payment freeze on the rails (N31), which stops the plant before any listing, and a data-localisation order that partitions the qualification record (N22) for the Asian build. This is the one future where N22 is at risk. R8 (N19) breaches second, early 2028. R5 (N05) breaks a quarter later only if a listing names cold heads first. R3, R4 are costs; R7 is a Placeholder. Confidence moderate: high that the tools exist, low on the date. Logic stock at N17 longer than the licence queue removes the break.

**F4, a part stops, helium no longer decides.** First break: the cold head (N05) by 2029, both plants stopped for most of 18 months, the installed base breaching through N34. The parent's exit from MR is also when a grey-market cold head appears: one maker, high value and a stopped line is the textbook counterfeit case, and a substandard cold head on a sealed magnet is a dark scanner. That is a cost on the stop, not a second break. R4 (N03) breaks instead only if a notice names wire first. R1, R2 break through R5; R3, R6, R8 are costs. Confidence moderate; the counterfeit route has no incident record for this part class.

## Critical factors

The shared encryption break (C4) lands wherever each future's first break already sits, because a single qualified site is both a supply risk and a target. Duration is 35 days, the longest public recovery record. Cost: in F1, a 30 to 45 day stop at N16 if it lands on N23 or N26 inside a hold window, a cost only if it lands at the plant with cover above 35 days; in F2 and F4, 35 days plus the cold-head restart, worse if it coincides with the first break; in F3, a stop longer than 30 days at N17, most likely as a freeze on N31. At the wire mill (N03) it is a cost in every future until it outruns the wire buffer, a Placeholder for Sourcing. One Placeholder reading removes it everywhere: a tested 30-day manual fallback at plants and sole suppliers.

## Predetermined versus uncertain

Predetermined: one wire mill, one cold-head maker, one record. Uncertain: outcomes, whether the event occurs; causation, whether a fallback works; value, whether a counterfeit part is a quality or a delivery problem.
