# Security, phase 6a, round 1, summary

## Key points

The shared encryption break (C4) is one event with four landing sites: the helium logistics layer in F1, the cold-head maker in F2 and F4, the China plant and its payment rails in F3, each a stop longer than 30 days at 35 days of recovery (N19, N23, N26, N05, N17, N31; Panel judgement).

In F1 an encryption event at a gas carrier or the port terminal system is a second door to the helium cover break, and cover above 60 days or a tested 30-day manual fallback removes it (N16, N19, N23, N26; Placeholder).

In F2 and F4 the cold-head maker is both the first break and the richest target on the map, so an encryption event there is the first break arriving early, with F4 adding a grey-market cold head as a cost on top (N05, N16, N17, N34; Panel judgement).

In F3 a cyber-law investigation with a payment freeze stops the China plant before any listing is published, and a data-localisation order makes it the only future where the qualification record is partitioned (N17, N31, N22, N10; AI-suggested, Quality and regulatory confirms).

The wire mill carries a 35-day stop as a cost in every future until it outruns the wire buffer, which Sourcing has not stated (N03; Placeholder).

## In one hundred words

One break is shared by all four futures: a 35-day loss of systems, landing wherever each future's first break already sits, because a single qualified site is both supply risk and target. F1 reaches it through the gas carriers and the port, F2 and F4 through the cold-head maker, F3 through the China plant's rails under a cyber-law investigation. Breaks belonging to one future: helium cover in F1, the logic listing in F3, the counterfeit cold head in F4. Every page assumes the design record is intact. One Placeholder removes the shared break everywhere: a tested 30-day manual fallback.
