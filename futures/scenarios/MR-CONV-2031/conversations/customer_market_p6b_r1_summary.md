# Customer and market, phase 6b, round 1, summary

## Key points

Confirming the Placeholders (A10) and testing the manual fallback (A9) work in all four futures and cost almost nothing, so they rank first and second. N16, N17, N19, N05. Panel judgement.

A second cold-head maker (A1) ranks third because its eighteen months cannot be recovered once the sealed share passes half, and the installed base already runs on this part. N05, N34, N17, N18. Panel judgement.

Two actions are missing: rewriting delivery clauses and publishing delivery performance before health systems do, and an allocation rule for scarce magnets that protects share where the OEM is one of two or three suppliers. N36, N35, N25 to N29. Panel judgement.

A8, ramp capacity to cover Asia, is the only action that keeps an Asian delivery date when the China plant stops. N16, N21, N29. Panel judgement.

## In one hundred words

The customer sees a date kept or missed, not a node. On that test A10 and A9 hold in every future and cost a phone call and a planning week, so they come first. A1 comes third: eighteen months of requalification means a 2027 start is the last that lands before the sealed share passes half, and the cold head already sits in every installed magnet the hospital's service contract depends on. Two actions are missing: delivery clauses and published performance that manage what a late install costs, and a rule for who gets the magnet when the line is short.
