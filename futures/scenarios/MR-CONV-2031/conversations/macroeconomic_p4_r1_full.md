# Macroeconomic, phase 4, round 1

## Analysis

Bottom line: this lens prices the bill in all four futures and opens a break in only two, both through a supplier's parent deciding that MR is a small customer. Every future assumes the design and qualification record (N22) is intact; the encryption break (C4) is carried in phase 6a. Demand shape (C5) changes each bill, never the break.

Shared starting point (P17): policy rate 3.75 to 4.00 percent, 4.1 percent projected through 2027 (Federal Reserve, 16 Sep 2026); producer prices up 5.4 percent a year (BLS, 10 Sep 2026); hospital margins 2.5 percent (Kaufman Hall, 27 Aug 2026); memory prices tripled under allocation to 2028 (TrendForce, 30 Sep 2026); Europe's 2016 to 2021 MR cohort passes ten years old by 2031 (COCIR, 2023). Replacement need is fixed; order timing is not (N35, N36).

### F1 Cost only, helium still decides

What happened. 2026 to 2028: the Qatari units stay impaired and producers keep allocating. The United States plant buys cover at a world price while Canadian and domestic sources fill its volume (USGS, Feb 2026). The China plant, which bought Gulf helium, sits behind an allocation queue (P3). Hospitals defer rather than cancel, so the backlog lengthens. 2028 to 2031: memory allocation eases and Western helium is priced rather than rationed.

What is true for my nodes. The cost of a fill and of cover (N19, N34) roughly doubles against 2025. Panel judgement, moderate confidence. Memory (N11) and copper (N04, 50 percent content duty, Federal Register 5 Aug 2025) add cost, not stops.

What fails first. R2, the China plant (N17), through helium allocation at the Asian end, in the first half of 2027. AI-suggested; the S&OP council confirms the plant's helium origin. United States cover (N19) holds, though every grid event walks through it (NERC, 29 Jan 2026).

Cost. Asian installs run more than 30 days late for two to three quarters; no Western line stop. Most confident: United States helium is a price problem, not a volume problem. Least confident: how long the Asian queue lasts (outcomes).

### F2 Cost only, helium no longer decides

What happened. 2026 to 2028: Hormuz reopens, allocation letters stop, sealed 1.5 tesla magnets (FDA K253779, 5 Feb 2026) take most new builds and helium is a buyers' market. 2028 to 2031: a 3 tesla sealed clearance arrives; the cold-head and wire parents fill their order books with fusion, physics and quantum work (P18) while hospital margin pressure cuts MR volumes in 2027.

What is true for my nodes. Helium (N01, N34) is a cost line again; memory (N11) normalises by 2028; orders bunch late in the horizon (N35).

What fails first. R5, the cold heads (N05), through the sole maker's allocation to quantum buyers after a 2027 MR order cut lowers the OEM in its queue; lead time passes 30 days around 2029. Panel judgement, low confidence. Demand opens a break only through a supplier.

Cost. One quarter of late installs while a second source is qualified; no line stop. Most confident: helium stops being the line's margin. Least confident: whether the parent keeps MR (causation).

### F3 A part stops, helium still decides

What happened. 2026 to 2028: the truce lapses and a listing of cryogenic parts strands the China plant's cold heads and wire while the Gulf is still impaired, so the Asian plant loses parts and cover together (N17, N03, N05, N19). 2028 to 2031: tariffs on finished scanners (P8) arrive; a weaker dollar makes the United States plant cheaper to build in but imports dearer.

What is true for my nodes. Helium cost as in F1; Section 232 inputs carry 10 to 12.5 percent (P7); tariff pass-through compresses hospital margins (N35).

What fails first. R2, the China plant (N17), through the cold-head listing (N05), by mid-2028. AI-suggested; Sourcing confirms the China plant's cold-head source. Its helium queue only shortens the window.

Cost. Eastern Hemisphere line stop longer than 30 days; Western installs late at the margin. Most confident: the bill is the highest of the four. Least confident: whether United States cover (N19) breaks on the same winter (outcomes).

### F4 A part stops, helium no longer decides

What happened. 2026 to 2028: sealed magnets convert behind a trade wall; helium falls to a cost. 2028 to 2031: a listing or parent exit takes the only cold-head source, which now carries the thermal load of every magnet shipped and in the field (N05, N34).

What is true for my nodes. Helium cheap; copper and chips tariffed; European replacement need comes due (COCIR, 2023), so the stop lands on rising orders.

What fails first. R5, the cold heads (N05), through a listing or sale, by 2029. AI-suggested; Engineering confirms the maker's location and ownership.

Cost. Western line stop longer than 30 days, plus installed-base service breaches. Most confident: this stop costs most, because no magnet has a helium fallback. Least confident: the date (outcomes).

## Critical factors

The cold-head parent's view of MR (N05) and the China plant's helium origin (N17) decide which risk breaks first; rates and the dollar decide the bill.

## Predetermined versus uncertain

Predetermined: rate path to 2027, tariffs in force, Qatari impairment, memory allocation to 2028, fleet age. Uncertain: Asian allocation politics (outcomes); the parents' priorities (causation); litres at test, a Placeholder for Engineering.

## Contradictions with other lenses

Round 1 is isolated. Carried: the Financial lens ranks supplier exit a stop with no bridge; this lens ranks it a 2029 break in two futures only.

## Blind spots of this lens

I assume price clears helium; a physical shortage no price fixes would pull the F1 and F3 breaks earlier. I underweight allocation politics at the Asian plant.
