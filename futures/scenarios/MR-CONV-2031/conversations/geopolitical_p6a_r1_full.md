# Geopolitical, phase 6a, round 1

## Analysis

Bottom line: through this lens two risks break in every future, the China plant (R2, N17) and the United States magnet plant (R1, N16), because each sits where a state holds a lever over what it needs. The China plant is inside a potential counterparty's borders and buys helium with no allocation seniority. The United States plant depends on a helium market that producer states and the United States government allocate. The carrying node changes with the future; the plants do not. Panel judgement throughout unless a source or Placeholder is named.

**F1, cost only, helium still decides.** The lever is Qatar's closed route and the United States allocation letters (straits.live, 11 October 2026; USGS, February 2026). R8 helium cover (N19) breaks first in the winter of 2028 to 2029, or any hurricane season from 2027, and takes R1 (N16) with it: a 30 to 45 day stop and a quarter of Western installs late. R2 (N17) breaks on schedule in the first half of 2028 as the back-of-queue helium buyer. The rest cost. Confidence moderate on the China queue, low on the United States break, which rests on a cover Placeholder and an untested grid path. Cover at N19 above 60 days removes it.

**F2, cost only, helium no longer decides.** The lever moves to one Japanese cold-head maker (N05). R5 breaks between 2029 and 2031 with no schedule, and both plants (R1, R2) stop for longer than 30 days because 18 months of requalification outlasts any stock. R7 (N20) is the runner-up, late installs in 2029, low confidence. Confidence moderate: the sole source is public (openFDA, read 11 October 2026); the date is not. A maker's address outside the Nankai Trough zone removes the hazard version.

**F3, a part stops, helium still decides.** An entity listing names the China plant's logic supplier within a quarter of the truce lapsing, first read 10 November 2026. R6 (N10) and R2 (N17) break together in the second half of 2027, a stop longer than 30 days with no second plant sized for Asia (N21). R8 (N19) and R1 (N16) breach second, by early 2028, on allocated helium. R5 costs, because cold-head stock is deeper. Confidence moderate: the listing tools were used on the sector in 2025; the date rests on the truce notice and an unpublished licence approval rate. Logic stock at N17 longer than the licence queue turns the break into a cost.

**F4, a part stops, helium no longer decides.** The lever is a parent company, not a state. R5 (N05) breaks by 2029 and runs into 2030; R1 and R2 stop with it for most of 18 months, the highest bill. R6 costs, absorbed by stock raised after the notice. R4 (N03) costs unless a producer-country notice names wire before cryocoolers, which moves the first break to the mill in early 2029. Confidence moderate on the node, low on the date. The Placeholder is the price at which the OEM loses its cold-head allocation.

**Shared and single breaks.** R1 and R2 break in all four. R5 belongs to the sealed side (F2, F4), R6 to F3 alone, R8 to the helium side (F1, F3). R3, R4 and R7 break in none; R7 is a Placeholder for Human resources that no state moves.

**The shared encryption break (C4).** A 35-day loss of systems breaches the line everywhere, because 35 exceeds 30 before recovery begins. It lands where a state-adjacent actor already has reason to be: in F3 and F4 at the China plant (N17) or its logic route, under the amended Chinese cyber law, for 35 days plus the licence queue that follows an investigation. In F1 it lands at the gas carriers (N23) or the port terminal (N26) and walks through the helium cover, so the stop is 35 days plus the refill of N19. In F2 and F4 a hit on the cold-head maker is indistinguishable from the first break. Confidence moderate in the stop futures, low in the cost futures, where Security leads.

## Critical factors

Who holds the helium allocation order; whether a listing is addressed to the China plant or its suppliers; whether a producer-country notice can reach the cold-head maker; the Taiwan Strait lane (N29) under grey-zone pressure.

## Predetermined versus uncertain

Predetermined: the Gulf repair runs past 2028; one cryocooler maker has left; the United States plant cannot cover Asia. Uncertain: the truce notice and licence approval rate (outcomes), cover days and logic stock (Placeholders), whether a 35-day outage is an attack or an accident (causation).

## Contradictions with other lenses

Financial places the China plant queue first in F1; this lens agrees it breaks and disputes only the order, settled by the cover figure at N19. This lens keeps the parent exit, not a listing, as the F4 mechanism.

## Blind spots of this lens

It assumes closures happen; most threatened ones do not. It underrates how fast a second cold-head source or a non-United States logic path removes a dependency, and reads the F4 parent as a state when it is a balance sheet.
