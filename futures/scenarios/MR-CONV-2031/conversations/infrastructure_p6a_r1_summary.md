# Infrastructure, phase 6a, round 1, summary

## Key points

Across the four futures the eight risks break in only three physical places: helium cover at the United States magnet plant when helium still decides, the sole cold-head maker when it does not, and the China plant when a state stops a part (N19, N05, N17; Panel judgement).

In F1 and F3 the helium cover breaks when a grid curtailment or flood takes the liquefier's power and the oversize road while a container is inside its 35 to 48 day hold, a 30 to 45 day stop that a cover figure above 60 days removes (N16, N19, N24; Placeholder).

In F2 and F4 the cold head breaks both plants at once, and this lens adds that a Nankai Trough event closes the maker's ports as well as its plant, so even undamaged parts cannot move (N05, N30, N16, N17; AI-suggested, Sourcing confirms the address).

The United States plant cannot cover Asia in any future because ramp and test capacity takes 24 months to change, so F3's idle lane to Japan and India stays idle (N21, N29; AI-suggested, Site operations confirms).

The shared 35-day encryption break is worst at the gas carriers in F1 and F3, where it runs the cover out and stops the line for 50 to 60 days; at the port terminal it delays exports in every future without a line stop (N23, N26, N19; Panel judgement, moderate confidence).

## In one hundred words

Through the infrastructure lens the four futures reduce to three break points. Where helium still decides (F1, F3), the margin is the cover at the United States magnet plant, and a grid curtailment or flood during a container's hold window stops the line for 30 to 45 days from winter 2028. Where it does not (F2, F4), one Japanese cold-head plant and its ports hold every magnet, and a stop runs toward 18 months. The China plant breaks only when a state stops a part. The encryption break lands hardest on the gas carriers, because the cover runs out before systems return.
