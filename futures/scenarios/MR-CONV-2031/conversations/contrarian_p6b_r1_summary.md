# Contrarian, phase 6b, round 1, summary

## Key points

The panel will over-recommend A1, a second cold-head maker, because it assumes a second qualified maker exists when one maker has already left the market, and because its sealed-only wording hides that conventional magnets need cold heads too (N05, R5; Panel judgement).

All four futures share one hidden assumption, that the design and qualification record is intact and the plants can run on paper for a month, so A9 is robust only inside it and my missing action M1, an escrow and restore test of the record, is the cheapest check on the map (N22, N16, N17; Panel judgement).

A11 is not an action but the uncertainty the OEM decides itself; waiting a year chooses F1 and F3 by default (N19, N05; Panel judgement).

A4, backup power for the liquefier, is the cheap ordinary action the panel will under-rate; it covers the first break in F1 for a few million (N16, N19, R1, R8; AI-suggested, Site operations confirms).

My top three are A1 reworded to both designs, A9, and A10, all working in four futures, with A1 first because its 18-month clock makes a year's wait the most expensive (N05, N22, N19; Panel judgement).

## In one hundred words

The panel's favourite, a second cold-head maker, is also its weakest: nobody has shown a second maker exists, and the one maker who already left says the market is shrinking. Reworded to cover conventional magnets too, it works in all four futures, so it still ranks first, but only once Sourcing names the maker. Every future quietly assumes the design record survives; test that with an escrow and restore before anything else. The conversion decision is the OEM's own, not an uncertainty. Backup power for the liquefier is cheap and covers the first break; do not let it sink under the bigger items.
