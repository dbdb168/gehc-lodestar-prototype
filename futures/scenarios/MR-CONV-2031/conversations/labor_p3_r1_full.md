# Labor, phase 3, round 1

## Analysis

Bottom line first. The labour question that can go two or more ways by 2031 and move the most of the eight screened risks is not a strike and not a visa rule. It is whether the magnet plant's trades are still the same trades in 2031, and whether anyone outside the plant is training them. Seven uncertainties follow, ranked by how many of the eight risks each moves; R1 to R8 are the screen's order and the table at the end names them. This lens moves none of R6, the control logic. Every fact comes from the phase 2 transcripts with their source and date.

**L1. By 2031, does the magnet plant need helium hands or cryocooler hands?** Moves R1, R5, R7 and R8, four of eight. Sealed 1.5 tesla magnets holding 0.7 to 7 litres of helium are cleared at three makers including the OEM and still use niobium-titanium wire and a Gifford-McMahon cold head (FDA K181479, 3 Aug 2018; K253779, 5 Feb 2026). If output shifts to sealed magnets, the liquefaction and transfer trades behind N19 shrink and test helium per magnet falls, so the same cover lasts longer (R8 eases; litres per sealed test are AI-suggested, Engineering confirms). Cryocooler trades grow and the cold head becomes the only thing keeping a magnet cold (R5 tightens; N33 swaps cold heads instead of topping up helium). R7 moves because one 18-month trade replaces another, and R1 because a plant mid-conversion runs two processes on one roster. Kind: actions, the OEM's conversion pace, with a causation element on what a sealed line needs in people. Directions: the plant stays mostly conventional; most 1.5 tesla output converts by 2029; or 1.5 tesla converts while 3 tesla does not and the plant carries both skill sets at half scale. Uncertain because the clearance is done but the production mix and hospital adoption pace are not yet visible. Observable: the sealed share of new orders, held by the S&OP council, and new sealed clearances in the FDA 510(k) database at accessdata.fda.gov, updated weekly. Confidence moderate: the technology facts are sourced, the staffing consequence is Panel judgement.

**L2. Will the two United States plants have the hands they need, and if the local market fails, do machines fill the gap?** Moves R1, R3 and R7, three of eight. The ageing workforce, the Wisconsin gap of 294,000 by 2032 and the South Carolina competition with a 4,000-worker vehicle plant are predetermined (phase 2, P12; DWD and WEDC, Nov 2025; Post and Courier, 3 Sep 2023). The rate at which this roster leaves and what replaces it is not. Kind: outcomes, with a causation element on whether automation removes the need. Directions: the market supplies replacements at a higher wage and the risk shows as cost; the market fails for maintenance, electrical and assembly trades and the plants automate faster than people leave, which Wisconsin employers are already reported doing (WPR, 4 May 2026); or the market fails and automation does not reach winding and cryogenic work, so output falls with no supplier failing. Uncertain because the nearest trades run younger than the all-worker median, welders at 39.3 and electromechanical assemblers at 42.4 against machinists at 45.7 (BLS table 11b, 2025), so a cliff is a property of one roster, and because a recession or a cancelled vehicle plant would loosen the market within a quarter. Observable: employment and projected openings from the two state workforce agencies, and BLS table 11b each January. Confidence high on the states, low on the plant.

**L3. If the United States magnet plant stopped, could the China plant wind its magnets with its own people?** Moves R1, R2 and R7, three of eight. The node map gives N16 and N17 36 months to replace and says the design record N22 is what makes every replacement slow. The labour form of that is tacit knowledge, the winding and ramp steps one roster knows and the other does not. Kind: causation, how much of the trade lives in N22 rather than in heads, with a value element, whether a cross-trained second roster counts as cover. Directions: both plants wind to one written process and either can cover the other at the cost of shipping lanes; the processes have diverged and cover means an 18-month retrain, past the 30-day line; or trade rules (phase 2, P9) stop the China plant serving the Western Hemisphere whatever its people can do. Uncertain because no public source describes either plant's process, and China's working-age trend remains unsourced. Observable: internal only, the share of winding steps with written instructions and the count of staff qualified at both plants, held by Engineering and Human resources.

**L4. Do the sole wire mill and the sole cold-head maker keep enough skilled people as their home countries shrink and fusion and quantum work bids for the same hands?** Moves R4 and R5, two of eight. Japan's working-age share is 59.6 percent of a population falling 550,000 a year (Statistics Bureau of Japan, 1 Oct 2024); Germany loses a further 3.5 million working-age people by 2050 (KfW, 1 Jul 2025); the mills' parents fill order books with fusion and physics work and the United States cryocooler maker belongs to a quantum-cryogenics buyer (phase 2, P18). Kind: outcomes, sharing a causation element with U-S1: does MR lose its people before it loses the parent's interest. Directions: the plants hold their workforce through automation and skilled visas; skilled staff move to fusion wire and MR keeps capacity on paper with longer lead times; or one plant in one town loses its core team and an 18-month requalification starts without any stoppage being announced. Uncertain because headcount, age and town of each plant are AI-suggested, for Sourcing to confirm on a supplier visit, and because Japan and Germany both widen and tighten skilled visas inside the horizon. Observable: the parents' annual filings, the two statistics offices, and quoted lead times for wire and cold heads, which Sourcing sees in every quote.

**L5. Does the United States immigration regime close the door on magnet and cryogenic engineers?** Moves R1, one of eight. The 100,000 dollar fee on new H-1B (specialty occupation visa) petitions runs to 21 Sep 2027 and is vacated by court order, so it is not collected (Mintz, 23 Sep 2026; Fragomen, Sep 2026). Kind: actions, by government and courts. Directions: the fee is reinstated on appeal and hiring from abroad for N16 and N22 stops at the margin; the fee dies and the route stays slow but open; or the proclamation lapses in 2027. Uncertain because it turns on a pending appeal. It reaches engineers, not the technicians at N20. Observable: the Federal Register and the appeals court docket.

**L6 and L7. Does the 2030 longshore negotiation produce a stoppage longer than three days, and does the field force keep pace with installs?** Each moves none of the eight but sits on the tolerance line. The master contract at N26 runs to 30 Sep 2030 and the last stoppage lasted three days (ILA, 7 Feb 2025; USMX, 11 Mar 2025); medical equipment repairers at N33 grow 13 percent to 2035 with about 8,200 mostly replacement openings a year (BLS handbook, 27 Aug 2026). Both are outcomes. Directions for the port: a short stoppage as in 2024, a longer one if automation is reopened, or none. For the field force: a cost problem, or installs slipping past 30 days while magnets ship on time. Observable: ILA and USMX announcements from early 2030, and the BLS handbook yearly. Nodes N25, N26, N33.

## Critical factors

L1 decides which trade R7 is about by 2031. L2 and L3 decide whether R1 fails through people. L4 is the quiet one, a trade lost in a town nobody watches.

## Predetermined versus uncertain

Predetermined, from phase 2: the demographic wave, the state labour gaps, the port contract date, the union density floor at both plant states, the visa direction and the supplier countries' shrinking working-age populations. Uncertain: L1 to L7, with L3 partly internal, like U-L1.

## Contradictions with other lenses

Not applicable in round 1. This lens expects to differ from the technology lens on L1 if it reads conversion as fast, and from the contrarian on L2 if it reads national trade ages as settling the plant's; the sealed order share and the roster age would settle both.

## Blind spots of this lens

I see people and miss automation, so L2's middle direction may be the likeliest and I have given it the least evidence. I give the 2030 port negotiation a line it may not deserve. I have no source for the China plant's labour market, the mills' towns, or the staffing of a sealed-magnet line; every staffing consequence in L1 is Panel judgement.
