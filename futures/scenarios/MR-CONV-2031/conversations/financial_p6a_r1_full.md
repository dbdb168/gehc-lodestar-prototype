# Financial, phase 6a, round 1

## Analysis

Bottom line: two breaks are shared by all four futures, the sole cold-head maker (R5, N05) and the 35-day encryption event (C4). The other six break only where trade or helium puts them in the path. The cold head is shared for a balance-sheet reason: MR is a minority of its parent's revenue (phase 2, P18, AI-suggested, Sourcing confirms), so the OEM is a small customer of a supplier it cannot replace in 18 months.

F1, cost only, helium still decides. Helium cover (R8, N19) breaks first in the winter of 2028 to 2029, a 30 to 45 day stop at the US magnet plant (R1, N16). Cover is inventory carried at a 4 percent rate against prices rising 5 percent a year (Federal Reserve, 16 September 2026; phase 2, P17), so it thins unless funded. The China plant (R2, N17) breaches on its allocation queue in the first half of 2028. Cover above 60 days at N19 removes the US break. Confidence low to moderate; the carry cost is sourced, the curtailment path is not.

F2, cost only, helium no longer decides. Cold heads (R5, N05) break between 2029 and 2031 at the sole maker, stopping both plants (N16, N17) for longer than 30 days, with 18 months of requalification behind the stop. A second route needs no hazard: a strategic buyer acquires the parent and stops serving medical. A second maker qualified before the sealed share passes half removes the break. Confidence moderate on the node, low on the date.

F3, a part stops, helium still decides. The China plant (R2, N17) breaks in the second half of 2027 through its logic supply (R6, N10), for two quarters or more. Helium cover (R8, N19) breaches by early 2028. Per quarter this is the costliest future: share moves through tender criteria (N36), payment rails (N31) become a licence queue, and business interruption cover excludes government action, so the stop is uninsurable (Panel judgement). Logic stock at N17 outlasting the licence queue removes the break. Confidence moderate; the licence approval rate is unpublished.

F4, a part stops, helium no longer decides. Cold heads (R5, N05) break by 2029 when the parent allocates capacity to better-paying buyers and a dual-use listing closes the licence route; both plants stop for most of 18 months and the installed base loses service cover through N34. This is the highest bill and the most predetermined: a parent with a fuller order book needs no listing to deprioritise a minority customer. A multi-year agreement from the 2027 sourcing cycle removes the break; the price at which allocation is lost is a Placeholder. Confidence moderate.

The encryption break (C4). A 35-day loss of systems is a line stop in all four futures. At the wire mill (N03) requalification outlasts inventory; at the US plant (N16) it breaches alone; at the cold-head maker (N05) it coincides with the first break in F2 and F4. In F1 the gas carriers (N23) and port terminal (N26) also walk through helium cover, adding a quarter of late Western installs. Cyber cover carries sublimits and a state-action exclusion, so in F3 and F4 it is partly uninsurable (Panel judgement). Confidence moderate on 35 days, low on the site.

## Critical factors

The bill turns on the cold-head parent's MR share and ownership (Sourcing confirms), the carry cost of helium cover against a stop treated as total loss, the China plant's logic stock in weeks, and insurability (none for government action, partial for cyber).

## Predetermined versus uncertain

Predetermined: the sole cold-head source, its parent's minority MR share, the 18-month requalification and the carry cost of inventory. Uncertain: whether the parent allocates away or sells (outcomes); whether a grid rating becomes a curtailment (causation); whether tenders punish lateness (value); whether a second maker can be qualified by 2029 (actions).

## Contradictions with other lenses

Six lenses say the cold head breaks at U2 end B, five say it costs. This lens: it breaks in F2 and F4 and costs in F1 and F3, because helium is the fallback that turns an outage into a bill. A confirmed MR share above a third of the parent's revenue would weaken this case.

## Blind spots of this lens

This lens reads ownership and misses engineering: it may overrate how fast money buys a second maker and underweight the F1 storm path, which no balance sheet shows.
