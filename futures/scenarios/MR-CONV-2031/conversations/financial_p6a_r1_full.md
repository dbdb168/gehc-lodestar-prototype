# Financial, phase 6a, round 1

## Analysis

Bottom line: through this lens two of the eight risks break in every future and the other six break only where trade or helium puts them in the path. The two shared breaks are the sole cold-head maker (R5, N05) and the 35-day encryption event (C4), which is a line stop wherever it lands. The cold head is shared for a balance-sheet reason, not an engineering one: MR is a minority of its parent's revenue (phase 2, P18, AI-suggested, Sourcing confirms), so the OEM is a small customer of a supplier it cannot replace inside 18 months. That is true in all four futures; what changes is whether the stop arrives by hazard, by parent allocation, or by licence.

F1, cost only, helium still decides. Helium cover (R8, N19) breaks first in the winter of 2028 to 2029, with a 30 to 45 day stop at the US magnet plant (R1, N16). Financially this is a working-capital failure: cover is inventory carried at a 4 percent policy rate with producer prices rising about 5 percent a year (Federal Reserve, 16 September 2026; phase 2, P17), so cover thins each budget cycle unless someone funds it. The China plant (R2, N17) breaches on its allocation queue in the first half of 2028 because it has no allocation seniority and pays spot. The Placeholder that removes the break is cover at N19 above 60 days; a cover figure below the container hold keeps it. Confidence low to moderate: the carry cost is sourced, the curtailment path is not.

F2, cost only, helium no longer decides. Cold heads (R5, N05) break between 2029 and 2031 by a hazard or outage at the sole maker, stopping both plants (N16, N17) for longer than 30 days with 18 months of requalification behind the stop. The financial reading adds a second route to the same node with no hazard needed: a parent for whom MR is minority revenue can be bought by a strategic buyer in fusion or quantum work who does not keep serving medical. The Placeholder that removes the break is a second qualified maker before the sealed share passes half, or a Sourcing-confirmed MR share high enough that the parent cannot walk. Confidence moderate on the node, low on the date.

F3, a part stops, helium still decides. The China plant (R2, N17) breaks in the second half of 2027 through its logic supply (R6, N10), within a quarter of an entity listing, and stays down for two quarters or more. Helium cover (R8, N19) breaches second by early 2028. The financial cost is the one this lens rates highest per quarter, because this is the only future in which share moves through tender criteria (N36) and because the bloc split turns payment rails (N31) into a licence queue that adds days to every invoice. Contingent business interruption cover excludes government action, so none of this stop is insurable. The Placeholder that removes the break is logic stock at N17 longer than the licence queue, in weeks, set by Sourcing. Confidence moderate: the listing tools exist; the licence approval rate is unpublished.

F4, a part stops, helium no longer decides. Cold heads (R5, N05) break by 2029 when the parent allocates capacity to better-paying buyers and a dual-use listing closes the licence route; both plants stop for most of 18 months and the installed base breaches service cover through N34. This is the highest bill of the four and the one this lens finds most predetermined, because a parent with a fuller order book from physics and fusion does not need a listing to deprioritise a minority customer. The Placeholder that removes the break is a multi-year supply agreement from the 2027 sourcing cycle at a price the OEM will pay; the price at which the allocation is lost is itself a Placeholder for Sourcing. Confidence moderate.

The encryption break (C4). A 35-day loss of systems lands most expensively on the sole cold-head maker, because that is the one site where the loss coincides with a first break in F2 and F4 and where the OEM has no second buyer position. At the wire mill (N03) it is a line stop in all four futures, since 18 months of requalification outlasts any wire inventory a mill holds for one customer; at the US magnet plant (N16) it is a 35-day stop that breaches on its own. In F1 the gas carriers (N23) and the port terminal (N26) also walk through helium cover, so the cost is a stop plus a quarter of late Western installs. In F3 the Security lens expects it more often. Cyber cover is still written in 2026 but with sublimits and a growing exclusion for state-attributed action, so in F3 and F4 it is partly uninsurable (Panel judgement). Confidence moderate on the 35 days (the longest public record); low on which site.

## Critical factors

Four things decide the bill: the cold-head parent's revenue share from MR and its ownership (Sourcing, Engineering confirm); the carry cost of helium cover against the price of a 30-day stop, which the scope packet treats as a total loss for the stop's duration; the China plant's logic stock in weeks; and whether any of the stops is insurable, which through this lens is no for government action and partial for cyber.

## Predetermined versus uncertain

Predetermined: the sole cold-head source, its parent's minority MR share, the 18-month requalification, and the carry cost of inventory at current rates (phase 2, P17 and P18). Uncertain, by kind: whether the parent allocates away or sells (outcomes); whether a grid rating becomes a curtailment (causation); whether tenders punish lateness (value); whether a second maker can be qualified at all before 2029 (actions).

## Contradictions with other lenses

Six lenses say the cold head breaks at U2 end B, five say it costs. This lens says it breaks in F2 and F4 and costs in F1 and F3, because helium is the fallback that turns a cold-head outage into a bill. A Sourcing-confirmed MR share above a third of the parent's revenue would weaken this lens's case.

## Blind spots of this lens

This lens reads ownership and misses engineering: it may overrate how fast money buys a second maker, and it underweights the F1 storm path because no balance sheet shows it.
