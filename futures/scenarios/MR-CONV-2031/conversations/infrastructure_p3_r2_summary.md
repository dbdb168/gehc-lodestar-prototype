# Infrastructure, phase 3, round 2, summary

## Key points

This lens accepts the moderator's axes, C1 (restriction becomes a stop) and C2 reworded as "in 2031, does a magnet still wait for helium that has to be shipped cold", because every one of the four corner combinations can physically occur and the pair moves seven of the eight risks (N01, N05, N16, N17, N19, N21, N27; Panel judgement).

C3 is rejected as an axis because the Contrarian lens showed sealed adoption runs through the trade wall, and C7, this lens's own grid candidate, is a regional cause rather than a state of the world (N16, N18, N19; Panel judgement).

C4 goes to phase 6a as a shared break, because the physical restart of liquefaction and a stopped ramp is the same in every future; C5 is a cost modifier on all four; C7 is a modifier on the two futures where helium is still shipped cold, since a winter curtailment shortens cover through the on-site liquefier (N16, N18, N19, N21, N36; AI-suggested that the liquefier is grid-dependent, Site operations confirms).

Round 1 is corrected on two points: the Gulf route reaches the United States plant's cover through price and allocation rather than containers, given Qatar's 28 percent and Canada's 47 percent shares of United States imports, and the storm case at the magnet plant is a basin flood cutting grid, road and trailer access together (N01, N16, N19, N23, N24; USGS, Feb 2026; Natural disasters lens).

## In one hundred words

The axes are trade stop or trade cost, and helium still shipped cold or not. They are independent: the Gulf can stay shut under open trade, and sealed magnets can finish converting behind a wall. Together they move seven risks; technicians stay a Placeholder. An encryption event is a break every future carries, not an axis. Demand shape changes the bill, never the break. A grid curtailment matters only where helium is still liquefied on site, so it modifies two futures. This lens corrects itself: the Gulf route hits the United States cover through price and seniority, and the storm case is a basin flood.
