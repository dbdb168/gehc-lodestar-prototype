# Financial, phase 4, round 1

## Analysis

Bottom line: the futures sort by insurability and bridge. F1 and F3 are helium queues; F2 breaks nothing; F4 hangs the line on one supplier whose parent has better customers. Every future assumes the design and qualification record (N22) is intact; C4 is carried in phase 6a. Demand shape (C5) changes the bill only.

### F1 Cost only, helium still decides

What happened. From 2026 to 2028 tariff tiers stay (P7), the Gulf repair slips past 2028 (U-H1 settles toward A), and the policy rate holds near 4.1 percent with producer prices up 5.4 percent a year (P17). From 2028 to 2031 helium is allocated. The United States plant (N16) buys from a private reserve and Canada (P4); the China plant (N17) buys Asian spot (P3). Cover (N19) is inventory on a 4 percent balance sheet, so each week of cover costs more every year. Panel judgement.

What breaks first. R2, the China plant (N17), through an allocation letter that cuts a helium delivery while the container fleet (N23, P5) is fully booked, crossing the 30-day line in 2028 to 2029. AI-suggested; the S&OP council confirms the plant split.

What it costs. A line stop at N17 of 30 to 60 days about once a year; Western installs stay inside the line at a higher helium bill. Panel judgement.

Most confident: cover thins exactly when needed, because rate and price both rise. Moderate, on P17. Least confident: the Gulf timing (U-H1, outcomes).

### F2 Cost only, helium no longer decides

What happened. By 2028 the Gulf reopens to insured traffic and a 3 tesla sealed magnet is cleared. From 2028 to 2031 sealed builds take most orders, helium is a buyers' market, and the capital in cover (N19) is released. The cold head (N05) carries every magnet's thermal load while its sole maker's parent fills its book with physics work (P18).

What breaks first. Nothing through my nodes; this future rules out a stop. Nearest the line is R5, cold heads (N05), as a cost in lead time and price. Panel judgement.

What it costs. Margin only. Installs stay inside the line; the bill is tariffs (P7), allocated memory (P16) and a dearer cold head.

Most confident: releasing cover working capital is the largest financial change in any future. Moderate. Least confident: whether the parent keeps MR a priority (U-S1), the one event that turns F2 into F4.

### F3 A part stops, helium still decides

What happened. From 2026 to 2028 the truce lapses at the 2027 sourcing cycle, entity listings cut the China plant's parts flow, and a producer country lists cold heads or wire (U1 settles toward B). The Gulf stays impaired. From 2028 to 2031 the China plant (N17) lacks cold heads (N05) and logic (N10) while both plants queue for helium (N19).

What breaks first. R2, the China plant (N17), through a listing of cold heads or logic, in 2027 to 2028, ahead of any helium break. AI-suggested; Sourcing confirms the parts flow.

What it costs. An Eastern Hemisphere line stop past 30 days with no second plant to cover (N29), plus Western installs late through thin cover. The loss is the OEM's: a stop by trade rule or owner exit sits outside contingent business interruption cover. Panel judgement, low confidence, no policy wording carried.

Most confident: a stop by rule or owner exit brings no insurance recovery. Moderate. Least confident: whether listing or owner exit comes first (outcomes).

### F4 A part stops, helium no longer decides

What happened. From 2026 to 2028 sealed conversion runs behind a rising trade wall. From 2028 to 2031 helium no longer gates shipment, cover (N19) is drawn down, and the stop lands on the cold head (N05) when its sole maker's parent sells the line, allocates it to physics customers, or sees it listed (P18).

What breaks first. R5, cold heads (N05), in 2029 to 2030, at both plants at once: an 18-month requalification with no second source and no helium margin to hide behind. AI-suggested; Engineering confirms.

What it costs. The largest loss of the four: a line stop in both hemispheres for 18 months less cold-head stock held. A year of that stock is small working capital against the line's value, a Placeholder. Panel judgement.

Most confident: a sole supplier for whom MR is a minority of parent revenue is one board decision from exit. High, on P18. Least confident: whether 18 months is the true replacement time; my blind spot says longer.

## Critical factors

Owner intentions at the wire mill and cold-head maker (N03, N05). Helium allocation seniority at the China plant (N17, N19). Rate and producer prices behind every week of cover (N19). Insurance exclusions for trade and owner events (N05, N16, N17).

## Predetermined versus uncertain

Predetermined in all four: tariff tiers (P7), the private United States reserve (P4), the cryocooler acquisition and parent order books (P18), the rate path (P17). Uncertain: Gulf timing (outcomes), owner exit (outcomes, with a causation element), the sealed test draw (causation), whether a late install is punished (value).
