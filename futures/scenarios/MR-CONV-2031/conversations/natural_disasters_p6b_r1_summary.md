# Natural disasters, phase 6b, round 1, summary

## Key points

Confirming the Placeholders (A10) and running the 30-day manual fallback drill (A9) work in all four futures, cost almost nothing, and rank first and second; a year's delay leaves every other action sized on guesses and every plant with an untested plan (N16, N17, N18, N19, N05; Placeholder and Panel judgement).

A second cold-head maker (A1) ranks third because its eighteen-month qualification cannot absorb a lost year before the sealed share passes half in 2029, and it only counts if the second maker sits outside the Nankai Trough strong-shaking zone that may hold the sole maker and the compressor maker together (N05, N06; Panel judgement).

Helium cover above the hold plus the grid rebuild (A3) and backup power for the liquefier (A4) work in F1 and F3 and are a cheap hedge elsewhere; the act-by date is the 2027 hurricane season and the elevated Carolinas grid rating, not the 2028 winter (N16, N19; AI-suggested, Site operations confirms).

This lens adds two actions: a correlated-hazard test that closes the magnet plant, its port and its helium lanes in one storm, and a second Atlantic port with a pre-season helium position (N16, N26, N23, N24; Panel judgement).

## In one hundred words

The cheapest actions are the ones that work everywhere: confirm the Placeholders and drill the 30-day manual fallback. A second cold-head maker comes next because an eighteen-month qualification cannot lose a year before 2029, and only if the second maker is outside the Nankai zone. Helium cover and backup power at the United States plant bind on the 2027 hurricane season and grid rating. The candidate list tests nodes one at a time; weather does not. One hurricane closes the plant, the port and the helium lanes together, so the panel needs a correlated-hazard test and a second Atlantic port.
