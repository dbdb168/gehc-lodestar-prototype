# Financial, phase 4, round 3

## Analysis

Bottom line: I vote N17 for F1, N05 for F2, N17 for F3 and N05 for F4, and the one thing the four pages lack is the cold-head parent's revenue mix and debt profile at N05.

**F1.** I keep my own draft: the China plant (N17) breaks first, in 2028, because it buys helium with no domestic producer behind it and sits at the back of an allocated queue today. Infrastructure and Natural disasters put the break at helium cover (N19), but both need a hazard and a cover Placeholder shorter than the rebuild. Mine needs only the queue, which exists now.

**F2.** I withdraw "nothing breaks" and adopt Customer and market: the cold head (N05) breaks when a sealed fleet's spares stock, sized to the order book rather than the fleet, meets an unscheduled outage. I reject the parent-allocation route in F2; an owner leaving the product is U1 end B by the phase 3 note, so it belongs in F4. Labor's technicians (N20) rest on an unconfirmed roster age.

**F3.** I adopt Geopolitical: the China plant (N17) through programmable logic (N10), the thinnest stock on the floor, within a quarter of a listing in 2027. Security's payment freeze (N31) needs a second state to act first.

**F4.** I adopt Macroeconomic: the cold head (N05), not wire (N03). A parent for whom this product is a minority of revenue (P18) reallocates output by a board decision with no notice, which is how sole sources vanish. The wire mill's other customers buy the same wire, so no mill drops it alone. The Contrarian says the cold-head break is partial if the sealed share is a minority in 2029; it is not, because the node map says a conventional magnet cannot ship without the cold head either.

## Critical factors

The four pages differ on both axes: F1 is a helium queue under open trade, F2 a sealed fleet on one cold head, F3 a state stranding the China plant while helium still decides, F4 an owner or a state stopping the cold head with no helium fallback. F2 is inconsistent only where it borrows a parent exit from F4. Dates run in order: F3 stops in 2027, F1 in 2028, F4 in 2029, F2 from 2029 to 2031; F4 lands after F1 and the drafts say why (sealed conversion and the listing clock).

## Predetermined versus uncertain

Predetermined: one cryocooler maker has already left (P18), cover costs more each year (P17), and the China plant has no domestic helium. Uncertain, as outcomes: the parent's allocation decision, Gulf repair timing and the truce notice. Uncertain, as value: whether a late install is punished (N36).

## Contradictions with other lenses

Missing across all four pages: the cold-head parent's share of revenue from this product, its debt maturities and its exit horizon (N05). Every page dates the parent's move by sealed share, but a board acts on its own filings, not the OEM's conversion pace. AI-suggested; Sourcing confirms. Contradiction with Natural disasters and Infrastructure on N19 against N17 stands; the cover Placeholder settles it.

## Blind spots of this lens

I price ownership and miss the engineering case for wire; if a mill's qualification is tighter than a cold head's, the Contrarian is right. I underweight how long a cold plant takes to restart.
