## Critical factors

The helium producer map (N01), the first instrument on any register naming the China plant or its suppliers (N17, N10), and the cold-head parent's capacity allocation (N05).

## Predetermined versus uncertain

Predetermined: geography fixes which plant each state can strand, and the Hormuz repair runs years (IOM3, 23 March 2026). Uncertain, outcomes: which part a notice names first, and whether any notice comes. Uncertain, causation: whether a destination-addressed control strands one plant or both.

## Contradictions with other lenses

The one thing missing across all four pages: export controls name a destination, not a part alone. A Japanese or United States control on cryocoolers or wire aimed at China strands N17 and leaves N16 supplied; only an owner exit or a producer-state ban on all buyers stops both plants. Macroeconomic, the Contrarian and Technology shift write both plants stopping on one listing. The first notice's destination clause settles it. Magnet-grade wire's dual-use status is still AI-suggested; Quality and regulatory confirms.

## Blind spots of this lens

This lens reads every instrument as used and underrates how often a listed part keeps flowing under licence. It also underweights the owner-exit route it has adopted, because a board is not a state.
