# Natural disasters, phase 6a, round 1

## Analysis

Bottom line: weather breaks the line only where helium still decides (F1, F3); geology breaks it only where the cold head carries every magnet (F2, F4). One Carolinas hurricane takes the plant's power (N16), the oversize road (N24) and the export port (N26) together. Panel judgement.

**F1.** The first break is helium cover (R8, N19), reached through the US magnet plant (R1, N16). A 2018-class flood or a winter curtailment takes the liquefier's feeder and the trailer road (N24) while the next container is in its 35 to 48 day hold. Earliest: any hurricane season from 2027; scheduled: winter 2028 to 2029, the table's date. The stop runs 30 to 45 days, a breach, with a quarter of Western installs late. The China plant's queue (R2, N17) breaches in the first half of 2028 without weather. Confidence low to moderate: rating to curtailment has no record (causation). The break goes if cover at N19 exceeds 60 days or N16 has backup power off the plant feeder.

**F2.** The first break is the cold head (R5, N05) at the one Japanese maker, by earthquake, typhoon or outage. Both plants (R1, R2) stop for longer than 30 days because 18 months of requalification outlasts any stock. The window opens in 2029 with no schedule (outcomes). Weather at N16 or N18 only costs here; no helium margin stands behind the site. Confidence moderate on the node, low on the date. A maker's address outside the Nankai Trough zone removes the hazard path, not the outage path.

**F3.** The first break is the China plant (R2, N17) through logic (R6, N10) in the second half of 2027, which no hazard moves. Helium cover (R8, N19) breaches second by early 2028; the F1 storm brings it forward to 2027. The Tianjin plant and its lane (N29) share one typhoon footprint, but the listing lands first. Confidence moderate; the China date rests on an unpublished licence rate.

**F4.** The first break is the cold head (R5, N05) by allocation in 2029. A Nankai event at the maker in 2028 pulls it forward a year at the same cost. Both plants stop for most of 18 months and the installed base loses service cover (N34). Confidence moderate.

**Shared and particular.** Shared by all four: the C4 encryption event, and the exposure of N16 and N18 to one Atlantic storm track. Shared by F1 and F3: helium cover at N19 breaks on a weather or grid event. Shared by F2 and F4: the cold head at N05 breaks. Belonging to one: the logic stop at N17 (F3) and the parent's exit from MR (F4). Technicians (R7, N20), hub (R3, N18) and wire (R4, N03) break in no future here.

**The encryption break, C4.** A 35-day loss of systems breaches in every future. In F1 it lands worst at the logistics layer: a gas carrier (N23) or the port terminal (N26) locked for 35 days walks through the helium cover (N19) and stops N16 for 35 to 50 days. In F2 it lands at the cold-head maker and stops both plants. In F3 it lands at the China plant (N17) or its payment rails. In F4 it is indistinguishable from the allocation stop. Only a tested 30-day manual fallback (Placeholder) removes it. Confidence high that it breaches; low on where it lands.

## Critical factors

The hazard that matters is a feeder outage longer than the helium cover, not the headline hurricane. Cover days at N19, backup power at N16 and the maker's address decide whether three risks break or cost.

## Predetermined versus uncertain

Predetermined: the grid ratings, the Atlantic storm track over N16, N24 and N26, the 18-month cold-head requalification. Uncertain: whether a rating becomes a curtailment (causation), when a Nankai event occurs (outcomes), the cover days (Placeholder).

## Contradictions with other lenses

This lens holds that a cold-head loss breaks the line in F1 and F3 too, since 18 months exceeds any stock, against the five lenses that call it a cost there. Engineering stating how long a magnet ships without a new cold head settles it.

## Blind spots of this lens

This lens overweights storms it can map and underweights drought on hydropower feeding the wire mill, heat limiting rigging at N32, and tier-two suppliers it cannot locate.
