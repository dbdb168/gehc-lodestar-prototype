# Geopolitical, phase 2, round 1

## Analysis

The bottom line is that four things are already fixed for the magnet line to 2031: helium supply is politically concentrated and two of the four producer states have already acted on it; niobium comes from one country whatever anyone does; the China plant sits inside a procurement regime that rewards being made in China; and the Gulf sea lane is, at the date of writing, a war zone rather than a route. What is not fixed is when the Gulf reopens, whether the US and China keep their truce, and whether the Taiwan Strait moves from grey-zone pressure to interdiction.

**Helium is a four-state market and two of the four states have already acted (N01, N19, N23, N34).** The United States Geological Survey (USGS) puts 2025 world helium production at 190 million cubic metres, of which the United States produced 81, Qatar 63, Russia 18 and Algeria 11 (USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries, 5 Feb 2026, https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-helium.pdf). The same source records that US and European Union import bans on Russian helium continued through 2025, and that the United States is a net exporter whose imports came from Canada (47 percent), Qatar (28 percent) and Algeria (10 percent) over 2021 to 2024. Russia then added helium to its export licensing list by Decree No. 404 of 14 Apr 2026, with exports outside the Eurasian Economic Union needing prime-ministerial approval until 31 Dec 2027 (Global Trade Alert, https://globaltradealert.org/intervention/154600; Interfax, 14 Apr 2026, https://interfax.com/newsroom/top-stories/117116/). This holds unless the EU lifts its ban, which has no expiry, or Russia revokes the decree early. For this product, the US magnet plant (N16, N19) sits in the one producer that exports more helium than it imports, while the China plant (N17) has no domestic source of scale (China produced 3 million cubic metres in 2025) and buys from Qatar and Russia. AI-suggested for the OEM's sourcing split; Sourcing confirms.

**Qatar's helium is physically interrupted and part of it is damaged for years (N01, N23, N27, N34).** Iranian strikes on Ras Laffan from 28 Feb 2026 halted liquefied natural gas production and helium with it; QatarEnergy declared force majeure on 4 Mar 2026 (Exiger, 16 Mar 2026, https://exiger.com/perspectives/iran-war-disrupts-one-third-of-global-helium-supply/). A materials-institute summary reports QatarEnergy saying repairs will take up to five years and cut annual helium exports by 14 percent (IOM3, 23 Mar 2026, https://www.iom3.org/resource/middle-eastern-war-disrupting-helium-supply.html). I could not reach QatarEnergy's own statement, so those figures are secondary reports of a primary claim. Predetermined: roughly a tenth of 2025 world capacity is out until about 2031 regardless of the war's end. Not predetermined: when the rest restarts. This fails only if the damage estimate was wrong.

**The Gulf lane is not a route at the date of writing (N27, N23).** Lloyd's List Intelligence counted 117 non-Iranian Hormuz transits in the week of 14 to 20 Sep 2026, mostly tankers and short-haul feeders, with major container lines still avoiding the strait and hostile action rated highly likely (30 Sep 2026, https://www.lloydslistintelligence.com/resources/blog/strait-of-hormuz-brief-30-september-2026). An AI-assisted tracker citing IMF PortWatch reported four transits on 4 Oct 2026 against a pre-war baseline of 85 a day (straits.live, 7 Oct 2026, https://straits.live/briefs/2026-10-07); I rate that source low and the two figures disagree. On the Red Sea leg, Bab el-Mandeb transits in Aug 2026 were 70 percent below pre-crisis and Suez 39 percent below, with the Houthi embargo narrowed to Saudi-linked ships (Lloyd's List Intelligence, 18 Sep 2026, https://www.lloydslistintelligence.com/resources/blog/red-sea-brief-18-september-2026). Predetermined: Gulf deliveries into 2027 run through a chokepoint under attack. Uncertain: the reopening date.

**Niobium is a one-country material and its biggest buyer is China (N02, N03).** Brazil produced 104,000 tonnes of 112,000 world total in 2025 (93 percent) and Canada 6,000; the United States is 100 percent import reliant; 49 percent of Brazil's 2024 ferroniobium exports went to China (USGS, Feb 2026, https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-niobium.pdf). No new mine of scale changes this by 2031. Leverage sits with Brazil, which has not used it, and with China as the dominant buyer for its own wire mills. Panel judgement: the magnet-grade wire mill (N03), not the ore, is where this reaches the OEM.

**The China plant now sits inside a reciprocal procurement wall (N17, N29, N36).** The European Commission excluded Chinese bidders from EU public procurement of medical devices above 5 million euros from 20 Jun 2025, with no expiry (Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/1197, https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2025/1197/oj/eng). China's Ministry of Finance answered on 6 Jul 2025 by barring EU enterprises from government procurement of imported devices at or above 45 million yuan, naming MR, while exempting products made in China by foreign-invested subsidiaries (CMS, Jul 2025, https://cms.law/en/chn/legal-updates/china-retaliates-against-eu-by-limiting-procurement-of-eu-medical-devices). The inference is that a Tianjin-built scanner keeps Chinese market access that a Wisconsin-built one loses. AI-suggested as to which markets each plant serves; the S&OP council confirms.

**Advanced logic tariffs are legislated but narrow (N10, N12).** Proclamation 11002 of 14 Jan 2026 imposed 25 percent on defined advanced logic chips under HTS 8471.50, 8471.80 and 8473.30, with duty-free quotas for firms building US fabs under the Taiwan agreement of 15 Jan 2026 (Covington, Feb 2026, https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/02/a-month-in-semiconductor-policy-section-232-measures-bis-rule-and-taiwan-deal-signal-strategic-push/). FPGAs (field-programmable gate arrays) are outside those headings; widening to N10 is uncertain.

**Payment rails already run on two systems (N31).** CIPS, China's cross-border interbank payment system, had 194 direct participants in mid-2026 while the yuan carried 3.1 percent of SWIFT payments (FXC Intelligence, May 2026, https://www.fxcintel.com/research/analysis/cips-volumes-may-2026). A sanctions rupture would stop dollar settlement, not China-side settlement.

## Critical factors

The Qatar restart date, the Hormuz reopening date, and whether Russia's licence steers helium toward China decide whether N01 and N19 breach the 30-day line in 2027. Brazil's niobium policy and China's feedstock share decide N03's cost, probably not its availability, by 2031.

## Predetermined versus uncertain

Predetermined, high confidence on public statistics: the four-state helium market with Russia licensed and sanctioned to end-2027 (N01, N34); Qatar capacity partly out to about 2031 (N01); niobium 93 percent Brazilian (N02); procurement walls with no expiry (N17, N36); CIPS as a parallel rail (N31). Moderate confidence, sources disagree on degree: Hormuz closed into 2027 (N27).

Uncertain, listed without analysis for phase 3: when Hormuz reopens (outcome, N27); when Ras Laffan restarts in full (outcome, N01); whether the US-China truce, reported extended to 10 Jan 2027 (New Straits Times, Sep 2026, https://www.nst.com.my/amp/business/economy/2026/09/1543289/china-us-agree-tariff-cuts-us60bil-goods-including-agriculture), holds through the 2027 sourcing cycle (outcome, N17, N31); whether the Section 232 medical equipment investigation of 26 Sep 2025 (Federal Register 2025-18729, https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/09/26/2025-18729) ends in a tariff on finished scanners (action, N17, N18); whether Taiwan grey-zone pressure, with coast guard incursions at a 2026 high in June while air sorties fell 58 percent year on year (Taiwan Security Monitor, Jul 2026, https://tsm.schar.gmu.edu/?p=1629), becomes interdiction (outcome, N29, N10); whether the China-Japan dual-use spiral begun by MOFCOM Announcement No. 1 of 6 Jan 2026 (Greenberg Traurig, Feb 2026, https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/2/china-imposes-escalated-export-controls-on-dual-use-items-to-japan) reaches cold heads shipped from Japan to Tianjin (causation, N05, N17).

## Blind spots of this lens

I count what states can stop and discount how often they choose not to; the 2017 Qatar blockade resolved in months, and Russia's helium licence may be paperwork rather than a cut. I also underrate substitution: USGS notes higher-temperature superconductors in development for MR, which would shrink N01 by 2031.
