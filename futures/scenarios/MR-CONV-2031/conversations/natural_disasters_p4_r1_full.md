# Natural disasters, phase 4, round 1

## Analysis

Bottom line first. The hazards are the same in all four futures: a correlated inland storm behind the US magnet plant, a Nankai Trough event under the Japanese cold-head source, a typhoon or earthquake over the Taiwan foundry. The axes change only which node the hazard walks through to the 30-day line: helium cover (N19) where helium still decides, the cold head (N05) where it does not. Every future assumes the design and qualification record (N22) is intact; the encryption break (C4) sits in phase 6a. Demand shape (C5) changes each bill, not the break.

### F1 "Cost only, helium still decides"

What happened. 2026 to 2028: the truce renews, the Section 232 report stays on finished scanners, nothing lists wire or cold heads. The Gulf repair runs toward three to five years (QatarEnergy via IOM3, 23 March 2026); the US plant buys allocated domestic helium. The Carolinas grid turns elevated in 2027 (NERC, 29 January 2026). 2028 to 2031: the Midcontinent grid is high from winter 2028; conventional builds still dominate; no 3 tesla sealed clearance arrives.

On my nodes. N19 is the margin every weather event walks through; helium moves on a 35 to 48 day liquid clock (N23); the port closes two to three days per hurricane (N26, SC Ports Authority logs); N17 cannot lend cover.

What breaks first. R8 helium cover (N19), through a correlated storm: a Pee Dee basin flood of the 2015 to 2018 class, a ten-day grid rebuild (Duke Energy, 30 September 2024), N24 closed, the trailer late. The plant stands; the liquefier is dark. Window: any hurricane season from 2027. Panel judgement.

Cost. A line stop at N16 of 30 to 45 days; every Western Hemisphere magnet in that window ships late. Count: Placeholder.

Most confident: the port is not the breaking node (high, on logs). Least confident: cover days at N19, a Placeholder; if cover exceeds the grid rebuild, my lens has no break here.

### F2 "Cost only, helium no longer decides"

What happened. 2026 to 2028: Hormuz reopens, allocation letters stop, a 3 tesla sealed magnet is cleared, the test draw is a cylinder. 2028 to 2031: sealed magnets take most new builds; helium is a service risk (N34) only; the cold head carries every magnet's thermal load.

On my nodes. N19 and weather at N16 and N18 shrink to days. N05 is the one cryogenic dependency left, at an unconfirmed address under a classification raised in September 2025 (Headquarters for Earthquake Research Promotion).

What breaks first. R5 cold heads (N05), through a Nankai Trough event and its secondary perils (tsunami, port, power) at the Japanese source, 18 months to requalify. No date: a 30-year probability of 60 to 90 percent is a classification, not a schedule. Panel judgement; address AI-suggested, Sourcing confirms.

Cost. Both plants stop once cold-head inventory runs, weeks after the event, far past 30 days; the installed base loses service parts.

Most confident: no weather route to 30 days at the US sites alone (moderate, on return periods). Least confident: whether N05 sits inside the Nankai zone.

### F3 "A part stops, helium still decides"

What happened. 2026 to 2028: a producer notice lists cold heads or wire, or an entity listing cuts the China plant's parts flow; the Gulf stays impaired. 2028 to 2031: the US plant carries more conventional magnets for more markets, test capacity (N21) concentrates there, and cover at N19 thins.

What breaks first. The trade stop belongs to another lens and lands in 2027 to 2028. From mine, R1 US magnet plant (N16) through N19: a Carolinas winter-morning curtailment or the F1 storm on a plant running flat out with N17 isolated. First hurricane season after the stop. Panel judgement.

Cost. The F1 stop with more magnets behind it and no Eastern cover; lateness lands in two regions.

Most confident: grid, road and trailer fail together on the Helene record (high). Least confident: whether the US plant takes the extra load, an S&OP council decision, not a hazard.

### F4 "A part stops, helium no longer decides"

What happened. 2026 to 2028: sealed conversion runs behind a trade wall; a listing names cold heads. 2028 to 2031: every magnet depends on N05 under licence, inventory thin.

What breaks first. R5 cold heads (N05). The trade stop breaks it first; an earthquake or typhoon at the source during a licence hold makes it unrecoverable, because alternative route and hazard sit in one country. Weeks after inventory runs; N16 and N18 see only days of weather. Panel judgement.

Cost. Both plants stop together, the longest stop of the four.

Most confident: weather at N16 and N18 cannot reach 30 days alone (moderate). Least confident: whether the compressor second source (N06) holds while cold heads do not.

## Critical factors

Three numbers decide how real this is: cover days at N19, the addresses of N03 and N05 against hazard maps, and backup power at N16. All Placeholders or AI-suggested.

## Predetermined versus uncertain

Predetermined: port closures of days (N26), the ten-day grid rebuild (N16), grid ratings from 2027 and 2028 (N16, N18), the raised classification over N05. Uncertain: which end of U2 holds (outcomes), the sealed test draw (causation), earthquake timing (outcomes, no schedule).

## Contradictions with other lenses

Round 1 is isolated. From phase 3: the cold-head split at U2 end B; I side with the six who say the risk breaks.

## Blind spots of this lens

I cannot place N03 or N05 on a map, so F2 and F4 rest on an unconfirmed address. I underweight drought on hydropower feeding N17 and heat at N32.
