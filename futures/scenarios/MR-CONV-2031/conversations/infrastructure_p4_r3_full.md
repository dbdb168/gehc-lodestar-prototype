## Analysis

Bottom line: the four pages hold on both axes, the dates run in order, and the one physical gap is that every F3 and F4 page assumes the United States plant could cover Asia if allowed, while none tests whether its helium-intensive ramp and test capacity (N21) could carry two hemispheres under allocation.

**F1, trade a cost, helium still decides.** I adopt my own first break, helium cover at the United States magnet plant (N19), and take Natural disasters' earlier window, any hurricane season from 2027, as the front edge. Financial's China plant (N17) wins only if Asian spot helium is thin and cover at N17 is shorter than at N16; both are Placeholders. Panel judgement, moderate.

**F2, trade a cost, helium no longer decides.** I adopt Technology shift's cold head (N05). A sealed fleet with no helium reserve puts the whole thermal load on a part made at one plant in a Nankai Trough zone. My round 2 flood at the United States plant (N16, N24) depends on neither axis and belongs in the shared-break column. Labor's technicians (N20) are a slow install, not a stop.

**F3, something stops, helium still decides.** I adopt Geopolitical's China plant (N17) through logic (N10), the thinnest stock on the floor. Security's payment freeze (N31) may land a quarter earlier but needs a second state to act. Helium cover (N19) breaks second, which matches F1.

**F4, something stops, helium no longer decides.** I adopt Macroeconomic's cold head (N05) over the Contrarian's wire (N03). Wire is a heavy, slow input that five countries mill and that inventory sits behind; the cold head is a live part a parent can reallocate and that service swaps compete for, with no helium margin. If a notice names wire first, the Contrarian is right. Panel judgement, moderate.

## Blind spots of this lens
