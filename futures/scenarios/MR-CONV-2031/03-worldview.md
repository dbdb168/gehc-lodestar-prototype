## Core prediction

The next five years bring more disruption to delivery, not less, and most of it will arrive through vulnerabilities the OEM already carries rather than through new threats. In the owner's words, moving a plant out of a country changes the vulnerability, not the threat.

## Reasoning

The function has been reacting to external events as they land. It has little visibility of its own internal vulnerabilities. Leaders' first question is about on-time delivery today, and the function wants to show where delivery will be disrupted next even after today's root causes are fixed.

## Key uncertainties

Whether good enough data exists to act on. Whether leaders will act on tagged, incomplete data or wait for canonical data that may never arrive. Whether the business will fund proactive changes when the pull is for visibility of today's problems.

## Cruxes

A future that shows one vulnerability breaking first, with the cost of waiting beside the confidence, would move the owner to act on imperfect data. Evidence that a second source can be qualified inside the decision clock would change the default from pause to act.

## Mental models

Resiliency as the ability to absorb shocks while keeping service, quality and compliance at acceptable cost. Fragility as external threat multiplied by internal vulnerability. Tolerance set by the value of the product, not by appetite for risk (the expensive car and the everyday car in a hailstorm). Monitoring that marries external signals with internal vulnerabilities into a story.

## Implications they see

Start with one product and work it end to end. Flag what is suggested and what is confirmed and act on the suggested where the cost of waiting is high. Serve the leaders' on-time delivery question first, then show what is coming.
