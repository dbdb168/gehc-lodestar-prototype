## What is shared and what belongs to one future

**Shared by all four futures.**

- Both plants breach the tolerance line in every future (R1, R2). The carrying node changes with the axes, helium cover on the helium side and the cold head on the sealed side, but there is no future in which a plant stays inside the line. Eleven lenses, and the Contrarian's point that the panel had not named this as a shared break is accepted here. Panel judgement.
- The 35-day encryption event is a stop longer than 30 days in every future. See the paragraph below.
- The sealed side always lands on the sole cold head. F2 and F4 differ in cause (hazard or outage against parent exit plus listing) and in bill, not in node. Technology shift and Financial add that no hazard is needed on the sealed side: a parent that earns a minority of revenue from MR can allocate away. AI-suggested, Sourcing confirms the parent's MR share.
- Three risks cost in every future and break in none: the United States hub (R3), the wire (R4) and the technicians (R7). The Contrarian's reading stands: this is because no future reaches them first, not because the world spares them. The three one-town trades age on no axis (Labor), and the wire mill carries the encryption event as a cost only while inventory outruns 35 days, which Sourcing has not stated (Security, Macroeconomic). Placeholder.
- The United States plant cannot cover Asia in any future, because ramp and test capacity at N21 takes 24 months to change (Infrastructure, carried from phase 4). AI-suggested, Site operations confirms.

**Shared by a pair.**

- Helium cover (R8) breaks in F1 and F3 only, where helium still decides. The mechanism is the same on both pages: a grid curtailment, flood or storm takes the liquefier's power and the oversize road while a container is inside its 35 to 48 day hold (Natural disasters, Infrastructure). It fails as working capital before it fails as logistics: cover carried at a 4 percent policy rate against producer prices rising about 5 percent a year thins each budget round, which is why F1 breaks in winter 2028 rather than later (Financial, Macroeconomic; Federal Reserve, 16 September 2026).
- The cold head (R5) breaks in F2 and F4 only. A Nankai Trough event closes the maker's ports as well as its plant, so undamaged parts cannot move (Infrastructure). The hospital sees a dark scanner rather than a slow install, because a sealed fleet warms when the cold head fails (Customer and market).

**Belonging to one future.**

- F3 alone breaks the China plant through United States-origin logic (R6) behind an entity listing, second half of 2027. It is the earliest break on the map and the only future where a trade rule is the first mover (Foreign trade and payments, Geopolitical). The stop lasts as long as the licence queue, and a stopped plant restarts on rehiring time, not licence time, because a tight labour market takes its technicians (Labor). Government-action stops are uninsurable (Financial). It is also the only future in which share moves within one tender cycle, in Japan and India, because a China-built competitor keeps delivering (Customer and market).
- F4 alone stops both plants for most of 18 months, through a producer-country dual-use notice on cryocoolers plus the parent's exit. Price is the mechanism here and nowhere else: the price at which the OEM loses its cold-head allocation is an unset Placeholder, and a price the OEM would pay turns the break into a cost (Macroeconomic). F4 adds a grey-market cold head as a cost on top (Security).
- F1 and F2 carry no trade break. Tariffs, customs delays and payment friction stay inside the 30-day line (Foreign trade and payments). Lateness in F1 and F2 is common to the market, because every competitor is short of the same helium or cold head at the same time, so a tender committee does not score it against one supplier (Customer and market).

## The shared encryption break

The 35-day encryption event (C4 from phase 3) breaches the tolerance line in all four futures. Eleven of eleven. It is one event with four landing sites, and it lands wherever each future's first break already sits, because a single qualified site is both the supply risk and the richest target (Security).

- At either plant (N16, N17) it is a 35-day line stop in every future, because winding is instrumented and test is a digital record (Labor, Technology shift).
- At the gas carriers (N23) or the port terminal (N26) it is worst in F1 and F3: a locked carrier walks through the helium cover and stops the United States plant for 35 to 60 days, longer than the event itself, because the cover runs out before systems return (Infrastructure, Natural disasters, Security). At the port it holds every export magnet for 35 days in every future without a line stop (Foreign trade and payments, Infrastructure).
- At the cold-head maker (N05) it is a stop on the sealed side (F2, F4), indistinguishable from the first break arriving early, and a cost on the helium side where a reserve stands behind the cold head (Contrarian, Technology shift).
- At the wire mill (N03) it is a cost in every future until it outruns the wire buffer, which is unstated (Security).
- In F3 it lands on the China plant's payment rails (N31) and extends the licence queue; a cyber-law investigation with a payment freeze can stop the plant before any listing is published (Security, Foreign trade and payments).
- Cyber insurance partly excludes state action, so the stop is largely uninsured (Financial).
- Share can move in every future on this break and on no other in F1 and F2, because competitors are not hit together and a committee reads it as the supplier's own failure (Customer and market).

One Placeholder removes the shared break everywhere: a tested 30-day manual fallback at both plants and both sole suppliers. The Labor lens doubts one exists. Placeholder.

## Costs against what is being protected

Measured over five years: revenue, margin, installed base and public-health role.

- F1: one 30 to 45 day stop at the United States plant; about a quarter of Western installs late; the Asian queue breaches on schedule in the first half of 2028. Margin, not share.
- F2: both plants stop for longer than 30 days with no schedule; the sealed installed base warms; installs slip two to three quarters in 2029 on the trade swap. Installed base and margin; share only if competitors buy from a different maker.
- F3: the China plant stops for a licence queue plus rehiring time from late 2027; Asian installs two quarters late; share moves in Japan and India; the United States cover breaches second in early 2028. The costliest per quarter and uninsurable (Financial).
- F4: both plants stop for most of 18 months from 2029; the installed base breaches service cover; technicians lost and rehired into 2030; Europe's replacement peak goes to a competitor. The highest bill.

Demand shape (C5) scales every bill and no break. Whether tenders score late delivery at all is unverified; if they do not, the F3 and F4 cost is margin, not share (Customer and market, N36). Placeholder.

## How sure the panel is

- High that the encryption event breaches, and that both plants breach in every future.
- Moderate on which node each future breaks on.
- Low on every date after 2028. The F2 and F4 dates are votes on an undated event; F4's 2029 rests on an allocation nobody has announced, settled by the parent's segment disclosure (Contrarian). The F3 date rests on a notice not yet written.
- Low on the F2 second break at the technicians (Labor).

## Corrections and disagreements carried forward

- Four lenses (Customer and market, Financial, Foreign trade and payments, Security, Technology shift) hold R1 to a cost in F3 because the China plant breaks first and the page's helium breach is the second break. The table records the majority, a break in early 2028, and notes it is the second break on that page.
- Security's conditional breaks (wire listed before the cold head in F4, cold head listed before logic in F3) are kept as the order-of-listing watch for phase 7, not as table entries.
- The Contrarian's finding that R3, R4 and R7 are untested rather than safe is accepted and carried to phase 6b: actions on those nodes are not justified by the futures, only by their age.
- The one dated labour event, the longshore master contract expiry on 30 September 2030 (N26), is a days-long cost in every future. Carried as phase 2 Labor record; Logistics confirms. Not verified against a phase 2 file in this round.

## What phase 6b inherits

The candidate actions on every page, now ranked by how many futures they work in: the manual fallback test and the Placeholder confirmations work in all four; a second cold-head source and the parent agreement work on the sealed side; cover above hold plus the grid rebuild and backup power work on the helium side; logic stock and a non-United States logic path work in F3 only. Actions on the hub, the wire and the technicians are age actions, not future actions, and 6b should say so.
