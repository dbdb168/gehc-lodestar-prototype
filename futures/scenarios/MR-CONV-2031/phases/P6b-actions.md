# Phase 6b: actions, what works in every future and what is a bet

Read date 11 October 2026. One round: eleven isolated views, each lens testing the eleven candidate actions in `P6b-candidates.md` against all four futures and adding at most two missing ones. This is the only phase in which the panel recommends. The structured register is `actions.json` (26 actions). Transcripts are `conversations/<lens>_p6b_r1_full.md`.

## Bottom line

Three actions work in every future and the panel ranks them first: confirm the Placeholders (A10), test a 30-day manual fallback at both plants and both sole suppliers (A9), and qualify a second cold-head maker (A1). The first two cost phone calls and weeks of staff time. The third is the one action with a real clock: 18 months of requalification against a 2029 break, so a start after mid-2027 lands too late. Everything else is either insurance for the pair of futures the read date leans toward (helium cover and backup power, F1 and F3) or a bet on one future (stock at the China plant, F3; a contract with the cold-head parent, F4).

The nearest date on the list is not in 2027. Stock at the China plant (A5) has to be bought before the truce notice, first read as 10 November 2026, because stock bought after a listing may itself need a licence.

## The ranking and why

Ranked by futures covered, then by the cost of waiting a year.

1. **A10 Confirm the Placeholders.** Measurement, not protection (Contrarian), but every other action is sized from the cover days, the test draw, the logic stock weeks and the allocation price, and all of them are synthetic. Cost: phone calls. Waiting a year puts the 2027 capital plan on guesses.
2. **A9 Manual fallback test.** The shared 35-day break has no external clock, so the test is the only clock. The Security lens adds that the wire mill and the cold-head maker must be in the room, because the OEM's own network is rarely the way in. Labor adds that the people who ran the line on paper are the ones nearest retirement.
3. **A1 Second cold-head maker.** The one action with a hard clock and the highest cost of waiting: in F4 both plants stay stopped into 2030. The Contrarian's condition is accepted: one maker has already left the market, so until Sourcing names a second maker with a letter of intent this is a slide, not an action. If no second maker exists, the fallback is A2 plus A7.
4. **A12 Design record copy.** Added by two lenses independently and ranked above every built action because it is the assumption every page rests on and it costs almost nothing.
5. **A3 and A4 together, helium cover and backup power.** They pay only where helium still decides, which is where the read date leans, and they must exist before the winter of 2028, which puts the start in the second quarter of 2027. A4 is the cheap ordinary action the panel will under-rate (Contrarian). A8 raises the helium draw at the plant, so the 2027 capital plan must size A3 and A8 together (Infrastructure).
6. **A5 Stock at the China plant.** A bet on one future but the only action dated by the truce notice rather than the 2027 cycle, and that date is first.
7. **A2 Agreement with the cold-head parent.** A bet on F4, because a contract moves neither a licence nor a flood, but the 2027 cycle is the last normal negotiation before the parent's next review. If it is signed, it carries a change-of-control clause and step-in rights to tooling and drawings (Financial, Security).
8. **A13, A14, A17: the checks.** Control classifications, a credit watch and a contract-expiry calendar cost nothing, work everywhere and are done now.

The capital-heavy actions, A6 and A8, are bets on a stop and land late: A6 is a 2029 answer to a 2027 break, and A8 is useless in F1, where the United States plant itself fails.

## Disagreements carried forward

- **A1's reach.** Four lenses say it works in all four futures because the same cold head cools conventional magnets, so the sole source is a risk on the helium side too. Seven hold it to the sealed futures because helium is the fallback that turns a cold-head outage into a bill. The register records the majority and notes the minority; the Placeholder that settles it is the cold-head parent's MR revenue share and the maker's address.
- **A6 in F4.** Five lenses add F4 because a non-United States logic path shortens the licence queue there. The F4 page says the logic listing is absorbed with stock, so the break it prevents is F3's only. Recorded as a bet on F3.
- **A2 with a change-of-control clause.** Financial says the clause makes it work in all four. Geopolitical and Foreign trade reply that a licence refusal overrides any contract. Recorded as a bet on F4, with the clause required if it is signed.
- **A11.** Nine lenses say it is not an action but the decision that chooses the pair of futures. Labor says it is a hiring decision in disguise, because the two lines need different trades and competence takes 18 months. Both are recorded: it stays in the register as a decision with its own triggers.

## What phase 7 inherits

The owner's worldview can now be read. The actions that work everywhere (A10, A9, A1, A12) are the ones a leader can take on tagged, incomplete data, which is the owner's stated crux. The bets (A2, A5, A6) are the ones that wait on an indicator.
