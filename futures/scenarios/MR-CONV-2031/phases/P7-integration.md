# Phase 7: integration, the owner's worldview against the four futures

Read date 11 October 2026. Moderator only. `03-worldview.md` was unsealed after phase 6b. It is a Placeholder: no owner interview has happened, and the file was reconstructed from the resiliency function's own words in the discovery conversation of 9 October 2026, so this contrast is between the panel's futures and the function's stated beliefs, not the product owner's. Confidence that it represents the product owner: low.

Each belief is marked confirmed, challenged or silent against each future, with the crux named where the futures and the belief part company.

## Belief 1. The next five years bring more disruption to delivery, not less, and most of it arrives through vulnerabilities the OEM already carries rather than through new threats.

| F1 | F2 | F3 | F4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Confirmed | Confirmed | Confirmed | Confirmed |

All four futures breach the tolerance line, and in every one the break lands on a node that was already on the risk list in 2026: helium cover, the cold head, the China plant's logic. No future breaks on a threat the screen did not name. The panel adds one sharpening: the vulnerabilities are not only carried but ageing. Three one-town trades age on no axis (Labor), and the cover thins each budget round at a 4 percent rate against rising prices (Macroeconomic). The shared encryption break is the same finding from the other side: a single qualified site is both the supply risk and the richest target (Security).

**Crux:** none. The futures and the belief agree. The panel's version is stronger than the owner's, because it names which vulnerability breaks first in each future and by when.

## Belief 2. Moving a plant out of a country changes the vulnerability, not the threat.

| F1 | F2 | F3 | F4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Confirmed | Confirmed | Challenged | Confirmed |

F2 and F4 are the clearest confirmation: once helium leaves the story, the trade axis changes the cause and the bill, not the node, because one Japanese part holds every magnet wherever the plants sit. F1 confirms it from the helium side: the United States plant waits for allocated helium whatever its address.

F3 challenges it. In F3 the break lands on the China plant because it sits inside a counterparty's borders, and the United States plant cannot cover Asia because its ramp and test capacity takes 24 months to change (Infrastructure). Geography does decide which plant a listing strands. The action the panel prices for this, A8, is the one that keeps an Asian date when the China plant stops.

**Crux:** whether the OEM's two plants are one network or two. If a listing addressed to one destination strands one plant and the other cannot cover, then where a plant sits does change the threat it faces. The settling evidence is the ramp and test capacity Placeholder at the United States plant, which Site operations confirms.

## Belief 3. The function has little visibility of its own internal vulnerabilities, and leaders' first question is about on-time delivery today.

| F1 | F2 | F3 | F4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Confirmed | Confirmed | Confirmed | Confirmed |

The panel ran on synthetic internal data and still found that the numbers deciding every page are internal Placeholders: cover days, backup power, the test draw in litres, logic stock in weeks, the cold-head parent's revenue share, the wire buffer. Eleven of eleven lenses ranked confirming those Placeholders (A10) first or second, ahead of any built action, because every other action is sized blind without them. That is the belief, measured.

**Crux:** none against the futures. Against the implications, one: the panel found that the missing data costs a phone call, not a system. The ten Placeholders have named owners and can be answered inside the 2027 sourcing cycle.

## Belief 4. Whether good enough data exists to act on, and whether leaders will act on tagged, incomplete data or wait for canonical data that may never arrive.

| F1 | F2 | F3 | F4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Challenged | Challenged | Challenged | Challenged |

The futures challenge the first half. Good enough data exists for the external half of every page: the Gulf transit count, the allocation letters, the grid ratings, the clearance records, the truce notice date, the parent's filings are all on dated public sources, and twelve indicators now read them. What does not exist is the internal half, and the panel's finding is that it is small and cheap to get.

On the second half, the futures give the leader the thing the owner said would move them: in each future one vulnerability breaks first, with a date, a cost against the tolerance line and a confidence. The actions that work in every future (A10, A9, A1, A12) are precisely the ones a leader can take on tagged data, because they pay whichever way the axes settle. Only the bets (A2, A5, A6) need canonical data, and for those the panel gives the indicator to wait on instead.

**Crux:** whether an action that works in all four futures still needs canonical data. The panel says no: if it pays whichever way the world goes, the data that would make it canonical does not change the decision. This is the crux of the whole exercise, and it is the one the owner named.

## Belief 5. Whether the business will fund proactive changes when the pull is for visibility of today's problems.

| F1 | F2 | F3 | F4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Silent | Silent | Silent | Silent |

No lens can see inside the OEM's funding decisions. What the panel can say is what proactive change costs here. The three actions that work everywhere cost phone calls, weeks of staff time and, for the second cold-head maker, low millions over 18 months. The capital-heavy items (A6, A8) are the ones the panel ranks lowest, as bets on one future. If the business funds only the cheap actions, it has still covered the shared break and sized everything else.

**Crux:** whether the 2027 capital plan carries the one dated item, the second cold-head maker. Its cost of waiting is both plants stopped into 2030 in F4. That is the number a funding conversation needs.

## Belief 6. Resiliency is the ability to absorb shocks while keeping service, quality and compliance at acceptable cost. Fragility is external threat multiplied by internal vulnerability. Tolerance is set by the value of the product, not by appetite for risk.

| F1 | F2 | F3 | F4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Confirmed | Confirmed | Confirmed | Confirmed |

The panel used all three. The screen multiplied threat reach by replaceability. The tolerance line is value-based (an install more than 30 days late, a stop longer than 30 days) and every break is measured against it. The costs are written against the five-year values in the scope packet: deliveries hospitals can plan around, revenue and margin, the installed base, the public-health role.

One sharpening. The futures show that "acceptable cost" has a shape the formula does not: in F1 and F2 lateness is common to the market and forgiven by tender committees, while in F3 and F4 and on the encryption break it is the supplier's own failure and costs share (Customer and market). Fragility multiplied out the same way in both cases; the cost did not.

**Crux:** whether tenders score late delivery at all (N36). Unverified. If they do not, the F3 and F4 cost is margin, not share, and the value being protected is smaller than the pages assume.

## Belief 7. Monitoring should marry external signals with internal vulnerabilities into a story.

| F1 | F2 | F3 | F4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Confirmed | Confirmed | Confirmed | Confirmed |

This is what the four pages are. Each one reads an external signal (a listing, a Gulf transit count, a clearance) through to an internal node (the cover, the cold head, the logic stock) and ends on a date and a cost. The twelve indicators are the external half of that marriage and the Placeholders are the internal half.

**Crux:** none. The panel's one addition is that the story needs a third element the owner did not name: the decision clock. A break in 2029 is a 2027 decision because requalification takes 18 months.

## Belief 8. Start with one product and work it end to end. Flag what is suggested and what is confirmed and act on the suggested where the cost of waiting is high. Serve the leaders' on-time delivery question first, then show what is coming.

| F1 | F2 | F3 | F4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Confirmed | Confirmed | Confirmed | Challenged |

The run is the first two sentences done. The third is challenged by F4 and partly by F3. In F4 the on-time delivery question of 2027 reads clean (nothing stops for 30 days, the logic listing is absorbed with stock) while the decision that prevents the 2029 break has to be taken in that same year. In F3 the China plant's helium queue reads as a slow worsening of on-time delivery in early 2028, after the listing has already landed. A leader who is served today's delivery question first, in those futures, is told that all is well in the year the clock runs out.

**Crux:** whether a clean on-time delivery reading in 2027 is evidence of resilience or of a 2029 break not yet visible. The futures say the two are indistinguishable from the delivery numbers alone, which is the reason the indicators exist.

## Where the worldview is silent

- The sealed conversion decision (A11). The owner's file does not mention the OEM's own product roadmap, yet it is the decision that chooses which pair of futures the line lives in. The panel's finding that waiting a year picks F1 and F3 by default is new to the worldview.
- Labour. The worldview does not mention the trades. The panel found that labour never breaks first but sets how long every stop lasts, and that the people who could run the line on paper are nearest retirement.
- The design record. Every page assumes it is intact; the worldview does not mention it; two lenses independently ranked an offline copy above every built action.

## What the owner interview must settle

The real interview, when it happens, replaces this file. Three questions matter more than the rest, because they are the cruxes above: whether the two plants are one network or two (belief 2); whether an action that works in every future still waits on canonical data (belief 4); and whether the 2027 capital plan carries the second cold-head maker (belief 5). The three internal numbers to bring to that conversation are the cover days at the United States plant, the sealed test draw in litres and the China plant's logic stock in weeks.
